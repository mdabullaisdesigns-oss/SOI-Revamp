## 2016-10
@ the-age-of-openness
Q What change does the editorial observe in how people of different faiths speak with one another today?
* Debate aimed at proving superiority has given way to open discussion with equal respect :: Yes. This openness is called a blessing of the age.
- Religious meetings are now forbidden
- Only scholars may now speak on religion
- People have lost interest in such questions
R “The culture of debate has been replaced by discussion. The watchword is that freedom of expression is supreme.”
@ the-age-of-openness
Q The editorial hopes people may reach the point of saying, “I am right, you may also be right.” What does it say this does NOT mean?
* That they end up uncertain – it means going forward with confidence and respect for human dignity :: Right. Open discussion strengthens conviction.
- That they must give up their own beliefs
- That all views are equally mistaken
- That discussion should be avoided
R “This does not mean that participants in discussion will ultimately emerge from their deliberations in a state of uncertainty.”
@ history-of-reaction
Q According to the article, why have so many revolutions replaced one “unwanted system” with another?
* Their leaders pursued a society free of all evil, which cannot exist; what they called evil was challenge to be managed :: Yes. Reaction without regard to realities fails.
- They lacked enough supporters
- They were opposed by other nations
- They moved too slowly
R “All the leaders and reformers were obsessed with an evil-free ideal society, while according to the law of nature the ideal cannot be achieved in this world… ‘evil’ is the wrong name for ‘challenge’.”
@ history-of-reaction
Q What does the article advise parents to give their children above material comfort?
* The spirit of hard work, so that they stand on their own feet :: Correct. Indulgence removes incentive.
- A large inheritance
- Freedom from all difficulty
- Introductions to influential people
R “The right way is for the parents to create the spirit of hard work in their children so that they stand on their own feet.”
@ tazkiya-or-purification
Q What does the article name as the price of attaining tazkiya (purification)?
* Giving up whatever stands in its way – bad habits, excess and frivolity :: Yes. To gain one thing, another must be let go.
- A long journey to a holy place
- A payment to a teacher
- Years of silence
R “The price for attaining tazkiya is abstaining from everything that goes against achieving this goal.”
@ unlimited-reward-of-patience
Q The Quran promises the patient a reward “without measure.” How does the article describe the path of patience?
* Staying good, moderate and principled even when treated badly or when it costs you :: Well read. Being good only to those who are good needs no patience.
- Waiting without acting
- Avoiding all contact with difficult people
- Returning each act in kind
R “Even when someone is unkind to you, you are good to him. Even if he adopts a provocative stance, you remain moderate in your behaviour.”
@ ritual-must-arise-from-the-spirit
Q A man migrated from Makkah to Madinah in order to marry a woman there. What did the Prophet say of his migration?
* It would not count as migration for the sake of God – an act is judged by its intention :: Yes. Form alone has only relative value.
- It was the most praiseworthy of all
- It should be repeated
- It was forbidden
R “Since this individual had migrated for his own private benefit, the migration would not be considered as migration for the sake of God.”
@ merit-culture-contacts-culture
Q The article describes a “merit culture,” a “contacts culture” and a third kind. What marks the third, a divine society?
* Each person’s concern is his own purification, with God – not people – as the focus :: Right. Success is measured by the Hereafter.
- Positions go to the best connected
- Everyone holds equal wealth
- No one works for a living
R “In a divine society, every person’s concern is one’s own purification… In contrast, in a divine society, the focus of people is entirely God.”
@ goodness-in-every-situation
Q How does a person of deep faith respond to joy and to difficulty, according to the article?
* With gratitude in joy and acceptance in difficulty – both drawing him nearer to God :: Exactly. Others swing between pride and despair.
- With pride in joy and despair in difficulty
- With indifference to both
- With fear in joy and anger in difficulty
R “If a man of deep faith experiences joy, his heart is filled with gratitude. And if he is faced with a difficulty, he accepts it, regarding it as God’s Will.”
@ the-word-of-god
Q What does the chapter Al-Ikhlas (112) say of God, as the commentary summarises it?
* He is One, depends on none while all depend on Him, and nothing is like Him :: Well read. This is the starting point of all Islamic teaching.
- He is the first among many
- He is part of the natural world
- He is known only to a few
R “God is not many. He is only one. All depend upon Him. He depends on none… a unique being Who has no equal.”
