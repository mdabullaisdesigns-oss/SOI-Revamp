## 2019-09
@ without-prejudice
Q When others plotted against the Prophet and his Companions, what course did the Quran set for them?
* Trust in God, rise above the situation and carry on with positive work :: Yes. No room was left for negative thinking.
- Uncover each plot and answer it in kind
- Withdraw from all contact with others
- Wait until the opponents grew tired
R “Ignoring the plots and antagonism, trusting only in God and rising above circumstances, they were to continue all activities which were of a positive nature.”
@ without-prejudice
Q What does the article say happens to someone obsessed with the idea that everyone is against him?
* He loses objectivity – like a man who sees only the thorns in a garden of roses :: Well read. He even suspects his own well-wishers.
- He becomes more alert and successful
- He gains many new allies
- He learns to trust more easily
R “One loses all objectivity in thinking… One becomes like the man who can see only the thorns in a garden of roses.”
@ growing-old-is-certainly-not-a-countdown
Q The article says ageing is “not the beginning of a countdown.” What is it, then?
* A “count-up” – a time of maturity and wisdom that can guide the next generation :: Right. “Old is gold.”
- A time to withdraw completely
- A time only for rest
- A burden to be endured
R “Ageing is not the beginning of a countdown, but is rather, the time for a count-up. The world can benefit greatly from the planning skills and wisdom of the aged.”

@ belief-in-god
Q Bertrand Russell traced man’s philosophical quest to the “terror of cosmic loneliness.” How does the article explain that loneliness?
* The need for God is woven into human nature, and only finding Him fills the inner vacuum :: Well reasoned. A rock feels no such lack.
- It comes from living in large cities
- It is caused by too much study
- It disappears with age
R “A human being by his very nature has interwoven in him the desire to submit to God… His feeling of loneliness or the sense of vacuum in his life is fulfilled by his discovery of and belief in God.”
@ islamic-virtues
Q A large sum reached Madinah and Umar wished to distribute it fairly. Whose suggestion did he adopt?
* Walid ibn Hisham’s – to compile registers, as he had seen done in Syria :: Correct. A good method was welcome from any quarter.
- His own first plan, unchanged
- A proposal to store it for war
- A plan to give it only to the elders
R “‘I saw that the rulers there had compiled registers for this purpose…’ Umar accepted this advice and delegated the task of compiling registers to three of his Companions.”
@ adapting-to-a-new-world
Q Before giraffes were flown from Kenya, they were played recordings of jet engines for weeks. What does the article compare with this preparation?
* The guidance of prophets and scriptures, which accustoms man to the conditions of the next world before he arrives :: Yes. To spare him the shock of arriving unprepared.
- Schooling before a career
- Training before a race
- Packing before a holiday
R “God sends His prophets to teach man the conditions that will prevail in the eternal world for which he is bound… and adopt its conditions before he gets there.”
@ adapting-to-a-new-world
Q In what way, says the article, will the next world differ from this one?
* Only the real worth of one’s deeds will count; clever words and unfair means will carry no weight :: Right. Perfect justice will prevail.
- It will be much like this one, only longer
- Success there will depend on rank
- Excuses will be accepted more readily
R “In the next world there will be only one basis of success, and that will be the real worth of one’s own actions.”
@ start-change-with-the-individual
Q A good tree has firm roots and branches in the sky. What principle of change does the article take from it?
* First consolidation, then expansion – begin with the individual before changing society :: Exactly. Otherwise one builds sandcastles.
- Grow upward as fast as possible
- Spread widely before taking root
- Change institutions first, people later
R “Real change in human life can be brought about by following a principle demonstrated in the natural world: first consolidation and then expansion.”
@ the-key-to-social-harmony
Q People differ in ability and success, and envy follows. What attitude does the article ask for?
* Put away envy and spite, give honour where it is due, and see all as equal before God :: Well read. “All men descend from Adam.”
- Insist that everyone achieve the same
- Avoid those who do better
- Hide one’s own success
R “We must discard hardened attitudes of envy, bitterness, spite, and… look upon ourselves and our fellow-men as being equal in the eyes of God.”
