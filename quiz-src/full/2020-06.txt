## 2020-06
@ marvels-of-man
Q Standing before a great monument, the author reflected that no star, tree or animal could have built it. What did he go on to reason from man’s superiority over the rest of creation?
* If a higher order of being exists in man, a still higher Being – God – is entirely conceivable :: Yes. Man is not the “full-stop” of the universe.
- That man needs no one above him
- That monuments should be preserved
- That animals lack feelings
R “When a higher state of existence is present in the form of man, then why should the existence of another higher state in the form of God not be a possibility?”
@ in-search-of-meaning
Q A person earns his living and is content for a time, then feels something is missing. What does the article say he is really looking for?
* Meaning – which is found in discovering God for oneself :: Right. “Man does not live by bread alone.”
- A better-paid position
- More leisure
- Wider fame
R “Bread can satisfy only one’s physical requirements; it fails to give any intellectual satisfaction, or add meaning to one’s life… everyone is in search of God.”
@ in-search-of-meaning
Q What does the article give as the sign of having truly found God, and the one condition of the search?
* A peace like a child’s in its mother’s arms; the condition is sincerity :: Well read. “The one who seeks finds.”
- Great learning; the condition is long study
- Many followers; the condition is eloquence
- Freedom from work; the condition is wealth
R “When you find Him, you will feel at peace with yourself and with the universe, like a little boy in the arms of his mother. The process of search has only one condition to it, and that is, sincerity.”
@ a-most-evident-mystery
Q We are certain of our own existence, yet the “I” cannot be observed. What does the article draw from this?
* What we know by understanding rather than by sight can still be certain – and so it is with God :: Yes. To deny one would mean denying the other.
- That the self is an illusion
- That only the body is real
- That nothing at all can be known
R “The cognizance of ‘I’ is at the level of perception or comprehension, and not at the level of observation. Exactly the same is true of God.”
@ likeness-of-god
Q Which modern example does the article use to make God’s unseen governance of the universe easier to grasp?
* We ourselves direct machines in outer space by remote control :: Correct. If man can, why not the Creator?
- Clocks that run without winding
- Telephones that carry the voice
- Lamps lit by a switch
R “We, ourselves direct the movements of machines in outer space by means of remote control, so why should we have any difficulty in accepting that there is a God who controls the universe by His own invisible system?”
@ god-a-source-of-conviction
Q In a violent storm at sea, a little girl played on calmly with her dolls. What was her explanation?
* Her father was the captain, and he would not let the ship sink :: Yes. A believer trusts God as the captain of his life.
- She did not know there was a storm
- She had sailed many times before
- She had been told to keep quiet
R “You know, my father is the captain of this ship. He is not going to let it sink.”
@ love-of-god
Q On what, according to the article, is the love of God naturally based?
* The discovery that one’s life and all that sustains it are gifts from Him :: Well reasoned. Love is acknowledgement of the Giver.
- Fear of punishment
- The habit of one’s family
- A feeling that cannot be explained
R “When one discovers this fact, one naturally feels a profound love for God. Thus, love of God is the outcome of one’s discovery of God’s munificence.”
@ wonder-of-wonders
Q The chemical elements of a human body are worth very little in the market. What point does the article make with this?
* From such ordinary matter God has made a living, thinking being beyond all price :: Right. A lost hand or eye cannot be bought back.
- That human life is cheap
- That chemistry is a useless science
- That the body should be neglected
R “Out of 12,000 rupees worth of matter, God has created a human being so wonderful that no price can be set upon him.”
@ experiencing-the-presence-of-god
Q An astronaut said that standing on the moon made him feel God’s presence. What does the article say we must do to feel the same on earth?
* Look at familiar things as if seeing them for the first time :: Exactly. Wonder is dulled by habit, not by distance.
- Travel to remote places
- Study astronomy
- Wait for an unusual event
R “We too have to look at everything—not as old and familiar—but as something new and exciting. We have to look at things as if we are seeing them for the first time.”
@ belief-in-god
Q The article contrasts a stuffed tiger in a museum with a living tiger in the forest. What two kinds of belief does this picture?
* Traditional belief, which stirs nothing; and conscious, living belief, which shakes the whole person with awe :: Well read. Only the second has value.
- Belief of the young and belief of the old
- Belief in private and belief in public
- Belief of the learned and belief of the simple
R “A traditional belief in God is a lifeless belief… A conscious belief in God arises from discovering God in all His powers.”
