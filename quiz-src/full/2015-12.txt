## 2015-12
@ ungodly-activities-in-the-name-of-god
Q After the Paris attacks, what example from Japan does the editorial offer to show how a people can be turned from violence?
* After the war, educated Japanese spread peaceful ideas through writing and speech until the national mindset changed :: Yes. Minds were re-engineered, not forced.
- Japan closed its borders for a generation
- Japan punished every former soldier
- Japan replaced its language and customs
R “This miracle occurred by way of dissemination of peaceful ideas through literature and several public speeches and broadcasts.”
@ ungodly-activities-in-the-name-of-god
Q By what, according to the editorial, should Islam be judged?
* By its scripture, not by the acts of those who claim it :: Right. The Quran likens killing one person to killing all mankind.
- By the conduct of the majority
- By the laws of Muslim countries
- By reports in the press
R “Islam should be judged through its scripture and not through the practice of Muslims.”
@ acknowledgment-of-the-truth
Q Why, according to the article, do people fail to accept a truth they could recognise?
* Psychological barriers – it may upset their way of life, lower their standing, or come from someone they look down on :: Yes. Honest thought would have led them to accept it.
- The truth is too hard to understand
- They have not been told it often enough
- Truth changes from age to age
R “They are psychologically inhibited from doing so. Acceptance of the truth might disrupt their materialistic life-pattern. They might have to lower their worldly status.”
@ power-of-an-individual
Q What method of lasting change does the article recommend, citing the Fabian Society?
* Patiently shaping individual minds and forming a small committed team; crowds do not build :: Well read. One sincere person outweighs a thousand half-hearted ones.
- Mass rallies and slogans
- Seizing power first
- Waiting for a great leader
R “Whatever one wants to accomplish at a mass level, one has to adopt the method of addressing individual minds.”
@ a-good-start
Q An elderly scholar gave his daughter and son-in-law his life’s savings in place of a lavish wedding. What is the article’s point?
* A wedding is the start of serious responsibility; money is better spent helping the couple begin than on display :: Correct. They built a business with it.
- Scholars are usually poor
- Weddings should not be celebrated at all
- Parents owe their children a fortune
R “One’s wedding is a very serious event in life, not just an occasion for senseless showing off… would it not be better to avoid ostentation altogether and to think of how best one can help the young couple?”
@ idleness
Q What does the article advise someone who has no financial need to work?
* To turn to higher aims and worthy causes and keep fruitfully occupied :: Yes. Idleness is called a poison.
- To enjoy well-earned leisure
- To leave work to those who need it
- To travel until an interest appears
R “If one is financially independent, one should turn one’s attention to higher things, pursue noble ends, serve worthy causes and keep oneself fruitfully occupied.”
@ thankfulness
Q What does the article name as “the price of heaven”?
* Gratitude – a thankful heart that rises above life’s difficulties :: Right. No one can be completely happy here.
- A trouble-free life
- Great wealth given away
- Long years of solitude
R “The price of heaven is gratitude; only those who have paid this price will enter heaven.”
@ dont-be-a-slave-to-your-desires
Q To what does the article liken desire in the working of a person’s life?
* Grease for the engine, not its driver :: Yes. It may motivate, but reason must steer.
- The fuel, without which nothing moves
- The brakes, which stop every journey
- The road, which decides the destination
R “It is like grease to an engine and not like the driver of that engine… Never judge a desire for its own sake—always judge it in terms of the results of its fulfilment.”
@ patience-source-of-certainty
Q Besides reward in the Hereafter, what benefit of patience in this life does the article describe?
* It saves one from despair and gives the steadiness to plan with confidence :: Well read. It keeps conviction firm.
- It makes others give way
- It brings quick results
- It removes all difficulties
R “Patience helps save you from despair… He lives with a strong sense of certainty. He is able to courageously plan the course of his life.”
@ mars-or-paradise
Q Commenting on plans to make Mars habitable, what does the article say man should work towards?
* Paradise, the future abode in God’s plan – and a change in his own thinking :: Yes. If such technology existed, it should first restore the earth.
- A colony on the moon
- Cities under the sea
- A longer human lifespan
R “The time has come for man to transform his thinking and work towards attaining Paradise, which according to the creation plan of God will be man’s future abode.”
