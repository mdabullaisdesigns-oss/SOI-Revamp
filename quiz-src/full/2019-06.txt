## 2019-06
@ the-spirit-of-modesty
Q The editorial names two things that make a person modest. Which?
* A scientific bent of mind and the fear of God :: Yes. Each makes one see oneself against something far greater.
- Poverty and old age
- Criticism and failure
- Silence and solitude
R “There are two things in this world which make a person modest. One is a scientific bent of mind; the other is being God-fearing.”
@ the-spirit-of-modesty
Q How does a truly scientific mind escape pride, according to the editorial?
* It measures itself against the vastness of knowledge, not against less accomplished people :: Well reasoned. Before knowledge, everyone is small.
- It avoids the company of others
- It never speaks of its achievements
- It studies only small subjects
R “In relation to the person who has knowledge, one can think of oneself as greater than others but in relation to the vast world of knowledge, everyone is less in stature.”
@ missionary-zeal
Q Father Heras could have taught the European history he knew. What does his choice of an unfamiliar subject show, in the article’s view?
* One with a purpose chooses what serves the goal and accepts the labour it costs :: Right. Not what is easiest or best paid.
- He disliked European history
- He wanted a higher salary
- He was told there was no other vacancy
R “If one has a purpose in life, then one will overlook all other considerations and concentrate on achieving one’s goal.”
@ a-happy-ending-to-the-tragedy-of-life
Q Seeing the newly built capital at the close of British rule, a visitor remarked, “What a magnificent world they built to leave.” How does the article apply this to everyone?
* We each build a world here and must leave it; only the Hereafter gives the story a happy ending :: Yes. Without it, life is a tragedy.
- Great cities never last
- Builders are seldom thanked
- One should not build at all
R “Life is a tragedy when seen without the Hereafter. This tragedy can only be given a happy ending with the addition of the life to come.”
@ sir-syed-ahmad-khans-legacy
Q What simple formula does the article credit to Sir Syed Ahmad Khan?
* Ignore the problems and avail of the opportunities :: Correct. Problems and opportunities exist side by side.
- Solve every problem before starting work
- Wait until conditions are ideal
- Protest until grievances are heard
R “He gave the people a viable formula: Ignore the problems, feed the opportunities, and, by availing of opportunities, build your life.”
@ sir-syed-ahmad-khans-legacy
Q Why, according to the article, are reformers so often opposed in their own time and praised later?
* Contemporaries cannot foresee the results of their guidance; later generations can see them :: Well read. Foresight is rare; hindsight is easy.
- Because reformers are usually rude
- Because people dislike the educated
- Because reformers seek opposition
R “Those who nowadays extol his virtues, do so in retrospect, having had the opportunity to see the results of his guidance, whereas his own contemporaries were so lacking in foresight.”
@ a-good-society
Q Several large efforts to build a good society disappointed. What does the article call the basic shortcoming?
* Thinking only in terms of systems, instead of reforming individuals – the building blocks of society :: Right. Reform individuals and society follows.
- Too little money was spent
- The wrong laws were passed
- There were too few leaders
R “Our basic shortcoming is to think purely in terms of systems… The individual is the primary unit of society. If individuals are reformed, society follows suit.”
@ a-good-society
Q What spirit does the article require for a dialogue between communities to succeed?
* Reconciliation, not rivalry – ready to give as well as take, with the result in mind :: Yes. “A brotherly meeting,” not a contest.
- Each side defending its prestige
- Claim and counter-claim until one side yields
- Settling who was at fault in the past
R “The feeling at work behind dialogue is one of reconciliation and not of rivalry… while taking from others they will also be willing to give.”
@ the-message-of-the-quran
Q How does the article describe the opening chapter of the Quran in relation to the rest?
* A miniature picture of the believer, of which the rest of the Quran is the enlargement :: Exactly. Gratitude, submission and a plea for guidance.
- A historical introduction
- A list of the chapters to follow
- A prayer unconnected with what follows
R “The opening chapter of the Quran is a miniature picture of such a believer, and the rest of the Quran an enlargement of this picture.”
@ the-word-of-god
Q In this world a person’s words are weighed by his wealth and following. What, says the commentary, will carry weight on the Day of Judgement?
* Truth alone :: Well read. Falsehood will be left without arguments.
- The number of one’s supporters
- The eloquence of one’s speech
- The grandeur of one’s position
R “Truth alone will be of weight that Day… all the weight will be on the side of the Truth: Falsehood will be divested of all its arguments.”
