@ the-aim-of-islam
Q Some call religion a private matter, others a complete social order. How does the article itself describe the aim of Islam?
* The individual’s discovery of God, which transforms him from within :: Yes. It addresses the individual, not society as a mass.
- The building of a strong state
- The preservation of old customs
- The winning of debates
R “The aim of the religion of Islam is to discover our Creator… Islam addresses itself to the individual, not to society as a whole.”
@ jesus-in-the-islamic-scripture
Q What were the first words of the infant Jesus, as the Quran relates them (19:30)?
* “I am God’s servant. He has given me the Book and made me a prophet” :: Right. Spoken from the cradle, in his mother’s defence.
- “Peace be upon this town”
- “Take me to the temple”
- “I have come to rule”
R “I am God’s servant. He has given me the Book and made me a prophet; He has made me blessed wherever I may be… He has made me dutiful toward my mother.”
@ repetition-in-the-quran
Q Why, according to the article, does the Quran return to the same themes again and again?
* To engrave its message on the mind – what one loves, one gladly hears repeatedly, each time with new meaning :: Well read. As a mother kisses her child again and again.
- Because its chapters were arranged by chance
- Because earlier parts were forgotten
- To make the book longer
R “This has been done in order to engrave the message of the Quran in the minds of its readers… It is only natural that one should repeat something one loves or enjoys.”
@ lessons-from-nature
Q Two swallows’ mud nest collapsed with the eggs in it. What did they do next?
* Found a rougher surface and rebuilt with mud mixed with grass and straw – a stronger nest :: Yes. They corrected the cause of failure.
- Abandoned the house altogether
- Rebuilt the same nest in the same place
- Took over another bird’s nest
R “Whereas the swallows had made their first nest just from mud, they made this one from a mixture of mud, grass and straw.”
@ lessons-from-nature
Q What does the article ask human beings to learn from the swallows?
* After a failure, look for the reason and try again with better planning and harder work :: Correct. “It is a wise man who learns from his mistakes.”
- Build homes only from natural materials
- Avoid tasks that may fail
- Depend on others in hard times
R “If a bird or animal fails in some effort, it seeks to understand the reason for its failure. It does not simply give up… and rectifies its errors by harder work and better planning.”
@ great-goals
Q “Great things are done when men and mountains meet. This is not done by jostling in the street.” How does the article apply Blake’s lines?
* Great aims need patient assessment, sacrifice and long struggle – not slogans and processions :: Well reasoned. Noise is not work.
- Great aims are reached only in the mountains
- Crowds are needed for every great work
- Poets understand life better than leaders
R “Just raising slogans or making speeches at big gatherings does not mean that any great work can be accomplished.”
@ sound-judgement
Q Abu Moosa al-Ashari said a judge should not decide until the truth is “as clear as day.” To whom does the article extend this rule?
* Everyone who forms an opinion about another person – and if the matter is unclear, one should stay silent :: Right. All will answer for their judgements.
- Only to judges in courts
- Only to rulers and governors
- Only to witnesses
R “The principle that Abu Moosa laid down is applicable not only to judges but to every human being as well… if the truth is not ‘as clear as day’ then there is only one thing to do, and that is hold one’s peace.”
@ wisdom-from-the-prophet
Q The Hadith names three things displeasing to God. Which are they?
* Idle talk, asking too many questions, and wasting money :: Yes. Each makes a person a burden on society.
- Sleeping, travelling and trading
- Silence, thrift and caution
- Laughing, eating and resting
R “And three things cause Him immense displeasure: idle talk, asking too many questions and wasting money.”
@ an-identity-card
Q A village boy joined a queue for sweets at a school and was asked for his identity card. What does the story illustrate?
* The rewards of the Last Day are for those who qualified for them beforehand :: Exactly. Turning up at the window is not enough.
- Village children are treated unfairly
- Schools should feed all children
- One should always carry documents
R “The rewards of that day will only be for those who have qualified for them… who, before the coming of that day, have shown themselves deserving of divine approval.”
@ the-practical-approach
Q Man’s mind knows no boundaries, yet his physical existence is tightly limited. What does the article call the great test in this?
* To find a balance between thinking and action, accepting one’s real limits :: Well read. Humility amid apparent greatness.
- To think less and act more
- To ignore the body’s limits
- To avoid all ambition
R “The greatest test of humanity is to discover a balance between thinking and action.”
