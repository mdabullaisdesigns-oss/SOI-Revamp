Q The editorial says the political empires of the past cannot return. What kind of “empire” does it say is open to any group today?
* Peaceful, non-political ones – in education, industry, media and ideas :: Yes. This is what the present age makes possible.
- A military alliance of nations
- A return to rule by kings
- A single world government
R “Building non-political empires is possible for any group in this age… educational empire, industrial empire, media empire, as well as spiritual and ideological empire.”
@ peace-of-mind-for-what
Q What does the article give as the deeper purpose of peace of mind?
* It keeps thinking in a positive direction, which makes intellectual growth and wisdom possible :: Right. Only a peaceful mind draws the right lessons.
- It helps one sleep soundly
- It improves one’s income
- It keeps other people at a distance
R “A peaceful mind is another name for a positive mind… Only a peaceful mind will be able to take the right lessons from these sources.”

- Wealth, travel and leisure
- Memory, habit and custom
- Rank, title and reputation
R “There are three major sources of intellectual development—study, observation and discussion.”
@ progress-in-the-long-haul
Q A newspaper begun on a wooden press in 1782 was, two centuries later, printed by laser and computer. What does the article say allowed it to benefit from each new technique?
* It never stopped publishing – enthusiasm and perseverance kept it going :: Yes. Had it stopped, the techniques would have passed it by.
- It was bought by a rich company
- It moved from city to city
- It had no competitors
R “It was only because of its continued publication that it could benefit from all the new improved techniques… great work requires two things in particular: Limitless enthusiasm and perseverance.”
@ the-mosque
Q The words “Allahu Akbar” – God is great – are repeated throughout the prayer. What does the article say is implied in them?
* That the one saying them is not great – a lesson in modesty :: Yes. It is meant to rid the worshipper of arrogance.
- That the congregation is large
- That the prayer is nearly over
- That the mosque is a great building
R “Implicit in these words is the idea that the person uttering them is not great. Their frequent repetition is a lesson in modesty.”
@ the-mosque
Q Where, according to the article, does the real proof of a worshipper’s submission to God appear?
* In his dealings with other people afterwards, in humility and goodwill :: Well read. The prayer ends with words of peace to all.
- In the length of his prostration
- In the beauty of his recitation
- In how often he visits the mosque
R “Real proof of this submission to God will only become manifest, however, in subsequent dealings with other human beings.”
@ problem-or-challenge

@ mind-boggling-universe
Q The discovery of an immense black hole is said to teach two lessons. Which?
* That the universe has an all-powerful Creator, and that He is merciful in making the earth fit for us :: Yes. Together they instil modesty.
- That the universe is hostile, and that the earth is in danger
- That science is complete, and that religion is not needed
- That the sun is small, and that space is empty
R “On the one hand, they strengthen the conviction that this Universe has an all-powerful Creator. On the other hand… this all-powerful Creator is so Merciful.”
@ love-and-knowledge
Q “A loving heart is the beginning of all knowledge.” What does the article add to make Carlyle’s saying complete?
* That it means loving even where there is cause for hatred :: Exactly. Carlyle himself rose above old prejudice to study the Prophet fairly.
- That love must be returned to be real
- That knowledge must come before love
- That only family can be truly loved
R “Its meaningfulness cannot be properly understood unless these words are added to it, ‘Loving, despite there being a cause for hatred’.”
@ through-fire-and-water
Q According to the remark quoted from Dale Carnegie, what distinguishes a person of sense?
* The ability to profit from losses :: Yes. Anyone can make use of gains.
- The ability to avoid all losses
- The ability to hide losses from others
- The ability to blame losses on bad luck
R “The really important thing is to profit from your losses. That requires intelligence; and it makes the difference between a man of sense and a fool.”
