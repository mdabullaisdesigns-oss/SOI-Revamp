## 2019-11
@ embracing-islam-in-totality
Q The editorial contrasts a stone dropped in water with a dye. What should faith be like in a believer’s life?
* Like dye – blended into his whole being, not a separate object sitting within it :: Yes. “We take on God’s own dye.”
- Like the stone – solid and unchanging
- Like the glass – holding everything in
- Like the water – taking any shape
R “The relationship between Islam and a true believer should be like that of dye and water, not of stone and water.”
@ embracing-islam-in-totality
Q A man asked the Prophet for something that would win him the love of both God and people. What was the reply?
* Renounce the world, and God will love you; renounce what people possess, and they will love you :: Right. Do not covet what is in others’ hands.
- Give gifts to God’s house and to people
- Speak well of God and of people
- Pray much and visit people often
R “If you renounce the world, God will love you; and if you renounce what people possess, they will love you.”
@ islamic-perspectives

* To keep to their own methods – “You know better about your worldly affairs” :: Well read. Proven practical knowledge is welcomed.
- To plant a different crop
- To wait another year and see
- To ask the growers of Makkah
R “Then the Prophet told the date growers to adhere to their own methods, and said, ‘You know better about your worldly affairs’.”
@ belief-in-prophethood
Q Why, according to the article, does man need prophets although he has been given a mind?
* The mind grasps what is apparent, but not unseen realities such as God and the next world :: Yes. The prophet supplies what reason cannot reach.
- Because most people cannot read
- Because the mind works too slowly
- Because laws change in every age
R “This mind can only grasp things that are apparent… so far as God and the next world are concerned, they must remain forever invisible—beyond the reach of human perception.”
@ belief-in-prophethood
Q How does the article describe a prophet in the Islamic sense?
* A human being like others, distinguished only by being chosen as God’s messenger :: Correct. So his life can serve as an example.
- A being who is more than human
- An angel in human form
- A king appointed over his people
R “A prophet in the Islamic sense is no different from any other human being. His uniqueness lies simply in his being the chosen messenger of God.”
@ the-unerasable-past
Q A politician once wiped an interview from the tape with a magnet. What contrast does the article draw with the next world?
* Nothing recorded there can be erased or evaded; it deals only in realities :: Right. No gadget, lawyer or friend will avail.
- Recordings there are kept private
- Only good deeds are recorded there
- Records there fade with time
R “Search as one may, one will never find any instrument which will erase what is indelibly recorded on God’s tape.”
@ salvation-in-the-hereafter
Q The Prophet said that no one’s deeds will by themselves take him to Paradise. How does the article explain this without making deeds unimportant?
* Deeds are required, but Paradise far exceeds their worth – it is granted by God’s mercy, not purchased :: Well reasoned. To neglect deeds would be “wishful thinking.”
- Deeds do not matter at all
- Paradise is given by lot
- Only a prophet’s deeds are counted
R “Paradise will not be granted to anyone without the requisite deeds, but in its ultimate sense… it is granted by the compassion of God.”
@ potent-energy
Q When an atom is broken it releases far greater power. What does the article say of a person who is “broken” by defeat?
* His hidden strength is released and he rises with new resolve :: Yes. Defeat does not ruin; it awakens.
- He is finished for good
- He must wait for others to rebuild him
- He should avoid further effort
R “Just as breakage does not destroy matter, so defeat does not ruin man… When man is beset by defeat, his inner forces are released.”
@ islamic-culture
Q The Quran calls God “Lord of the universe,” not “Lord of the nation.” What does the article read in this?
* That Islam’s outlook is universal, not limited to one people :: Exactly. And the Prophet is called a mercy for the world.
- That nations are unimportant
- That the universe has no borders
- That each nation has its own Lord
R “This shows that Islam believes in unbounded universality and not in limitation.”
@ the-word-of-god
Q Moses went towards a distant fire to fetch a burning brand and received prophethood instead. What does the commentary observe about such gifts?
* God gives a special blessing suddenly and unexpectedly, so that it is attributed wholly to Him with deep gratitude :: Well read. Not to one’s own planning.
- They come only to those who ask for them
- They are given after long notice
- They are earned by travel
R “When Almighty God blesses any subject of His with a special gift, He gives it suddenly and unexpectedly, so that the recipient may attribute it directly to God.”
