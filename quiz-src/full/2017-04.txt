## 2017-04
@ a-sane-voice-from-america
Q The editorial quotes the Quran’s image of a torrent whose foam is carried off while “whatever is of use to man remains behind.” What does it conclude?
* A community must prove useful – a “giver” that stands on merit – to hold a respected place :: Yes. Favours achieve nothing.
- The strong always sweep away the weak
- Rivers should be controlled by dams
- Wealth, like foam, should be thrown away
R “According to the above verse of the Quran, Muslims must prove themselves a ‘giver’ community.”
@ defeat-in-one-arena
Q King Roger II of Sicily had defeated the Muslims in battle. Whom did he choose to prepare his atlas of the world, and what does this show?
* The Muslim scholar Al-Idrisi – learning kept its honour even after military defeat :: Right. Excellence in knowledge is the lasting strength.
- A Norman monk – conquerors trust only their own
- A Greek sailor – maps belong to seafarers
- No one – the atlas was never made
R “Though defeated on the field of battle, Muslims continued their intellectual and academic dominance, even in the court of their conqueror.”

@ more-than-money

- Sent him away with nothing

@ labour-of-a-lifetime

* The believer’s lifelong effort, sustained by faith in the reward of the Hereafter :: Exactly. Strong belief lifts one above circumstances.
- A merchant’s search for profit
- A ruler’s wish for fame
- A student’s hope of a prize
R “As Helen Hoover laboured for over half a century… so the believer labours all his life for the attainment of reward in the Hereafter.”
@ parenting
Q What does the article say is the harm in pampering children?
* It makes them comfort-loving and unable to meet the demands of the world outside the home :: Well reasoned. Caring is good; pampering is not.
- It costs parents too much money
- It makes children too fond of study
- It causes quarrels between parents
R “Pampering fosters an easy-going nature in children… They cannot meet the challenges which they have to face in the external world.”
