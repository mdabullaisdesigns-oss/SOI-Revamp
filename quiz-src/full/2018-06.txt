## 2018-06
@ different-is-beautiful
Q The Makkans had rebuilt the Kabah smaller than Abraham’s original, and the Prophet left it so. What principle does the editorial draw from this?
* Plan on the basis of what remains, rather than spend time recovering what was lost :: Yes. It could be used at once, just as it was.
- Old buildings should never be altered
- Smaller buildings are always better
- Advice from others should be ignored
R “The Prophet letting the Kabah continue in its square shape was an example of re-planning on the basis of what was already there.”
@ different-is-beautiful
Q Why does the editorial call differences of opinion “beautiful”?
* They open the way to dialogue, which triggers new ideas and intellectual growth :: Right. As the falling apple set Newton thinking.
- They show who is right and who is wrong
- They keep people at a safe distance
- They make life more entertaining
R “If there are differences of opinion among people, it paves the way for dialogue. This dialogue becomes the stepping-stone to new thoughts and ideas.”
@ the-arabs-and-the-emergence-of-islam
Q Who were the people known as Hanif among the ancient Arabs?
* Truth-seekers who wished to worship God rightly and readily answered the Prophet’s call :: Well read. “O God, I do not know how You should be worshipped.”
- Traders who led the caravans
- Poets of the desert tribes
- Guardians of the oases
R “These people were truth seekers… These people, true in nature took no time in responding to the call of Prophet Muhammad.”
@ the-arabs-and-the-emergence-of-islam
Q How, according to the article, did the hard and simple life of the desert shape its people?
* It made them realists with qualities such as truthfulness, keeping promises, courage and hospitality :: Yes. Nature was their school.
- It made them fond of luxury
- It made them distrust all strangers
- It left them without any moral code
R “This extremely simple, austere life made them realists. All this was responsible for producing in them the qualities which the Arabs called manliness.”
@ blame-thyself
Q Which three words does the article say people find hardest of all to utter?
* “I am wrong” :: Correct. Self-love resists admitting a mistake.
- “I forgive you”
- “I need help”
- “I am sorry”
R “To utter the three damning words, ‘I am wrong’ is so difficult that in the long history of humankind there have been very few cases of people who have actually brought themselves to utter them.”
@ focused-thinking
Q What “secular aspect” does the article find in belief in the oneness of God?
* It builds the habit of concentration – an integrated mind directed to a single point :: Right. The opposite of a “grasshopper” mind.
- It improves physical health
- It increases one’s wealth
- It reduces the need for study
R “When a believer adopts the belief that God is only one, he develops the habit of concentration… Thus tawhid leads to focused thinking.”
@ love-of-god
Q A pinch of dye colours a whole glass of water. What does this illustrate in the article?
* Love for the Creator naturally spreads into love for all people :: Well reasoned. Love has a practical, social side.
- A small mistake spoils a whole life
- One person can mislead a crowd
- Feelings fade when mixed with duties
R “When a person has love in his heart for his Creator, at the same time he cannot resist showing his love to his neighbours.”
@ islamic-virtues
Q Two men brought the Prophet an inheritance dispute with no witnesses. When he warned that taking another’s due would be like accepting a firebrand, what did they do?
* Each wept and offered his own share to the other :: Yes. He then told them to divide it fairly and draw lots.
- Each demanded that the other swear an oath
- Both withdrew and never spoke again
- They asked for another judge
R “At these words, both the Ansar broke down and wept. ‘Prophet of God!’ they both cried out, ‘he can have my rightful share!’”
@ islamic-virtues
Q Someone addressed Abdullah ibn Umar as “most noble of men, son of the most noble.” How did he reply?
* He rejected the praise: he was just one of God’s servants, and such praise destroys a man :: Exactly. Humility in the face of flattery.
- He thanked the man warmly
- He returned an equal compliment
- He kept silent and smiled
R “I am just one of God’s servants; in Him do I have hope, and Him do I fear. By God, you are bent on destroying a man with such praise.”
@ the-way-of-balance-and-moderation
Q To which sphere does the article say the way of moderation applies?
* Practical dealings with others, so as to live in harmony – not matters of belief and principle :: Right. In principle, the ideal is always sought.
- Religious beliefs above all
- Neither practice nor belief
- Only matters of food and dress
R “Balance is required in practical matters and not in religious beliefs or principles.”
