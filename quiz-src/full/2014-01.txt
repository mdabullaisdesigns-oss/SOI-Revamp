## 2014-01
@ the-key-to-happiness
Q The article says a list of real needs ends in a full stop, but a wish-list has “only commas.” What follows from this?
* Happiness comes from contentment, not abundance, because desires never end :: Yes. “Happiness is not a destination; it is a state of mind.”
- We should write down our wishes and meet them one by one
- Needs and desires are really the same
- Happiness requires giving up all possessions
R “There is always a full-stop in the list of real needs, but the wish-list based on desire has no full-stop… it is only contentment that can give you happiness, not abundance.”
@ the-key-to-happiness
Q What practical benefit of contentment does the author describe from his own life?
* Freedom from distraction, leaving time for study, positive thinking and service :: Right. A happy person is free of tension.
- More wealth than he expected
- Fame among his readers
- Fewer responsibilities
R “This contentment has saved me from all kinds of distractions. And when you are saved from distractions, you have enough time to do all kinds of good things.”
@ nature-a-model-of-peace
Q Stars and planets move constantly yet never collide. What lesson does the article take from them?
* To pursue our own aims without coming into conflict with others :: Yes. Nature knows only positivity.
- To avoid all movement and change
- To keep far away from other people
- To follow whoever is strongest
R “This shows us how to proceed to our destination in life without coming into conflict with others.”
@ life-after-death
Q Mandela’s and Gandhi’s recorded speeches are still heard after their deaths. What argument does the article build on this?
* If one part of the personality – the voice – survives, the whole personality can survive too :: Well reasoned. The Quran says it is “as true as your ability to speak.”
- Great leaders never really die in people’s hearts
- Technology will one day end death
- Only famous people are remembered
R “If a part of our personality is alive after our physical death, why cannot our whole personality be alive after our death?”
@ life-after-death
Q The article notes that the body’s cells are wholly replaced about every ten years. What does it infer?
* The personality stays the same through these changes, so it need not end with the body’s final death :: Exactly. “Personality is changelessness in change.”
- A person becomes someone new every ten years
- The body is more lasting than the mind
- Ageing can be prevented
R “If in spite of nine ‘deaths’, the personality has remained intact, then why must it die when the physical body dies the tenth and final time?”
@ no-shortcuts
Q The article calls the demand for job reservations a kind of shortcut. What does it say truly lifts a community?
* Becoming able to meet competition and challenge :: Correct. Favours “only paralyse its abilities.”
- Stronger political leaders
- Larger grants from government
- Living apart from other communities
R “No amount of favours can uplift any community. The only way to empower a community is to make it capable of meeting the challenges that it faces.”
@ lessons-from-a-road-block
Q What does a “ROAD CLOSED” sign teach about life, according to the article?
* When the direct way is blocked there are other routes, even if slower and less direct :: Yes. They are open to those with “the eyes to see” and the courage to take them.
- A closed road means the journey should be given up
- One should wait until the road reopens
- Obstacles should be removed by force
R “For every major route which is blocked, there are always several minor roads that are open… For every closed door, there is always another which is open.”
@ the-spiritual-tree
Q How does human growth differ from the growth of a seed into a tree?
* A seed grows automatically; a person must develop himself by conscious effort :: Right. Potential is given by nature; unfolding it is our own task.
- A seed needs care; a person grows untended
- A seed has little potential; a person has none at birth
- A seed grows slowly; a person at once
R “A seed grows automatically, following the law of nature. Man however, is required to develop his personality by his own efforts.”
@ the-spiritual-tree
Q A tree takes food from soil, water, air and light. What is the parallel for a person?
* Drawing lessons and strength from every situation, including unwanted and negative ones :: Well read. Controlling anger, for instance, builds the art of turning negative into positive.
- Seeking the company of every kind of person
- Reading books on every subject
- Avoiding hardship so as to grow in peace
R “We must extract this nourishment for our development from all things, including negative things, and unwanted situations.”
@ salah
Q The Prophet compared the five daily prayers to a stream at one’s door in which one bathes five times a day. What did he mean?
* As bathing leaves no dirt on the body, the prayers wash away minor sins :: Yes. Prayer said in its true spirit keeps one from indecency and evil.
- Prayer must be preceded by a full bath
- Prayer is best offered beside water
- Prayer is as easy as bathing
R “The Prophet then explained that, in the same manner, the five daily prayers as ordered by God will erase all minor sins.”
