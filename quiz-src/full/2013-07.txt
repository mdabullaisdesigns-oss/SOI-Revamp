## 2013-07
@ be-aware-of-your-unawareness
Q How does the editorial distinguish discussion from debate?
* Debate aims at proving superiority; discussion aims at mutual learning :: Yes. Differences become a chance to learn.
- Debate is spoken; discussion is written
- Debate is for scholars; discussion is for ordinary people
- Debate ends in agreement; discussion does not
R “The purpose of debate is to establish superiority… The purpose of discussion—on the other hand—is mutual learning; taking something and giving something.”
@ be-aware-of-your-unawareness
Q A reader objected that the author had used examples from secular people. Which saying of the Prophet settled the matter?
* Wisdom is the lost property of a believer; wherever he finds it, he should take it :: Well done. The man replied that his knowledge had increased.
- Seek knowledge from the cradle to the grave
- Actions are judged by intentions
- The best of you are those of best character
R The author quoted: “Wisdom is the lost property of a believer; wherever he finds it, he should take it as his own.”
@ giving-as-god-wills
Q Why does the article say that whatever a person earns is “a direct favour from God”?
* Both his abilities and a world made to serve him are given by God :: Right. So wealth should be spent as God wishes.
- Because wealth comes only to those who pray for it
- Because human effort plays no part in earning
- Because earning is itself an act of worship
R “If the world and that which it contains had not been placed at man’s disposal, his physical and mental capabilities alone would never have enabled him to derive any benefit.”
@ practicable-formula
Q The article mentions Akbar’s Deen-e-Ilahi and Dr Bhagwan Das’s encyclopaedia. What do these examples show?
* Attempts to declare all religions one and the same have not succeeded :: Correct. The workable formula is “Follow one and respect all.”
- Rulers and scholars have always opposed religion
- India has no tradition of tolerance
- Religious unity needs the power of the state
R The article holds that treating all religions as equally true is “unnatural as well as irrational,” and points to the Quran: “For you, your religion; for me, mine.”
@ the-problem-of-evil
Q According to the article, why does suffering caused by human evil exist in the world?
* Man is being tested, which requires freedom, and some misuse it :: Yes. Without freedom, good deeds would earn no credit.
- Everything in history is fixed in advance
- God does not intervene in human affairs
- Suffering is an illusion
R “Man can be granted the credit for a good deed only on the condition that, despite having the opportunity to indulge in bad deeds, he chooses of his own free will to act virtuously.”
@ total-involvement
Q Elias Howe found in a dream where the eye of the sewing-machine needle should go. What does the article say the dream shows?
* That he was so absorbed in his work that it filled even his subconscious mind :: Exactly. Success follows complete involvement.
- That great inventions come by luck
- That dreams foretell the future
- That he should have rested more
R “What is a dream? It is the result of complete involvement… One achieves success in one’s aim only after complete involvement.”
@ religion-and-secularism
Q Why does the article call secularism “a great blessing for religion”?
* As a policy of non-interference, it ended persecution and left religious people free to work :: Well read. The two are called complementary.
- It has made religion part of the government
- It has shown all religions to be the same
- It has replaced religion with science
R “Secularism is a policy of non-interference in religious matters… There is no clash between religion and secularism.”
@ a-fools-paradise
Q What does the article mean by saying that man lives in “a fool’s paradise of his own making”?
* He measures himself by his own standards and worldly success, forgetting the reckoning before God :: Yes. Only God’s standard will count.
- He believes too readily in life after death
- He spends too much time in worship
- He trusts other people too easily
R “People have set up their own standards about what is right and what is wrong… How shocked they will be when they learn that only those were in the right, who lived up to the standard set by God!”
@ eid-ul-fitr
Q Why is charity (sadqa fitr) to be given well before the Eid prayer?
* So that the poor can also take part in the celebration :: Correct. Real happiness lies in sharing.
- So that the giver’s fast is counted
- So that mosques can be decorated
- So that the amount is known in advance
R “Alms should be given well before the Eid day so that the poor may take part in the celebrations as well.”
@ fasting-and-feasting
Q In the article’s reading, what do fasting and the breaking of the fast each represent?
* Fasting stands for this life of test and restraint; the breaking of the fast and Eid for the Hereafter :: Right. Eid gives “a faint taste” of what is to come.
- Fasting stands for the Hereafter; feasting for this world
- Both stand for the hardships of this world
- Fasting stands for the body; feasting for the soul
R “Fasting is a semblance of this test… In contrast to fasting, Iftar (breaking the fast), presents a semblance of life Hereafter.”
