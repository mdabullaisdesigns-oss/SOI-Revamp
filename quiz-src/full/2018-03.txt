## 2018-03
@ positive-aspects-of-failure
Q What does the article mean by a “mistake-plus”?
* A mistake that leads to introspection and so awakens the mind to re-plan better :: Yes. Regret alone only disheartens.
- A mistake made twice
- A mistake that harms others too
- A mistake hidden from everyone
R “A mistake becomes a mistake-plus when it awakens your mind. It thus enables you to re-plan your life.”
@ positive-aspects-of-failure
Q Which simple formula does the article suggest feeding into one’s thinking in times of difficulty?
* “It is all but temporary” :: Right. No difficulty goes on forever.
- “It is someone else’s fault”
- “It could not be helped”
- “It will always be so”
R “Every difficulty is bound to disappear after some time… So, you have to feed this simple formula into your thinking, ‘It is all but temporary’.”
@ universal-brotherhood
Q The article calls one principle “a master key to human ethics.” Which?
* Treat others as you would like them to treat you :: Well read. “Like for his brother what he likes for himself.”
- Keep to your own people
- Speak only when spoken to
- Give more than you receive
R “It is the principle of treating others just as one would like to be treated by them… one single key which suffices to open all locked doors.”
@ intentions
Q How does the article regard a religious act performed for public praise or worldly gain?
* As ill-intentioned and without value before God, whatever its outward look :: Correct. God knows the inner reality.
- As acceptable if done correctly
- As better than doing nothing
- As a matter between the person and society
R “Ostensibly religious acts, if performed for worldly gain or public commendation, are in this sense ill-intentioned.”
@ on-the-eve-of-exams
Q With whom does the article advise a student to compete?
* With his own better self – discovering and developing his particular ability :: Yes. Not blindly with classmates.
- With the top scorer in the class
- With students of other schools
- With his brothers and sisters
R “Discovering one’s potential and turning it into reality is what is important rather than just blindly competing with others… The competition should be with your own better self.”
@ on-the-eve-of-exams
Q What should parents chiefly give their children at examination time, according to the article?
* Wise counsel and the principles of life – patience, realism, learning from failure – rather than pressure :: Right. Topping exams does not guarantee topping in life.
- Constant questioning about their preparation
- Rewards for every mark gained
- Comparison with other children
R “They must limit themselves to doing wise counselling. It would be counterproductive if they constantly question their children and apply mental pressure on them to perform.”
@ the-money-culture
Q “Money is a good helper. But it is not a good leader.” What does the article mean?
* Money should serve one’s needs and higher purposes, not become the goal of life :: Well reasoned. Man is greater than money.
- Money should be given to leaders
- Wealthy people make poor rulers
- Money should be avoided altogether
R “Money for the sake of money is bad, but money for the sake of some higher purpose is good; money as a necessity is good, but the money culture per se is a disaster.”
@ example-of-the-prophet
Q How widely did the Prophet define charity?
* Every good act – a smile, showing the way, removing a thorn from the road, a kind word :: Yes. Not only alms and feeding the poor.
- Only money given in secret
- Only what is given to relatives
- Only gifts made on festivals
R “He believed that every good act was a form of charity; smiling at a fellow human being; showing the road to a person who has lost his way; removing hindrances such as thorns and stones from the road…”
@ example-of-the-prophet
Q Asked how the Prophet lived at home, what did Aisha say?
* Like any ordinary man – he swept, mended his sandals, milked the goats and helped the servants :: Exactly. Simple living and humility.
- He kept apart and was served by others
- He spent the whole day in the mosque
- He left household matters to servants
R “‘Like any ordinary man’, she would answer. ‘He would sweep the house, stitch his own clothes, mend his own sandals… help the servants at their work, and eat his meal with them.’”
@ before-death-and-after-death
Q The article says keeping death in mind transforms life. Which change does it mention?
* One sees how absurd it is to trouble others or feel superior, since one must answer for it :: Well read. All we hold is “testing material.”
- One loses interest in work
- One gathers more possessions quickly
- One avoids meeting people
R “It will make you realize… how absurd it is to trouble others, because you will have to answer for this after death… It will make you ashamed of thinking yourself to be superior to others.”
