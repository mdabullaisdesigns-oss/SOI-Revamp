## 2020-10
@ global-library
Q The editorial calls every human being “a book.” What does it say will happen to that book on the Day of Judgement?
* It will be opened before him, and he will have to read the life he himself has written :: Yes. In his own language, whether he likes it or not.
- It will be read only by others
- It will be lost with his death
- It will be summarised in a single line
R “Man will be compelled to read the book he has written. This autobiography of man will be in his own comprehensible language.”
@ global-library
Q What does the editorial call the most serious aspect of this?
* There is no second edition – the story cannot be rewritten afterwards :: Right. The first edition is in our hands now.
- The book is very long
- Others will add to it
- It is written in haste
R “No one will have the opportunity to write a second edition of their life story. Writing the first edition of one’s biography is in the hands of every human being.”
@ striking-a-balance
Q “When the prayer is ended, disperse in the land and seek God’s bounty” (62:10). What balance does the article draw from these verses?
* Earning a living is fully allowed, but stays subordinate to one’s duties to God, which take precedence when they call :: Well read. Then one returns freely to work.
- Work and worship should never be mixed in one day
- Trade is to be avoided by the devout
- Prayer may be postponed for business
R “Economic activity is a natural requirement of human existence… However, it is essential that economic activities be subordinated to religious obligations.”
@ striking-a-balance
Q Which of these is among the conditions the article sets for a believer’s economic activity?
* To regard what he earns as God’s blessing and to keep within the limits God has set :: Yes. And to remember God throughout.
- To earn more than his neighbours
- To work only for himself
- To keep his earnings secret
R “Earning from economic activities should be considered a blessing of God… Economic activities should not be allowed to cross the limits imposed by God.”
@ the-superior-solution
Q Life is full of unpleasant experiences, yet man is asked to be grateful. How, says the article, is that possible?
* Only through patience – enduring difficulties is what makes thankfulness possible :: Well reasoned. The Quran pairs the two.
- By ignoring whatever is unpleasant
- By comparing oneself with the fortunate
- By waiting for perfect conditions
R “It is only when a person patiently endures worldly problems that it becomes possible for him to express his feeling of gratefulness to God.”
@ the-superior-solution
Q Why does the article call patience “the superior solution” when facing an adversary?
* It keeps one able to think and respond rationally, instead of reacting on impulse :: Correct. Impulse fails; reason succeeds.
- It makes the adversary feel ashamed at once
- It avoids the need to act at all
- It wins the sympathy of onlookers
R “When he adheres to patience and tolerance, he finds himself competent to make a rational move instead of an impulsive reaction.”
@ positivity-the-fertile-ground-for-god-realization
Q A plant grows only in fertile soil. What does the article name as the soil in which God-realization grows?
* A positive personality :: Right. One not ruled by reaction to circumstances.
- A wealthy household
- A quiet village
- A long education
R “The attainment of God-realization is possible only on the fertile soil of a positive personality.”
@ positivity-the-fertile-ground-for-god-realization
Q How does the article describe a “negative personality”?
* One who reacts to his surroundings, cannot rise above circumstances, and always looks at the dark side :: Yes. Such ground stays barren.
- One who speaks little
- One who prefers solitude
- One who asks many questions
R “A man with a negative personality is one who is of a reactionary disposition. He succumbs to the influence of his environment… He will always look at the negative aspects of events.”
@ half-the-story
Q Besides being unjust, what two practical harms does the article see in telling only the other side’s faults?
* It stops a right attitude from developing in oneself, and it leaves the problem unsolved :: Exactly. Satisfaction for the teller, no use to anyone.
- It takes too long and bores the listener
- It makes enemies and costs money
- It is soon forgotten and must be repeated
R “There are two disadvantages of such reporting. First, it puts a stop to the development of right attitude, and second, it does not solve the problem.”
@ the-word-of-god
Q “Have you considered the water that you drink?… If We so pleased, We certainly could make it salty.” What natural process does the commentary point to?
* Rain as a vast desalination system: pure water rises from the sea and the salt stays behind :: Well read. A reason for gratitude.
- Rivers filtering water through sand
- Ice storing water on mountains
- Wells drawing water from rock
R “When water vapour rises from the sea, pure water rises and the salt is left below. The fact is that the system of rain is a huge universal desalination process.”
