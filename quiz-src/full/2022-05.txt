@ the-existence-of-god
Q What does the article call the greatest proof of the existence of God?
* The existence of man himself – a conscious being who thinks, wills and acts :: Yes. To accept one’s own existence is to have grounds for accepting God’s.
- The size of the universe
- The age of the earth
- The testimony of ancient books
R “The greatest proof of the existence of God is the existence of man himself.”

* Faith should be explained to them with reasons, not handed over as a list of dos and don’ts :: Well read. This is the Age of Reason.
- They should be addressed in a louder voice

@ clarity-in-discourse

* Clear thinking beforehand – knowing what is relevant to the subject and what is not :: Yes. Clear words follow clear thought.
- A large vocabulary
- A pleasing voice

@ through-the-eyes-of-the-intellect

- The Caliph told a joke about washermen
- He recognised his old teacher among the guests
- He remembered that he had forgotten his mother
R “Imam Abu Hanifa said to my mother, ‘The knowledge he is acquiring will earn him pistachio faloodah’… One day, I was offered pistachio porridge.”
@ through-the-eyes-of-the-intellect

* By rising above surface appearances to look for the facts that lie behind them :: Right. Like turning from what is near to see what is far.

- By trusting first impressions
R “To develop this eye, a person needs to rise above the superficial observation. The facts lie behind the visible phenomena.”

@ natural-simplicity
Q On his pilgrimage the Prophet began the walk between the two hills at Safa, saying, “I am starting where God started.” What does the article see in this?
* A preference for simplicity – following the order in the Quran spared people from remembering two different sequences :: Well reasoned. Free of needless complication.
- A wish to finish the rite quickly
- A custom older than the Quran
- A choice made at random
R “The Prophet adopted the same sequence in pilgrimage as had been followed in the Quran, thus saving people the trouble of remembering two different orders. Simplicity is a factor essential to the spirit of Islam.”
