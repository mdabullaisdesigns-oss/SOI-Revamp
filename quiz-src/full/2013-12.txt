## 2013-12
@ make-it-a-starting-point
Q A Malaysian court barred Christians from using the word “Allah.” How does the editorial suggest Malaysian Muslims should see the matter?
* As common ground and a starting point for dialogue about the oneness of God :: Yes. The Quran invites to “a word common to us.”
- As an insult that calls for protest
- As a legal matter best left to the courts
- As proof that the two faiths cannot live together
R “Historically, the word ‘Allah’ has never been the monopoly of Muslims… Malaysian Muslims should welcome it and avail it as an opportunity for spiritual exchange.”
@ dua-according-to-the-quran-and-the-bible
Q Why does the article call dua (supplication) “the essence of worship”?
* It springs from realising that God is the Giver and man wholly dependent on Him :: Right. That realisation turns a person to God at once.
- It is the easiest act of worship to perform
- It can replace all other duties
- It must be said in a fixed form of words
R “When Man discovers God through his absolute attributes, he also discovers that, in comparison to God, he has no significance at all… it makes him immediately turn to God in supplication.”
@ principle-of-salvation
Q The article describes how, over generations, a prophet’s community changes its idea of salvation. What is the change?
* Good tidings given for faith and deeds come to be claimed as the birthright of the group :: Exactly. Religion turns into “a kind of national heritage.”
- People stop believing in the Hereafter
- The community forgets its prophet’s name
- Rituals grow fewer and simpler
R “Tidings which had been given on the basis of faith and righteous actions come to be considered to be the result of ethnic affiliations… But God does not have a special relationship with any particular community.”
@ freedom-of-expression
Q What happens, according to the article, in a society that takes away freedom of expression?
* People go on thinking but hide their thoughts, so it fills with hypocrites and stagnates :: Yes. Sincerity and creativity both need freedom.
- People stop thinking and live in harmony
- People become more religious
- People turn to science instead of opinion
R “If people are deprived of the right to free expression, it will not stop them from thinking, but it will certainly stop them from expressing their thoughts… [it] will finally be full of hypocrites.”
@ journey-towards-final-destination
Q Sir James Jeans wrote that man seems to have “strayed into a world that was not made for him.” How does the article explain this feeling?
* Man’s nature seeks complete fulfilment, which this limited world cannot give; his true home is Paradise :: Well reasoned. The earth is a selection ground.
- Man has damaged the earth beyond repair
- Man came to earth from another planet
- Man’s desires are a sickness to be cured
R “There is a contradiction between man’s nature, which seeks fulfilment of his desires, and the world, which is incapable of fulfilling man’s desires… Man’s eternal habitat lies in the world Hereafter.”
@ negus-the-king-of-abyssinia
Q The Makkan envoys asked King Negus to hand over the Muslim migrants without questioning them. What did he do?
* He refused to decide until he had heard the migrants himself :: Yes. Justice means hearing both sides.
- He accepted the gifts and sent the migrants back
- He expelled both groups from his land
- He asked his courtiers to decide
R Negus said: “I will never hand them over to you, unless they are called before me and I speak to them.” The article says judging on one side’s account is “a form of oppression.”
@ negus-the-king-of-abyssinia
Q Which of these did NOT sway Negus in reaching his decision?
* All of them left him unmoved – gifts, his courtiers’ advice and the migrants’ differing belief about Jesus :: Correct. He “focused only on the issue of justice.”
- Only the gifts; the courtiers persuaded him
- Only his courtiers; the gifts pleased him
- Only the difference of belief; he took offence
R “Negus was not influenced by the gifts of the Makkan delegation. Nor did he accept the advice and recommendations of his courtiers… The King did not bother about all this.”
@ discover-thanksgiving
Q The article uses the electric light switch as an example. What is it meant to teach?
* Gratitude has to be discovered by reflection; one who does not acknowledge people will not acknowledge God :: Right. We take blessings for granted.
- Modern inventions distract us from God
- Scientists deserve more thanks than anyone
- Electricity should be used sparingly
R “Shukr is not a ready-made thing. We have to apply our intellect to discover it… when they fail to acknowledge man, they also fail to acknowledge God.”
@ revealed-knowledge-and-secular-knowledge
Q After the date-palm growers of Medina had a poor crop, the Prophet told them, “You know the matters of the world better than I do.” What principle does the article draw?
* Matters governed by the laws of nature belong to observation and experiment, not to religious ruling :: Exactly. This separation opened the door to science.
- Farmers need not follow religion
- Prophets should not speak on daily life
- Tradition is always wiser than new ideas
R “What is demonstrable in nature, yielding itself to research and experiment, will be accepted by Islam as established, empirical knowledge.”
@ inter-faith-harmony
Q Why, in the author’s view, do interfaith conferences achieve so little?
* They treat a social question – how people of different faiths live together – as a theological one :: Yes. The answer is peaceful coexistence, not unity of religions.
- They are held too rarely
- They exclude ordinary believers
- They are opposed by governments
R “The problem is basically social, rather than theological… the ‘unity of faiths’ and ‘social unity of people of different faiths’ are two different and distinct issues.”
