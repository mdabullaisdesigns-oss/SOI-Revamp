## 2021-07
@ carrying-on-the-legacy-and-mission-of-maulana
Q Before his death, Maulana Wahiduddin Khan recorded a message on how his students should respond to the news of his passing. What were they to say?
* “Maulana is no more, but God is still alive!” :: Yes. As Abu Bakr said at the Prophet’s death – God alone is eternal.
- “The mission has ended with him”
- “Who will lead us now?”
- “Let us wait for another guide”
R “If tomorrow you receive the news, Maulana is no more, what should be your reaction? You should immediately exclaim, Maulana is no more, but God is still alive!”
@ the-sublimity-of-spiritual-experience
Q When Zakaria saw the spiritual provision granted to Mary and asked where it came from, what did she reply?
* “This is from God” :: Right. Such inner states are a gift, not of one’s own making.
- “This is from my family”
- “This is from my own effort alone”
- “I do not know”
R “‘Mary, where did this provision come from?’ She replied, ‘This is from God.’ (3: 37)”
@ prophetic-model-of-success
Q The Prophet met the severest hardships yet achieved great success. Which Quranic assurance does the article connect with this?
* “Surely with every hardship there is ease” (94:5) :: Well read. So there is no ground for despair.
- “Man was created impatient”
- “Every soul shall taste death”
- “Wealth and children are a trial”
R “This is what the Quran means by saying that ‘Surely with every hardship there is ease.’ (94: 5)”
@ the-spirit-of-selfless-devotion
Q When people work together, credit is never shared evenly. What does the article call the only way for a common effort to prosper?
* For people to forget their rights and remember their responsibilities :: Yes. The work itself is reward enough.
- For every reward to be divided equally
- For one person to do all the work
- For credit to be decided by vote
R “There is only one way for a common effort to prosper, and that is for people to forget about their rights, and remember only their responsibilities.”
@ train-your-thinking
Q A Persian couplet says two people hear the same door differently. How?
* One hears it being shut; the other hears it being opened :: Right. The difference lies in the mind.
- One hears it creak; the other hears nothing
- One hears a knock; the other hears a voice
- One hears it lock; the other hears it break
R “You hear the sound of a door being shut, while I hear the sound of a door being opened.”
@ train-your-thinking
Q What does the article call the “in spite of” principle?
* Staying positive and moving ahead despite opposition and unpleasantness, since these will always be there :: Well reasoned. Reaching the flowers despite the thorns.
- Giving up when conditions are unfavourable
- Refusing to deal with difficult people
- Complaining until matters improve
R “In spite of all opposition, he must attempt to incline people in his favour; in spite of there being a great deal of unpleasantness in this life, he must learn the secret of harmonious living.”
@ the-kings-dream
Q How did the Prophet Yusuf explain the king’s dream of the cows and the ears of corn?
* Seven years of good harvests would be followed by seven years of drought :: Yes. So the plenty had to be stored.
- Seven enemies would attack the kingdom
- Seven rivers would flood the land
- Seven sons would be born to the king
R “Seven fat cows means seven years of good harvests. After the first seven years, there’ll be seven more years of drought.”
@ the-kings-dream
Q Made minister, what did Yusuf do that kept the kingdom safe when the drought came?
* He had the surplus grain of the good years saved and stored :: Correct. Wisdom joined with foresight.
- He bought grain from other lands
- He moved the people elsewhere
- He dug canals to the sea
R “The new minister made sure that the extra wheat was saved and stored. When the water dried up and the drought finally came, the kingdom was safe once more.”
@ the-valley-of-ants
Q Marching with his army, the Prophet Sulayman heard an ant warn the others. What did he do?
* He smiled, halted his army so the ants could reach safety, and thanked God for His gifts :: Exactly. Power used with care for the smallest creature.
- He ordered the army to march faster
- He took another road without stopping
- He ignored the ants
R “He stopped his army so the ants could scatter to safety… Then the Prophet Sulayman thanked Allah for the gifts that He had given him.”
@ the-word-of-god
Q “Man is born impatient: when misfortune touches him he laments, and when good fortune comes he grows niggardly.” Whom do the verses (70:19-35) except from this?
* Those steadfast in prayer, who share their wealth with the needy and are faithful to their trusts and pledges :: Well read. Faith steadies both grief and plenty.
- Those who are born wealthy
- Those who live far from others
- Those who never face misfortune
R “But not so the worshippers who are steadfast in prayer; those who give a due share of their wealth to those who ask [for help] and to the destitute… and those who are faithful to their trusts and to their pledges.”
