## 2014-04
@ seven-billion-issues
Q The editorial recalls that motor vehicles created a problem on the roads which led to traffic rules. What does this example show about differences between people?
* Differences pose challenges, and challenges stir new thinking and progress :: Yes. They are to be managed, not removed.
- Differences must be controlled by strict laws
- Differences arose only in modern times
- Differences are best avoided by living apart
R “Differences create challenges. Challenges lead to new thinking. And new thinking results in progress and development… learn the art of ‘difference management’.”
@ the-jungle-of-forbidden-trees
Q How does the article compare Adam’s test in Paradise with our test on earth?
* The test is the same, but where he had one forbidden tree we live in a whole jungle of them :: Right. Whatever makes us forget God and the Hereafter is such a tree.
- His test was hard; ours is easy
- He was tested; we are not
- He had many forbidden trees; we have one
R “In Paradise there was only one forbidden tree, and in this world there is a jungle of ‘forbidden trees’… Only the one who examines his life and continuously introspects will be able to save himself.”
@ this-number-does-not-exist
Q What spiritual point does the article make from the recorded message “This number does not exist”?
* A call addressed to anything other than the one real God goes unanswered :: Yes. The first duty is to discover the real God.
- God answers only at certain times
- Prayer needs the right form of words
- Technology has made faith harder
R “If he gives the status of God to a non-god… the answer they receive from all of them, albeit in silent language, is that the number dialed does not exist.”
@ miracles-or-signs-of-god
Q In the article’s reading, what universal law was shown when Moses’ staff became a serpent?
* The law of conversion – that things in nature are constantly changed from one form to another :: Well reasoned. Seed to tree, egg to chick, matter to energy.
- The law of gravity
- The law that the strong overcome the weak
- The law that miracles belong only to prophets
R “Prophet Moses had demonstrated to people a universal and common law of God. This is the law of conversion… Conversion proves intervention, and when intervention is proved, the intervener is also proved.”
@ heavenly-personality
Q The Prophet said that those whose hearts are like the hearts of little birds will enter heaven. What quality does the article see in this likeness?
* A heart free of hatred and revenge – kept so by conscious choice :: Exactly. A bird has it by instinct; a person must choose it.
- A life free of all cares
- A love of travel and freedom
- A habit of rising early
R “A heavenly personality is one that is completely free of all negativity. A bird has this positive quality by virtue of its instincts, whereas a heavenly personality has this quality as a conscious choice.”
@ more-than-legal-rights
Q The physicist Satyendra Nath Bose was once given 110 marks out of 100. What lesson does the article draw?
* Prove yourself able and you receive more than your due – so choose “opportunity activism” over demanding rights :: Yes. Be a “giver-member” of society.
- Examiners often make mistakes
- Rules should be relaxed for the gifted
- Rights are won only by protest
R “If a person proves himself competent enough, he will be awarded more than his legal rights… the greatest empowerment is self-empowerment.”
@ blaming-others
Q The inventor of the AK-47 said he slept well because politicians were to blame. What does the article call this attitude, and what does it set against it?
* The “blame others” culture of Satan, against the “blame thyself” culture of the angels :: Correct. A wrongdoer’s proper course is to repent.
- Honesty, against false modesty
- Patriotism, against selfishness
- Realism, against idealism
R “The ‘blame thyself’ culture is the culture of angels, while the ‘blame others’ culture is the culture of Satan.”
@ political-realism
Q Why, according to the article, did the Prophet advise his followers not to enter into conflict with rulers?
* No political system here can be ideal, and endless conflict over it destroys the peace that constructive work needs :: Right. Egypt’s sixty years are given as the example.
- Rulers are always in the right
- Religion has nothing to say about justice
- Rulers punish opposition harshly
R “In accordance with the law of nature, no perfect or ideal political system can come into being in this world… practical wisdom demands that people should agree to settle with a less than ideal political system.”
@ surah-al-asr
Q In Surah Al-Asr, time is called to witness that man is in loss. Who are named as the exception?
* Those who believe, do good deeds, and urge one another to truth and steadfastness :: Yes. Imam Shafi said this chapter alone would suffice as guidance.
- Those who live to a great age
- Those who gather wealth for their children
- Those who withdraw from the world
R “Man is surely in a state of loss, except for those who believe and do good deeds and exhort one another to hold fast to the Truth, and who exhort one another to steadfastness.”
@ divorce-in-islamic-law
Q Why does the method of divorce given in the Quran extend over three months?
* To leave room for anger to cool and for the couple to reconcile :: Well read. Three pronouncements in one breath are called a misuse of the law.
- To allow time for dividing property
- To let the families choose new spouses
- To make divorce impossible in practice
R “This method of divorce over a period of three months was devised so that if the husband, in a fit of anger, wants to divorce his wife but later, when his temper cools down, changes his mind… he can do so.”
