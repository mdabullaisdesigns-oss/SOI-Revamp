## 2018-08
@ religious-base-plus-objective-thinking
Q A teacher asked his class whether a camel’s hoofs resemble a horse’s or a buffalo’s, and no one could say. What saying did he then teach them?
* “To know that you do not know is half of knowledge” :: Yes. That awareness makes one start to notice – the spirit of inquiry.
- “Knowledge is found only in books”
- “He who asks is a fool for a moment”
- “Seeing is believing”
R “He quoted an Arabic saying: To know that you do not know is half of knowledge… since all of us did not know that we did not know about a camel’s hoofs, we never paid attention even when camels went past us.”
@ religious-base-plus-objective-thinking
Q The author says the madrasa gave him his religious base. What did his later study of modern thought add?
* Objective thinking – forming views by unbiased, logical analysis :: Right. “An objective thinker is a right thinker.”
- A dislike of traditional learning
- Skill in public speaking
- A wish to live abroad
R “The greatest thing I acquired from studying modern thought was objective thinking. I had not learned this at the madrasa.”
@ hajj-the-pilgrimage
Q Of what does the gathering of pilgrims on the plain of Arafat remind them?
* The Day of Reckoning, when all will stand before God :: Well read. All alike, in simple dress, with one call.
- The battles of early history
- The markets of old Makkah
- The journey of the caravans
R “The plain of Arafat, where the pilgrims spend one whole day is like a picture of the Day of Reckoning.”
@ gratitude
Q Why does the article say that one who does not thank people is unlikely to be grateful to God?
* A person’s favour is plain to see, while God’s favours are grasped only by reflection – failing at the easier, one fails at the harder :: Well reasoned. Ingratitude becomes a habit of mind.
- Because people give more than God
- Because thanking people takes no effort
- Because God does not need thanks
R “One who fails to perceive an event which is directly observable cannot be expected to grasp something which can be apprehended only after a great deal of cogitation.”
@ islamic-virtues
Q The lamp flickered while Caliph Umar ibn Abdul Aziz sat with a guest, who offered to wake the servant. What did the Caliph do?
* Got up and filled the lamp himself – he was the same man before and after :: Yes. Rank does not excuse one from small tasks.
- Sat on in the dark
- Asked the guest to fill it
- Woke the servant himself
R “I was Umar ibn Abdul Aziz before I put oil in the lamp, and I am still Umar ibn Adul Aziz.”
@ islamic-virtues
Q Abu Hazim was asked how one gives thanks for one’s eyes. What did he answer?
* When you see good, speak of it; when you see evil, pass over it :: Correct. And likewise with what one hears.
- By keeping them lowered at all times
- By reading as much as possible
- By looking only at beautiful things
R “When you behold good, make mention of it and when you behold evil, pass over it.”
@ to-make-up-for-our-helplessness
Q Man has no power of his own. What, according to the article, has God given to make up for this?
* Supplication – one who earnestly asks will be given :: Right. His very helplessness, offered in prayer, becomes his means.
- Wealth to buy what he needs
- Authority over other creatures
- A long life in which to try again
R “God has shown us special mercy: He has made supplication sufficient to ensure that we receive.”
@ the-veil-of-satan
Q The article speaks of a “veil” that keeps people from seeing divine signs in nature. What form does it take?
* Taking everything for granted as mere cause and effect, and looking only for material gain :: Yes. So no lesson is drawn.
- Poor eyesight in old age
- Living far from the countryside
- Lack of scientific instruments
R “Satan tries to make man take everything around him for granted and not ponder or reflect upon them.”
@ what-is-truth
Q “The sun cannot overtake the moon… each floats in its own orbit.” What lesson for human society does the article take from this?
* If each person keeps to his own sphere, friction and conflict are avoided :: Exactly. As the heavenly bodies never collide.
- The strong should lead and the weak follow
- People should avoid one another
- Society should never change
R “If man were to apply this principle to his life, everyone would operate in his own given sphere, thus rectifying the friction which exists in human society.”
@ the-recognition-of-truth
Q Some asked why the Quran was not revealed to “some great man of the two towns.” What test does the article see in this?
* To recognise truth on its own merit, not because a person of rank presents it :: Well read. Judging by outward grandeur has no value.
- To honour the leaders of one’s town
- To wait for the majority to decide
- To prefer what is new to what is old
R “Acknowledgement of the truth—and here is the greatest test for man—is valid only when the truth recommends itself by virtue of its own merit.”
