## 2013-11
@ nairobi-and-peshawar-attacks-completely-anti-islamic
Q Whom does the editorial hold most responsible for attacks such as those in Peshawar and Nairobi?
* The leaders who gave Islam a political interpretation :: Yes. He calls political Islam “the greatest evil in the present age.”
- The governments of the countries attacked
- The foreign media
- The victims’ own communities
R “The real blame goes less to the attackers themselves than to their intellectual mentors. The real culprits are those leaders who have given a political interpretation to Islam.”
@ nairobi-and-peshawar-attacks-completely-anti-islamic
Q Some quote the verse “do not take the Jews and the Christians for friends” (5:51) to justify hatred. How does the editorial explain the verse?
* It was a temporary instruction about those then at war with the Prophet, not a rule about all Jews and Christians :: Correct. It gives no ground for hostility today.
- It applies to all non-Muslims for all time
- It was later removed from the Quran
- It refers only to trade
R “The Quranic verse quoted above is neither about all the Jews or Christians, nor is it a permanent teaching. It is a temporary instruction.”
@ the-fear-of-god
Q Philosopher-kings, collective ownership and home rule were each expected to end oppression. What does the article say they all lacked?
* The fear of God, which alone keeps those in power to truth and justice :: Exactly. Changing systems only changed the hands that held power.
- Enough time to prove themselves
- Trained administrators
- The support of other nations
R “The only factor that can affect true reform is the fear of God. Nothing else can hold a man in power to the observance of proper standards of truth and justice.”
@ wisdom-in-retreat
Q Imam Hasan had an army of 60,000, yet gave up the Caliphate after six months. What did his stepping back achieve?
* It ended civil war, so that Muslim energies turned to constructive work :: Yes. “Sometimes stepping back is the way to move forward.”
- It gave his family the Caliphate later
- It made Kufa the capital of the empire
- It forced his rival to share power
R “With Imam Hasan’s stepping back, internecine fighting among Muslims was replaced by internal solidarity.” The article calls it wise pragmatism, not cowardice.
@ the-message-from-the-quran
Q The angels feared man would shed blood on earth. Why, in the article’s explanation, did God still create him?
* Among mankind would be those who freely choose to submit to God – few, but as precious as grain amid straw :: Well read. Their worth justifies the whole field.
- The angels’ fear was mistaken
- Man would be given no freedom to do wrong
- The earth needed someone to cultivate it
R “They would be comparatively few in number, but they would be as precious as the grains of a crop. Grain is far outweighed by hay and straw, but so great is its value…”
@ the-greater-jihad
Q Returning from the Tabuk campaign, the Prophet spoke of going “from lesser jihad to greater jihad.” What is the greater jihad?
* The struggle against one’s own evil desires :: Right. It continues every day of a believer’s life.
- A larger military campaign
- The journey of pilgrimage
- The duty of ruling a state
R “The ‘lesser jihad’ is a military struggle, while the ‘greater jihad’ is the struggle against one’s own evil desires.”
@ war-as-an-instrument-to-acquire-power
Q Why does the article say it is no longer necessary to struggle for political power?
* Influence has shifted from politics to institutions, which are open to everyone :: Yes. Education, media, research and industry now do what power once did.
- Political power has been abolished
- Religion forbids all interest in public life
- Rulers today always act justly
R “The shift of the focus of influence from politics to institutions has rendered political power into nothing but a political headache.”
@ a-good-society
Q National government, a non-violent political movement, and legislation each failed to produce a good society in India. What, says the article, was the shared mistake?
* Thinking in terms of systems instead of reforming individuals :: Exactly. The individual is the building block of society.
- Copying foreign models
- Acting too slowly
- Spending too little money
R “Our basic shortcoming is to think purely in terms of systems… If individuals are reformed, society follows suit.”
@ key-to-peace
Q What is the author’s “scientific” understanding of peace?
* Peace is the absence of war; justice is a separate goal to be worked for afterwards :: Correct. Insisting on both together loses both.
- Peace exists only where full justice has been done
- Peace is a balance of military strength
- Peace means the victory of the rightful side
R “The purpose of peace is only to normalise the situation so that one may uninterruptedly avail of the opportunities that are present.” Hudaibiya and post-war Japan are given as examples.
@ belief-and-disbelief
Q Every person, says the article, needs something to look up to. How does the believer differ from others in meeting this need?
* He passes beyond created things, however grand, to the Creator Himself :: Yes. He is not overawed by wealth or great men.
- He gives up the need altogether
- He looks up only to the wisest of people
- He relies on himself alone
R “To believe in God is to look up to Him alone, while disbelief is to live in veneration of others besides Him… He presses on until, passing by all creation, he reaches the Creator himself.”
