## 2013-09
@ terrorism-in-the-light-of-islamic-teachings
Q How does the author define terrorism?
* Armed struggle by non-governmental actors :: Yes. Individuals and groups may work only by peaceful means.
- Any use of force by anyone
- Violence that crosses national borders
- War declared without warning
R “My definition of terrorism is that, it is an armed struggle by non-governmental actors… only an established government has the right to use arms.”
@ terrorism-in-the-light-of-islamic-teachings
Q The article compares a surgeon who cuts the wrong part with a person who is not a doctor using a knife. What distinction does this explain?
* A state that uses violence wrongly misuses a right it has; a non-state group has no such right at all :: Well reasoned. Both can be at fault, but in different ways.
- Trained fighters cause less harm than untrained ones
- Doctors, like rulers, are above the law
- Intention matters more than the act
R “There is a distinction between doing something which one has no right to, on the one hand, and misusing a right that one is legally entitled to.”
@ forbidden-tree
Q What does the forbidden tree stand for in the article’s reading of the story of Adam and Eve?
* The wrongs that man, though free, must keep away from :: Correct. Conscience is the guide that tells right from wrong.
- Knowledge that is hidden from man
- The wealth of this world
- The company of other people
R “Man was created as a free creature… The condition was that he should refrain from wrongdoing. The forbidden tree of paradise was a symbol of ‘wrongs’.”
@ artificial-flowers
Q What is “plastic” religiosity, as the article describes it?
* Outward religious form without fear of God and real concern for others :: Yes. It looks grand but has no fragrance.
- Religion practised with modern technology
- Religion learnt from books and not teachers
- Religion that changes from place to place
R “If you examine them closely they have none of the true essence of religion, which is, fear of God and genuine concern for other human beings.”
@ spirit-not-form
Q Three companions vowed to pray all night, fast every day and never marry. What did the Prophet’s reply teach?
* The spirit and quality of worship matter more than quantity; extremes are not his way :: Exactly. He prayed and slept, fasted and ate, and was married.
- More worship always brings more reward
- Vows once made must be kept
- Only prophets may worship at night
R “It is the quality of our worship that is important, far more than the quantity… Extremism in deeds is against all practical wisdom.”
@ true-jihad
Q What is the original and primary meaning of jihad, according to the article?
* Striving hard to obey God – above all against one’s own desires and ego :: Right. The Arabic word for war is qital.
- Armed war against unbelievers
- The conquest of new lands
- Any act done in anger for religion
R “The original meaning of jihad is striving very hard… the actual word for such a war in Arabic is qital, not jihad.”
@ true-jihad
Q When a large force marched on Medina, the Prophet had a trench dug. What does the article see in this?
* A deliberate choice to avoid battle, in keeping with his preference for the peaceful course :: Yes. The same choice appears at Hudaybiya and at Makkah.
- A plan to trap and destroy the enemy
- A sign that the Muslims had no weapons
- A custom borrowed to please the Quraysh
R “The digging of this trench provides an example of opting for a peaceful course instead of a violent course.”
@ controlling-anger
Q What should a person do at the moment of anger, according to the article?
* Stay quiet and calm down before dealing with the matter :: Correct. Rage is like a fire that dies for lack of fuel.
- Say at once what he feels
- Leave the relationship
- Seek out who was at fault
R “When we are angry, we should remain quiet, and try to calm ourselves before returning to dealing with the issue.”
@ prophet-of-peace-or-of-violence
Q Ali named a newborn son Harab (“war”), and the Prophet asked him to change the name to Hasan (“goodness”). What does the article take from this?
* The Prophet’s love of peace was such that he did not like even a name that suggested violence :: Well read. He wished to cultivate a peaceful character.
- The Prophet chose the names of all children in Medina
- Names decide a person’s fate
- Ali had wanted his son to be a soldier
R “His peace-loving nature was so heightened that he did not even like to hear the word Harab which had connotations of violence.”
@ ask-maulana
Q The Prophet stood up as a funeral passed. Told that the dead man was a Jew, what did he reply?
* “Was he not a human being?” :: Yes. He saw every person first as a human being.
- “I did not know.”
- “He was our neighbour.”
- “It is the custom of this city.”
R “The Prophet replied: ‘Was he not a human being?’ This ‘humanitarian’ outlook was typical of the Prophet’s vision of life.”
