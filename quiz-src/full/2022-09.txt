Q What purpose does the editorial say calamities serve for us?
* They wake us from forgetfulness and make us review how we live and what we believe :: Yes. “So that perhaps they may return.”
- They prove that life is governed by chance
- They show that effort is useless
- They are meant only for people of the past
R “The calamities serve the signal purpose of waking us up from our slumber of forgetfulness.”

@ conquers-all

- People fear those who behave well

@ a-practical-solution
Q A Companion advised his grandson: “One who does not bear with small harm from a foolish person will have to bear with great harm.” Why is this so?
* Reacting to the first hurt sets off a chain reaction in which things go from bad to worse :: Right. Better to leave the sleeping ego asleep.
- Foolish people are always powerful
- Small harms are always imaginary
- Patience is a sign of weakness
R “The best way of avoiding great harm from mischief-makers is to put up with initial hurt, for, if one does not, one will set off a chain reaction in which things will go from bad to worse.”

* It accepted the blame for its own ruin and turned its energies to peaceful reconstruction, without complaint or nostalgia :: Well read. It wasted no time bewailing lost opportunities.
- It prepared quietly for another war
- It waited for other nations to rebuild it

R “Japan accepted the blame for its own destruction, and, once having done so, was able earnestly to launch itself on its own economic uplift.”

@ the-goal-of-a-believer
Q Fatima, worn by housework, wished to ask her father the Prophet for a servant. What did he give her instead?
* Words of remembrance to say at bedtime – glorifying, praising and exalting God a hundred times in all :: Correct. “That will do more good than a servant will.”
- A promise of help the next year
- A share of the household of a neighbour
- Permission to leave the work undone

@ negligence-a-moral-deficiency
Q The article begins with bacteria – tiny, yet more dangerous than the tiger. To what in our conduct does it compare them?
* Small, short-lived moments of neglect, which do the greatest damage of all :: Yes. Left unchecked they become an ingrained attitude.
- Great and obvious crimes
- Honest mistakes made once
- Loud quarrels between friends
R “The greatest damage of all is done by those seemingly insignificant and often short-lived moments of neglect… Easy-going negligence can creep into our souls, like bacteria into the body.”
@ lesser-gratitude-greater-gratitude
Q “One who does not show gratitude for lesser things will not express gratitude for greater things.” How does the article explain this?
* Being thankful for a small thing opens the mind’s “file” of gratitude, bringing all its other blessings to the fore :: Well reasoned. A small thanks becomes a fountain.
- Small gifts are worth more than large ones
- Great blessings are too rare to notice
- Gratitude is owed only for small things
R “The file of the brain will open all the items of gratitude under this system of nature… Thus, a small event of gratitude becomes a cause of greater gratitude.”
