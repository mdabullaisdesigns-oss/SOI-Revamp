## 2016-08
@ un-islamic-culture-in-the-name-of-islam
Q The editorial recalls how Islam first spread in Bengal. How was it?
* Through the peaceful work and influence of the Sufis :: Yes. That is why violence done there in religion’s name is called a tragic irony.
- Through the conquest of armies
- Through trade agreements with kings
- Through migration from Arabia
R “Historical accounts show that Islam spread in Bangladesh through the peaceful activities of the Sufis, who had a tremendous influence upon the populations of that region.”
@ finding-common-ground
Q “Let us come to a word common to us” (Quran 3:64). What principle of social life does the article draw from this verse?
* Begin any joint effort from what the parties already share, as the actual situation allows :: Right. It is a matter of practical wisdom.
- Insist first on one’s own position
- Avoid those who think differently
- Wait until all differences have gone
R “The right way to begin any task involving social interaction is to find common ground between the concerned people, and then begin to operate within that frame of reference.”
@ hajj-a-pilgrimage
Q “Hajj is to stand in the plain of Arafat,” said the Prophet. What does the gathering there bring to mind, according to the article?
* The Day of Judgement, when all will stand before God alike :: Yes. All are in the same simple dress, saying the same words.
- A great market or fair
- The armies of ancient kings
- The founding of Makkah
R “The plain of Arafat, in which pilgrims spend one day, presents a picture of the arena of Judgement Day.”
@ hajj-a-pilgrimage
Q What does the article call the mark of a true pilgrim?
* Returning with faith in God strengthened and rekindled :: Correct. Otherwise it is only a tour.
- Having performed Hajj many times
- Having travelled the longest distance
- Bringing home gifts from Makkah
R “To return from Hajj with one’s faith in God strengthened and rekindled—that is the hallmark of a true pilgrim.”
@ tolerance-its-significance-today
Q The article says roses can be had only by tolerating thorns. What does this mean for unity among people?
* Unity is possible only in spite of differences, not by removing them :: Well read. Their total removal is impossible.
- Differences must first be rooted out
- Unity is not worth its cost
- Only like-minded people can unite
R “In this world, unity is achievable only by learning to unite in spite of differences, rather than insisting on unity without differences.”
@ tolerance-its-significance-today
Q According to the article, what shows an excellent character, as against a merely good one?
* Behaving well towards those who differ from you or criticise you :: Yes. Courtesy to the like-minded is easier.
- Agreeing with everyone
- Avoiding all discussion
- Winning every argument
R “If you behave properly with those holding divergent views from you or who criticise you, then you deserve to be credited with having an excellent character.”
@ on-the-occasion-of-differences
Q How should a believer conduct himself towards someone with whom he differs?
* Keep the difference to its own subject, continue the relationship, and stay free of ill-will :: Right. Refusing to greet or meet the person is called improper.
- Break off relations until he changes
- Warn others against him
- Treat him as wrong in everything
R “Mutual relationships should be maintained while continuing serious discussion on contentious issues.”
@ relatives-and-neighbours
Q Which of these did the Prophet say about a believer and his neighbour?
* No one is a believer whose neighbour goes hungry while he has eaten his fill :: Yes. Nor one from whose hand and tongue a neighbour is not safe.
- A believer should live far from his neighbours
- Neighbours are owed help only if they are relatives
- A neighbour’s affairs are no concern of ours
R “Nobody can be a believer, said the Prophet, if his neighbours pass the night hungry, while he has his stomach full.”
@ human-equality
Q If all people are equal, why are there differences of colour and race? What answer does the article give from the Quran?
* They serve for people to know one another, not as marks of rank; the only merit is piety :: Exactly. God looks at the heart, not the body.
- They show which peoples are older
- They are the result of past deeds
- They have no purpose at all
R “Such outward differences are meant to serve as means of identification and were never intended as indicators of superiority… The sole basis of superiority in Islam is piety.”
@ funeral-service
Q A dead body is washed, shrouded and laid to rest with great care. For whom, says the article, is this really meant?
* For the living, who are reminded that the same end awaits them :: Well read. They “experience death before death arrives.”
- For the dead, who feel the honour
- For the family’s reputation
- For the sake of ancient custom
R “It has significance for those who are still alive. Through a dead person an important lesson is conveyed to living people—that they, too, will meet precisely the same fate one day.”
