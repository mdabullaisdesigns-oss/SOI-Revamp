## 2023-07
@ i-was-important
Q A grave inscription read, “I was important, this was the illusion.” What does the article say produces this illusion?
* Using God’s blessings without acknowledging their Giver, until one feels he owns the world he has built :: Yes. Old age and death show it never belonged to him.
- Being praised too much by others
- Having too little education
- Living far from other people
R “He does not acknowledge the Bestower of all these blessings. As a result, he consciously or unconsciously comes to feel that he himself was the owner of the world he had created for himself.”
@ i-was-important
Q A man dying young told his visitors, “Don’t think about me; think about yourself.” What did he want them to realise?
* A healthy body is a gift from God that can be taken back at any time, so it calls for gratitude :: Right. Acknowledging the gift is the test of life.
- That illness can be avoided by care
- That visitors tire the sick
- That youth is the best time of life
R “Be grateful for having a healthy body. You eat, you drink, and you walk on the ground. All these things are God’s blessings.”

@ death-the-inevitable-event
Q How does the article contrast provision in this life with the life after death?
* Here God provides what is needed for the test; the life after depends entirely on one’s own actions :: Right. “You will reap as you have sown.”
- Both depend entirely on effort
- Both are provided without effort
- Neither can be known
R “It is God’s responsibility to provide every one of us with the things that we need to go through this test… Our life after death depends entirely on our actions in this world.”
@ there-is-gain-in-loss
Q A man cheated of his ancestral farmland went to the city and in time owned a factory. What does the article draw from this?
* Loss can be a ladder to progress, if one keeps courage and uses one’s abilities :: Yes. Another opportunity is always at hand.
- Farming is less rewarding than industry
- Relatives should not be trusted
- Villages should be left behind
R “In this world there are so many opportunities that here even in loss there is some aspect of gain… provided he does not lose courage.”
@ superior-morality
Q What is “superior morality,” in the article’s definition?
* Keeping to good conduct in every situation, on one’s own principles, whatever others do :: Exactly. Not returning like for like.
- Treating people as they treat you
- Being kind to those of one’s own group
- Avoiding people who behave badly
R “Superior morality is not based on the reactions of others, but rather on one’s own higher principles and standards.”
@ interview-with-maulana-wahiduddin-khan
Q What does Maulana say about the relation between his reading and his writing?
* Writing should be the result of reading; it should not be the motive for reading :: Well read. His own writing is a by-product of study.
- He reads only in order to write
- He writes first and reads afterwards
- The two are unrelated
R “An author’s work should be a result of their reading. Writing should not be the motivation for reading.”
