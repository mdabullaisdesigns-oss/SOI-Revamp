## 2013-08
@ islam-and-hinduism
Q A friend placed the Gita at the bottom of a pile of scriptures to test Swami Vivekananda. What does the article draw from his reply, “The foundation is really good”?
* A person who refuses to be provoked can turn a negative situation into a positive one :: Yes. The author calls this a forgotten teaching of Islam as well.
- Hinduism is older than other religions
- Sacred books should never be stacked
- Humour is the best reply to an insult
R “If an individual simply refuses to be provoked, he becomes so strong that he can turn any negative situation into a positive one.”
@ islam-and-hinduism
Q What is the author’s own view of the differences between religions?
* They are a blessing, if accepted with a positive mind so that people learn from one another :: Well done. He asks for partnership, not rivalry.
- They are an illusion; all religions say the same
- They are an evil that dialogue must remove
- They make debate the only meeting point
R “Differences in religion are not an evil but a blessing. We require only an acceptance of these differences with a positive mind, so that we can learn from each other and live as partners, rather than as rivals.”
@ in-the-presence-of-god
Q When the angels questioned the creation of Adam, God explained His plan instead of showing displeasure. What rule of conduct does the article take from this?
* Meet an objection by clarifying the facts, not with anger or personal attack :: Exactly. And once the truth is clear, accept it at once.
- Never question those in authority
- Answer objections with silence
- Leave disagreements for time to settle
R “If anyone objects or disagrees with any issue then it should not be responded to negatively or with personal attacks; instead, the issue must be clarified.”
@ ten-principles-of-islam
Q A man asked the Prophet for one piece of “master advice” to manage all the affairs of his life. What was the answer?
* Do not be angry :: Right. It means responding positively even when provoked.
- Pray through the night
- Give away your wealth
- Speak only when asked
R “The Prophet replied, ‘Do not be angry.’ It means that we must not react with anger even when provoked.”
@ living-in-the-past
Q What should a wise person do with bitter memories, according to the article?
* Deliberately forget them, as a traveller pulls out a thorn and walks on :: Yes. Retelling grievances builds a negative personality.
- Record them so they are not repeated
- Share them with trusted friends
- Keep them as a reminder to be careful of people
R “A wise person is one who deliberately forgets the bitter memories of the past, and who does not let his mind become a jungle of bitter thoughts.”
@ the-failing-of-intelligence
Q Why, according to the article, do many intelligent people stay away from religious work?
* They are drawn to immediate gain, and find it hard to set aside the ego :: Correct. The article separates this kind of intelligence from wisdom.
- They find religious teachings too difficult
- They have no time left after their studies
- Religious people do not welcome them
R “It is the attraction of immediate gain… Another trait associated with this kind of intelligence is egoism, which has no place in religion.”
@ scientific-interpretation-of-the-quran
Q What is the article’s position on reading science into the Quran?
* The Quran is a book of guidance, not science, though science can deepen our understanding of some verses :: Well reasoned. We must separate reality from exaggeration.
- Every verse holds a hidden scientific fact
- Science and the Quran deal with unrelated things
- Scientific study weakens faith in the Quran
R “The Quran is not at all a book of science… However, it is true that modern scientific research facilitates our understanding of the Quran.”
@ are-muslims-the-chosen-people
Q On what basis will salvation be given, according to the verses quoted in the article?
* Each individual’s own deeds, not membership of any group :: Yes. The Quran lists Muslims alongside other communities without special status.
- Belonging to the last revealed religion
- Descent from a prophet’s family
- The prayers of others on one’s behalf
R “Salvation will not be awarded generally to any group, but on the basis of personal merit… man shall have only that for which he strives.”
@ the-remembrance-of-god
Q What does dhikr – the remembrance of God – really mean, in the article’s explanation?
* To think about God so that His presence fills heart and mind :: Right. It is spontaneous, “as singing does to a bird.”
- To repeat His name a fixed number of times
- To recite the Quran in a fine voice
- To pray only in a mosque
R “In the Quran, dhikr, does not mean merely the repetition of words. It means to think about God often.”
@ the-word-of-god
Q The Treaty of Hudaybiya looked like a one-sided defeat. Why does the commentary say the Quran called it “a clear victory”?
* Ten years of peace opened the way for the free exchange of ideas, where monotheism prevailed :: Exactly. The contest moved from the battlefield to the field of thought.
- The Muslims were allowed to enter Makkah at once
- The Quraysh paid compensation
- The Muslim army grew stronger for the next battle
R “The end of fighting was in fact synonymous to the opening of the door of dawah… rivalry shifted to the field of intellect.”
