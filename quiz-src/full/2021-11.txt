## 2021-11
@ the-authority-over-religious-affairs
Q Asked at interfaith meetings about Islam’s “hierarchy,” what does the editorial say serves as the source of religious guidance?
* The Quran and the Prophet’s example, which every believer is encouraged to study – there is no central chain of command :: Yes. Scholars explain; they do not rule.
- A council elected every few years
- The ruler of each country
- A single hereditary office
R “The Quran and the practical model of conduct established by the Prophet Muhammad… serve as the fountainhead of religious guidance. There is no centralized authority.”
@ the-authority-over-religious-affairs
Q What does the editorial say the Prophet did whenever he had to choose between two courses?
* He chose the easier :: Right. “God desires ease for you, not hardship.”
- He chose the harder, as a discipline
- He put off the choice
- He let others decide
R “Whenever the Prophet had to choose between two options, he always chose the easier.”
@ living-a-focussed-life
Q The Wright brothers, bicycle makers working quietly, flew first; a famous scientist with funds and publicity did not. What does the article conclude?
* Great success needs steady focus and perseverance; attention before the work is done only hinders :: Well read. They shunned publicity until they had something to show.
- Small workshops are better than laboratories
- Publicity is essential from the start
- Scientists should not attempt inventions
R “Great success demands unwavering focus, dedication, perseverance, and hard work. Public attention, before the task is accomplished, will only act as hindrance.”
@ interfaith-speech
Q Listening to the river at night amid mountains, trees and stars, what “message” did the speaker hear in nature?
* Blessed are those who live together in peace and harmony – unity amid diversity :: Yes. Togetherness is the culture of the universe.
- The strong survive and the weak perish
- Nothing lasts, so nothing matters
- Each must look after himself
R “I felt that it was conveying this universal message: Blessed are those who live together in peace and harmony.”
@ interfaith-speech
Q What, according to the speech, is the one difference between man and the rest of the universe in following this harmony?
* The universe follows it by compulsion; man must follow it by choice – and in this lies his greatness :: Correct. Freedom gives the act its worth.
- Man is exempt from it
- Man follows it only in groups
- The universe follows it less perfectly
R “What the rest of the world is following under compulsion, man has to follow by his own choice. Herein lies the greatness of man.”
@ a-ruthless-teacher
Q A man who had been his country’s richest died its second richest. What does the article say death teaches everyone?
* Each thinks himself “first” until death shows that God alone is the reality – wise is he who learns it beforehand :: Right. Death is the most ruthless of teachers.
- Wealth should be spent quickly
- Rankings are always inaccurate
- Business is too risky to pursue
R “Death brings the message that the only reality is that of God; no other ‘I’ has any real existence. Death is the most ruthless of teachers.”
@ when-appearance-replaces-substance
Q A man showed Caliph Harun al-Rashid an astonishing trick of threading needles in mid-air. What did the Caliph say as he rewarded him?
* “If only you had developed this skill in some useful work” :: Yes. Cleverness is not the same as real work.
- “Teach this to my soldiers”
- “Perform it again tomorrow”
- “You are the wisest man in my court”
R “Harun al-Rashid… said, ‘Woe unto you, if only you had developed this skill in some useful work.’”
@ reading-between-the-lines
Q The article says every injunction has something written “between the lines.” What is unstated in the simple words “Speak the truth”?
* “…even if the truth appears harmful to you” :: Well reasoned. The sacrifice involved is left for us to infer.
- “…when it is convenient”
- “…to those who ask politely”
- “…unless others disagree”
R “When the simple words ‘Speak the truth’ are uttered, what they really mean is: ‘Speak the truth, even if the truth appears harmful to you.’”
@ understand-things-in-correct-perspective
Q “Those who listen to what is said and follow what is best in it” (39:18). What does the article call the first condition of such right thinking?
* To be objective – free of bias and prejudice, able to tell the relevant from the irrelevant :: Exactly. Meaning depends on the listener’s mind.
- To have a good memory
- To listen only to the learned
- To agree with the majority
R “The first condition of right thinking is that you should be objective and be free of all kinds of bias and prejudices.”
@ the-word-of-god
Q The Quran calls the Day of Judgement “the Day of loss and gain.” What does the commentary say people will find then?
* What was counted as gain in this world may prove loss, and what seemed loss may prove real gain :: Well read. The standard there is moral, not material.
- Everyone will be judged by his wealth
- Losses will be repaid in money
- Nothing done here will matter
R “Gaining, which was considered as such will actually turn out to be losing, while what was considered as losing will turn out to be gaining in the real sense.”
