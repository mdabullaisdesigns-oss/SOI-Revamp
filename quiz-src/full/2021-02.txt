## 2021-02
@ a-new-lease-of-life
Q A man died hours after saying how grateful he was for “a new lease of life,” and his family felt “cheated.” How does the editorial respond?
* Death cheats no one; man cheats himself by planning only for this life and not for the one that follows :: Yes. Real new life lies beyond death.
- Doctors should have warned him
- Such things happen only by chance
- He should not have spoken in public
R “Death cheats no one. It is man that cheats himself. The plans that he should be making for the world after death he makes for this life.”
@ clearing-the-roadblock
Q A “Road Closed” sign does not end a journey. What does the article say this teaches about life?
* When the main route is blocked there are always other, indirect ways to the goal for those who look :: Right. Slower perhaps, but one arrives.
- One should wait until the road reopens
- One should turn back home
- One should remove the barrier
R “For every major route which is blocked, there are always several minor roads which are open. It is just a question of having to go about things in a roundabout way.”
@ clearing-the-roadblock
Q If you cannot find a place in the front row, what does the article advise?
* Accept a place at the back for now, until one ahead falls vacant :: Well read. Compromise keeps one moving.
- Leave the gathering
- Protest until a place is made
- Stand in the aisle
R “When you fail to find a place for yourself in the front row, you can always make do with one in the rear until a place up ahead finally falls vacant for you.”
@ patience-vs-impatience
Q Faced with a problem, one may take responsibility or blame others. From what does each course spring, according to the article?
* The first from patience, the second from impatience :: Yes. No positive outlook is possible without patience.
- The first from weakness, the second from strength
- The first from age, the second from youth
- The first from fear, the second from courage
R “Whenever one faces a problem, one may either accept the responsibility for it, or attempt to lay the blame on others. The first course results from patience, the second from impatience.”
@ patience-vs-impatience
Q What “very important aspect” of the patient person does the article single out at the end?
* He takes the trouble to understand the other person’s point of view :: Correct. The impatient consider only their own claims.
- He never changes his mind
- He keeps silent in every dispute
- He avoids making any decision
R “A very important aspect of the patient man is that he will take the trouble to acquaint himself with the standpoint of others.”
@ the-privilege-of-paradise
Q “God is well pleased with them and they are well pleased with Him” (98:8). What does it mean for a servant to be pleased with God?
* To be content with God’s plan of creation and to live by it :: Right. Paradise is a reward, not a purchase.
- To receive whatever he asks for
- To be free of every difficulty
- To be praised by other people
R “God’s servant being pleased with God means he lived his life according to the creation plan of God. The servant was content and pleased with the creation plan of God.”
@ breaking-the-bonds-of-time
Q Asked how he found time for two demanding jobs, what did the man reply?
* “Time is nothing but a function of interest” :: Yes. Keenness shortens the work.
- “I never sleep”
- “I have many assistants”
- “I do each job badly”
R “When asked how he found the time to do so much work, he replied, ‘Time is nothing but a function of interest.’”
@ breaking-the-bonds-of-time
Q One man completed his travel papers in three weeks; another, with two months in hand, missed his chance. What made the difference?
* The first pursued the matter with urgency himself; the second grew complacent and left it to someone else :: Well reasoned. Ample time is no substitute for interest.
- The first had influential friends
- The second lived too far from town
- The first had done it all before
R “The constraints of time can easily be overcome by keenness and enthusiasm, but… where these elements are lacking, even the least time-bound of ventures are likely to fail.”
@ nugget-of-wisdom
Q The Prophet Job lost his wealth and later regained more. What lesson does the article draw from his story?
* No loss in this world is final; one who stays patient and hopeful can gain again :: Exactly. “The Lord gives, and the Lord takes away.”
- Wealth should never be owned
- Misfortune comes only to the careless
- Patience means doing nothing
R “God, being All Merciful, has devised the system of this world in such a way that here no loss or deprivation is final.”
@ the-word-of-god
Q According to the saying of the Prophet quoted, which three things continue to benefit a person after death?
* Continuing charity, knowledge from which others benefit, and righteous children who pray for him :: Well read. Everything else is cut off.
- Property, titles and monuments
- Fame, followers and written praise
- Savings, land and business
R “When a man dies, everything connected with him is cut off except three things: Continuous charity, knowledge from which benefit is derived and virtuous children who pray for him.”
