## 2019-10
@ shift-of-emphasis
Q The editorial sets two sentences side by side: “God is great, so I am not great” and “God is great, so you are not great.” What difference does it see?
* The first breeds humility and self-improvement; the second breeds pride and conflict :: Yes. The command is addressed first to oneself.
- The first is for scholars, the second for everyone
- The two mean exactly the same
- The second is the more accurate
R “‘God is great, therefore I am not great,’ this will engender a feeling of responsibility within you… ‘God is great, therefore, you are not great’; this will beget a psychology of pride.”
@ hollow-success
Q The heir to a great motor company said, “I am not a car. I’m a spiritual soul.” What does the article conclude from such cases?
* Wealth serves the body but cannot fill the soul’s emptiness; that is answered only by turning to God :: Well read. Amid plenty, one can still feel a void.
- The wealthy should give up business
- Fame brings more peace than money
- Success depends on one’s family name
R “No amount of wealth and fortune can supply the needs of the human soul… The only true answer to this quest for a sense of spiritual fulfillment can be found in turning to God.”
@ the-spirit-of-islam
Q A man asked the Prophet what to do in a certain matter, and was told, “Consult your heart about it.” What does the article understand by this?
* Islam accords with human nature – sound common sense points the same way :: Right. A religion of nature.
- Feelings matter more than knowledge
- Each person may make his own religion
- Advice from others is never needed
R “By the heart, the Prophet meant common sense. That is, what one’s common sense tells would likewise be the demand of Islam.”
@ the-spirit-of-islam
Q In the parable, doves complained to a Sufi after his disciple killed one of them. What exactly was their complaint?
* The travellers came dressed as men of God, so the birds felt safe – yet they behaved as hunters :: Yes. A spiritual person is one from whom no harm comes.
- Birds should never be eaten
- The travellers had camped too near the trees
- The disciple had not asked permission
R “All of you came here in the garb of Sufis yet acted as hunters… When we saw you in the guise of Sufis, we thought that we were safe with you.”
@ the-spirit-of-islam
Q According to the Hadith quoted, what is the highest form of worship?
* To pray as if you were seeing God :: Correct. A felt closeness to Him.
- To pray for the longest time
- To pray in the largest gathering
- To pray in a beautiful building
R “The Prophet Muhammad said the highest form of worship is to pray as if you were seeing God.”
@ the-makers-of-history
Q Of those who work in a joint effort, which kind does the article say actually “make history”?
* Those for whom the work itself is the reward, who do not mind receiving less credit than their due :: Well reasoned. They remember duties, not rights.
- Those who insist on full payment first
- Those who receive the most praise
- Those who lead from the front
R “Those alone can perform great tasks and attain to greatness, for whom the accomplishment of the task itself is the real reward.”
@ facing-misfortune
Q “Whatever misfortune befalls you is of your own doing” (42:30). Why does the article find hope in this?
* If our problems lie in our own hands, so do their remedies – we need not wait on others :: Yes. One can re-plan and try again.
- Because misfortune is always deserved
- Because others will soon suffer too
- Because nothing can be changed
R “It offers great hope for us; in that it has placed our problems in our own hands. It has not left us to be dependent on the charity and compassion of others.”
@ belief-in-angels
Q By what comparison does the article explain the role of angels?
* A large factory whose machines need attendants – the universe has unseen ones :: Right. In the factory the hands are visible; here they are not.
- A school with many teachers
- An army with many soldiers
- A market with many traders
R “Countless angels are appointed to ensure the proper functioning of the great factory of the universe.”
@ belief-in-angels
Q In what basic respect do angels differ from human beings, according to the article?
* Angels have no free will and always obey; man can choose between right and wrong :: Exactly. Hence man alone is tested.
- Angels are fewer in number
- Angels live only in the heavens
- Angels cannot communicate
R “They have no free will. They always obey God and never displease Him. Man, on the other hand, has been given free will and can choose between right and wrong.”
@ appearance-and-reality
Q Saad ibn Abi Waqqas directed a battle from a rooftop and was thought a coward, until it was known he was crippled by illness. What lesson does the article draw?
* Do not believe something discreditable about a person without finding out the full facts :: Well read. The whole picture often differs.
- Commanders should always fight in front
- Illness should be kept secret
- Soldiers may disobey a weak leader
R “Whenever something malevolent is said about other, it should not necessarily be believed without further investigation.”
