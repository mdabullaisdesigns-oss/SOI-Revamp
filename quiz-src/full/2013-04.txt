## 2013-04
@ arab-spring
Q Why was the author not hopeful that the Arab Spring would bring democracy?
* Democracy rests on education and institutions built beforehand, and these were lacking :: Yes. He calls for a “pre-democracy revolution.”
- The protesters were too few in number
- Foreign powers would not allow elections
- Arab rulers were too popular to be replaced
R “The base of democracy are institutions. Before establishing democracy we need to establish institutions and provide educational training. Without education, democracy is bound to turn into anarchy.”
@ arab-spring
Q What does the article call “the spirit of democracy”?
* The loser’s willing acceptance of defeat :: Exactly. Without it, elections lead only to a “politics of opposition.”
- Holding elections as often as possible
- Having many political parties
- Removing a ruler by popular protest
R “Acceptance of defeat is the spirit of democracy… the loser is the first to announce the acceptance of his defeat and sends his best wishes to the winner.”
@ god-the-greatest
Q Mathematicians have called God a mathematician and Picasso called Him an artist. What does the article make of this?
* Each observer meets in the universe a mastery far beyond his own, and so recognises God’s greatness :: Well read. God is the greatest mathematician, artist and genius.
- Each person imagines God in his own image, so none can be right
- God can be understood only through mathematics
- Artists understand God better than scientists
R “One who observes the universe is confronted with the awesome feeling that there is a being greater than himself at work in it.”
@ validity-of-a-subjective-argument
Q The article uses the statement “I am thirsty” as an example. What does it show?
* A subjective statement can be valid if it stands rational scrutiny :: Right. Thirst cannot be shown objectively, yet it is a real, natural need.
- Subjective statements are always unreliable
- Only measurable needs are real
- Bodily needs matter more than spiritual ones
R “If it stands rational scrutiny, the subjective statement is as valid as the objective statement.” The article says the same holds for the natural urge to believe in God.
@ global-warming-or-warning
Q A newspaper called Norway’s seed vault “a Noah’s Ark.” With whom does the article say it is better compared, and why?
* Noah’s son, who trusted a mountain to save him from the flood :: Yes. Noah told him no mountain could protect him from what God had sent.
- Noah’s companions, who were saved by obedience
- The people of Mesopotamia, who stored grain
- Noah himself, who planned ahead
R The son said, “I will betake myself for refuge to a mountain.” The article reads global warming as a warning that calls man to mend his ways, not merely to build shelters.
@ prophetic-wisdom
Q When the Quraysh quarrelled over who should set the Black Stone in the Kabah wall, how did the young Muhammad settle it?
* He placed the stone on a sheet that all the leaders carried together, then set it in place himself :: Well done. Everyone took part and no one lost face.
- He drew lots among the clans
- He gave the honour to the eldest man present
- He asked them to postpone the rebuilding
R The article calls it “a fine example of how a controversial matter can be amicably solved, and to everyone’s entire satisfaction, by having everyone participate.”
@ women-in-islam
Q Which maxim does Dr Farida Khanam give for the position of men and women in Islam?
* Equal in respect, but different in role :: Correct. They complement, not duplicate, each other.
- Equal in role, but different in respect
- Alike in every sphere of life
- Men first in public life, women first at home
R She likens them to a cart on two wheels: “Each of the wheels contributes equally to the running of the cart.”
@ women-in-islam
Q The article points to the rite of Sa‘i in Hajj, the running between Safa and Marwah. Why is it significant for the status of women?
* Every pilgrim follows in the footsteps of a woman, Hajira :: Yes. The article sees no “better demonstration of a woman’s greatness.”
- Only women are required to perform it
- It was introduced by the Prophet’s wife
- Women are excused from it out of respect
R “It is of the utmost significance that this was an act performed by a woman… God’s command to all men, literally to follow in her footsteps.”
@ making-instant-history
Q Why, in the author’s view, should we not expect the media to bring about social reform?
* Media is an industry concerned with the present moment, while reform is a mission :: Exactly. He calls this an objective assessment, not a criticism.
- Journalists are poorly educated
- Governments control what is printed
- People no longer read newspapers
R “Social reform is a mission, it cannot be a business.” Those who complain, he says, should “themselves start a mission for that purpose.”
@ religious-responses-to-the-troubles-of-our-age
Q Which two-point formula does the article give for a successful and peaceful life?
* Contentment in material things, idealism in spiritual things :: Right. Ideal justice is not achievable in a world of competition.
- Idealism in material things, contentment in spiritual things
- Demanding rights first, then performing duties
- Protest against injustice, then patience
R “As regards material things, to remain contented, and in spiritual matters, to be idealistic… material ‘status quoism’ and spiritual idealism.”
