## 2017-03
@ the-greatness-of-humility
Q The editorial describes a man who spoke with pride of his grandfather’s rank. What had this pride cost him?
* It kept him from education and useful work, leaving him poor :: Yes. Pride in ancestry stood in his own way.
- It lost him his inheritance in court
- It made his relatives envy him
- It took all his time in ceremonies
R “The psychology which went with being one of a noble lineage had prevented him from either acquiring a good education or engaging himself in some profitable business.”
@ the-greatness-of-humility
Q What does the editorial say produced the humility of the earliest believers?
* A keen awareness of the greatness of God :: Right. “God elevates the humble.”
- Their poverty
- Fear of their rulers
- The customs of their tribe
R “This psychology of humility, which marked their thoughts and deeds, was produced by their keen awareness of the greatness of God.”
@ legalistic-or-psychological-language
Q The article contrasts the language of “do’s and don’ts” with “the language of the psyche.” When is the second needed?
* When speaking to people not yet convinced – it addresses the mind and invites reflection :: Yes. Commands work only on those who already agree.
- When writing laws
- When speaking to children only
- When there is no time to explain
R “The language of do’s and don’ts is effective only with people who already agree with you. In contrast, the ‘language of the psyche’ is effective with and useful for everyone.”
@ the-human-personality
Q One brackish drop shows that the whole vessel of water is undrinkable. What does the article say this teaches about people?
* A single act reveals the whole character – unless the person keeps examining and correcting himself :: Well read. Admitting a fault and asking pardon erases its mark.
- First impressions are always wrong
- People change from day to day
- Small faults do not matter
R “A single human weakness cannot therefore be considered in isolation… It has to be looked upon as being representative of the entire personality.”
@ the-human-personality
Q According to the article, when does a person show his true mettle?
* When he sees his own wrong for what it is and humbly asks forgiveness :: Correct. It is not weakness or loss of face.
- When he never makes a mistake
- When he hides his mistakes well
- When he wins an argument
R “A man displays his true mettle when he sees his own wrong actions for what they are, and humbly asks forgiveness.”
@ success-in-perseverance
Q The astrophysicist Chandrasekhar had his paper ridiculed and rejected, and found no post in India. What did he do?
* He kept working, moved to Chicago, and in time his findings were accepted and honoured :: Yes. He received the Nobel Prize at 73.
- He gave up astronomy for teaching
- He withdrew his theory
- He waited for his critics to die
R “This was not enough to dishearten Chandrashekhar… Finally he gained success with his discoveries being acknowledged and accepted.”
@ success-in-perseverance
Q “Nature requires only a few months to grow a crop of cucumber,” but a hundred years for an oak. What is the point?
* Great success demands long effort; those unwilling to give it must settle for small results :: Right. Soft water wears away hard rock by persistence.
- Fast-growing plants are more useful
- Trees are wiser than vegetables
- Nature is unfair in its gifts
R “If you are not willing to accept the reality of a long struggle, then you shall have to be content with minor success: the necessary price of great success is long-term endeavour.”
@ the-period-of-youth
Q Why does the article advise particular caution in youth?
* Strength is at its height, but experience is not – so the gravest mistakes are often made then :: Yes. Opinions and actions should follow deep reflection.
- The young are watched more closely
- Youth is too short to matter
- The young are naturally lazy
R “In his youth, a person bubbles with energy, but in terms of experience and intellectual development, he still remains in a relatively undeveloped phase.”
@ the-greatest-blessing
Q What does the article name as a husband’s first responsibility towards his wife?
* To respect her and recognise her true worth, regarding her as a gift of God :: Exactly. With compassion, patience and tolerance.
- To provide a large house
- To make all decisions for her
- To keep her from her relatives
R “The first and foremost responsibility of a man towards his wife is to respect and appreciate her true worth.”
@ the-flight-of-the-bee
Q Bees leave the hive in the dark before dawn but return while it is still light. What does the article learn from this?
* Plans should rest on realities and foresight – knowing when light and darkness will come – not on wishful thinking :: Well reasoned. Otherwise we head into darkness unawares.
- One should always rise early
- Travel is safest in groups
- Work should stop at noon
R “Each of our steps should be based on realities and not on wishful thinking or vague suppositions.”
