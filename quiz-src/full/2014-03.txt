## 2014-03
@ the-value-of-a-human-being
Q How does the editorial say “abundant gratitude” (Shukr-e Kaseer) is reached?
* By discovering God’s universal bounties and reflecting on them as if made for oneself :: Yes. This “personalising” brings what he calls thrilling shukr.
- By repeating words of thanks many times
- By comparing oneself with those who have less
- By giving up worldly comforts
R “First you have to discover the universal bounties of God Almighty upon man, and then you have to reflect on what these mean to you personally… Personalise them.”
@ the-value-of-a-human-being
Q Why, according to the editorial, do even gifted and admired people fall into despair?
* They let the one per cent that is negative outweigh the ninety-nine per cent that is positive, and miss their own worth :: Correct. “Change your thinking, and you will discover your true value.”
- They are envied by those around them
- They have too little leisure
- They were not taught religion as children
R “They forget the 99% positive things they still have in their life and let the negative 1% overwhelm them… every person is born as a hero but his pessimistic way of thinking makes him valueless in his own eyes.”
@ desire-for-peace
Q What price, according to the article, must a believer be ready to pay for peace?
* To bear harm without retaliating, so that constructive work can go on :: Right. Positive action in place of reaction.
- To give up his beliefs
- To pay those who trouble him
- To leave his home and community
R “He must suffer all the harm and injustice done to him without making any attempt either to retaliate… The believer has to be willing to pay this price so that his pursuance of constructive ends should proceed unhampered.”
@ a-lesson-from-prophet-adam
Q When commanded to bow before Adam, the angels obeyed and Satan refused. What two ways of acting does the article see in this?
* Getting on with one’s work without confrontation, versus the “politics of opposition” driven by ego :: Yes. The remedy offered is the “politics of patience.”
- Blind obedience versus free thought
- Worship versus worldly work
- Strength versus weakness
R “To focus on one’s work without confronting the authorities is the method of the angels. And confronting the authorities… fired by the ‘politics of opposition’, is the method of Satan.”
@ the-existence-of-god
Q When we look at the stars we see light that left them long ago. How does the article use this fact?
* Much of what we accept is known indirectly, so God too can be known through His signs :: Well reasoned. Things are known by their effects.
- The stars we see no longer exist
- Eyesight is not to be trusted at all
- God lives beyond the farthest stars
R “When all the things of this world are accepted on the basis of indirect evidence, why is it that the presence of God should not be accepted on the same basis?”
@ learning-from-nature
Q Elephants and tigers almost never fight each other. What lesson for human beings does the article draw from animals’ ways of protecting themselves?
* Avoid needless confrontation and keep to one’s own constructive work – this is pragmatism, not cowardice :: Exactly. Animals do it by instinct; we must do it by choice.
- Only the strong survive
- It is best to strike first
- Weak creatures should live in fear
R “They teach us humans that we, too, should avoid unnecessary confrontation with others, and, instead, should focus on our own growth.”
@ blessing-in-disguise
Q What led Louis Braille to invent his system of reading?
* Losing his sight, which made him discover the power of touch in the fingertips :: Yes. A loss opened a new opportunity.
- A teacher’s instructions at school
- A machine in his father’s workshop
- A book he had read before he went blind
R “After losing his vision… he discovered the power of touch, which is inherent in the finger-tip… ‘disabled’ persons are actually ‘differently-abled’!”
@ recognizing-the-truth
Q One friend took a stone on the road for glass; the other, a jeweller, knew it for a diamond. What does the article say this teaches about truth?
* A lover of truth recognises it wherever it is, inside his own circle or outside it :: Right. Knowing only one’s own “necklace” is not enough.
- Valuable things are found by chance
- Only experts can know the truth
- Truth is whatever one’s own tradition holds
R “A real jeweller is one who recognizes a diamond wherever he sees it. In the same way a lover of truth is one who recognizes truth wherever it is.”
@ spiritual-role-of-women
Q After the first revelation the Prophet came home shaken. How did Khadija respond?
* She reassured him that no harm would come to one who spoke the truth and helped the poor and distressed :: Yes. The article gives this as an example of women’s gift for calming and reconciling.
- She took him at once to the leaders of Makkah
- She asked him to tell no one
- She doubted what he had seen
R “She did her best to assure him that no harm would come to him as he always spoke the truth, helped the poor and those in distress, and invariably treated others with respect.”
@ paradise
Q What positive part do our unlimited desires play, according to the article?
* They are a foretaste pointing to Paradise, and can move us to the deeds that earn it :: Well read. This world cannot fulfil them.
- They show what we should own in this life
- They prove that man is never to be satisfied anywhere
- They are a weakness to be rooted out
R “These desires are actually an introduction to paradise in advance… This world is only for man to prove himself worthy for an entry into paradise.”
