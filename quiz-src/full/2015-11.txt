## 2015-11
@ thoughts-on-countering-terrorism
Q The editorial quotes the dictum: “Since wars begin in the minds of men, it is in the minds of men that the defences of peace must be constructed.” What does it conclude?
* Violence done in religion’s name must be answered by correcting the misreadings of scripture behind it :: Yes. Minds are to be re-engineered along peaceful lines.
- More weapons are needed for defence
- Borders should be closed
- Religion should be kept out of schools
R “The terrorist phenomenon is based on misinterpretation of the scriptures. It can be eradicated only by universally publicising the right interpretation of the Islamic texts.”
@ on-the-other-side-of-death
Q The article gathers the last words of Alexander, Napoleon and Harun al-Rasheed. What do they have in common?
* Men of the greatest worldly success ended feeling they had failed and found no peace :: Right. The time to come to one’s senses is before death.
- Each was proud of his conquests to the end
- Each asked for a grand tomb
- Each blamed his generals
R “Most of the ‘successful’ men of this world have breathed their last overwhelmed with the feeling that they have been the greatest of failures.”
@ freedom-of-choice
Q “It is only human to err.” What, according to the article, is the glory of man?
* That he repents after a mistake and turns back – a “U-turn” that raises him :: Yes. God promises to accept repentance.
- That he never admits a mistake
- That he is stronger than the angels
- That he forgets his errors quickly
R “The glory of man is that he repents after he makes a mistake… It is an intellectual process that leads man to higher levels of personality development.”
@ perfection-of-the-creator
Q The universe shows no flaw. What does the article infer about what God has in store for man?
* A perfect Creator will not leave His gifts incomplete – desires given here will find fulfilment in Paradise :: Well reasoned. Hence one should never despair of His mercy.
- Man must perfect the world himself
- Nothing more can be expected than this life
- Only the flawless will be rewarded
R “When God is perfect, God’s bounties cannot be imperfect… these bounties will reach their perfection only in Paradise.”
@ nature-bears-witness
Q Some say the things of nature are parts of God Himself. What does the article give as the Quran’s teaching?
* They are not parts of God; they are created at His command :: Correct. Sun, moon and stars are creations.
- They have always existed without cause
- They are illusions
- They are lesser gods
R “According to the Quran, these things are not part of God, but are created at His command.”
@ a-most-evident-mystery
Q Descartes said, “I think, therefore I exist.” How does the article extend this reasoning?
* The “I” is known by perception, not sight; in the same way God can be known though unseen :: Yes. To deny Him would be like denying one’s own self.
- Whatever we think of must exist
- Thinking is more real than the body
- Only philosophers can know God
R “We shall have to believe in God on the basis of the same logical principle which Descartes employed to know himself.”
@ god-has-created-you-for-a-higher-purpose
Q When a plan of yours is prevented from working out, how does the article suggest you read it?
* As a hint that God intends something higher for you, and a call to rethink :: Right. Moses was not meant to stay in Pharaoh’s palace.
- As proof that you lack ability
- As a punishment for past wrongs
- As bad luck to be waited out
R “If your plans do not get fulfilled, it is an indication from God. It tells you that He has created you for something bigger than what you are presently involved in.”
@ ideology-or-history
Q The article separates a mission’s permanent ideology from its practical strategy. How does it apply this to the early history of Islam?
* The oneness of God is the lasting principle; the battles were responses to the conditions of the time :: Yes. As civil disobedience was to Gandhi’s non-violence.
- The battles are the principle; belief is the strategy
- Both are permanent and unchanging
- Neither has any relevance today
R “The verses of the Quran that talk of tawheed are part of the eternal ideology of Islam, while the verses that talk of qital… related to the practical strategy adopted in response to prevailing conditions.”
@ god-a-source-of-conviction
Q In a storm at sea a little girl went on playing with her dolls. Why was she not afraid?
* Her father was the captain, and she trusted he would not let the ship sink :: Exactly. Such is a believer’s trust in God.
- She did not know there was a storm
- She had been told to stay calm
- She had sailed many times before
R “‘You know, my father is the Captain of this ship. He is not going to let it sink.’… The same is true of a person of faith.”
@ the-word-of-god
Q A scholar said he understood the chapter “The Passage of Time” from an ice-seller’s cry in the market. What was the lesson?
* Life, like ice, is melting away; the time left must be used well :: Well read. Faith, good deeds and counsel to truth and patience.
- Ice should be sold quickly
- Merchants deserve sympathy
- Summer is a time of trial
R “‘O, people! Have mercy on one whose assets are melting away!’… just as the ice melts and reduces, similarly the lifespan given to man is fast passing away.”
