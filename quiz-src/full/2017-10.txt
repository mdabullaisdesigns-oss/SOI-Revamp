## 2017-10
@ the-end-of-history-is-near
Q Scientists now warn, on the basis of data, that the earth’s time is limited. With whose earlier message does the article compare this?
* The prophets, who warned that this world will end and each person will answer for his deeds :: Yes. The Quran calls them Nazeer – those who warn.
- The ancient philosophers of Greece
- The rulers of ancient Egypt
- The poets of Arabia
R “Previous warnings were given on the basis of divine revelation. The scientific community is giving the same warning on the basis of scientific data. The time for introspection has come.”
@ when-the-lion-speaks
Q The lion cannot tell its side of the story. In what way, according to the article, is truth like the lion in this world?
* It does not speak for itself now, but a time will come when reality is made plain to all :: Well read. The present state is temporary.
- It is fierce and frightens people
- It lives far from human society
- It is admired only from a distance
R “In the midst of this verbal uproar, truth remains concealed—truth does not speak of itself… A time is sure to come when the lion speaks.”
@ the-psychology-of-super-achievers
Q In a study of ninety leaders, many spoke of “false starts” but never of “failure.” What does this reveal about them?
* They treat a setback as a hurdle and turn disappointment into greater resolve and a better approach :: Right. They always have another idea to test.
- They never actually met with setbacks
- They hid their mistakes from others
- They depended on unusual talent
R “Disappointment spawns greater resolve, growth or change… super-achievers always feel there are other avenues they can explore.”
@ the-psychology-of-super-achievers
Q According to the article, peak performance is very often less a matter of adding new knowledge than of what?
* Developing capacities one already has but is barely aware of :: Yes. A sense of mission taps these hidden resources.
- Finding influential friends
- Waiting for the right time
- Copying the methods of rivals
R “It is not so much a question of adding to one’s knowledge and skills as of developing what is already there—capacities of which we are sometimes barely aware.”
@ avoiding-the-useless
Q “Those who turn away from everything vain” (23:3). What practical rule does the article draw from this?
* Weigh the likely result before acting, and refrain from what will yield nothing or prove counter-productive :: Correct. Life is too short to waste.
- Avoid all conversation
- Do only what brings quick profit
- Act first and reflect afterwards
R “Before taking any action, the believer must assess its possible result… If there is any possibility that your step will prove to be counter-productive, then refrain from taking it.”
@ garden-of-spirituality
Q How does the article distinguish spirituality from ecstasy?
* Spirituality is a state of keen intellectual awakening, not a trance that blurs the mind :: Right. It belongs with contemplation.
- Spirituality is a deeper kind of trance
- Spirituality is only for those who live in forests
- Spirituality means giving up thinking
R “Spirituality is very different from this, being marked by a high level of acute intellectual awakening, rather than a semi-somnolent state of mind.”
@ garden-of-spirituality
Q What does the honey bee teach the “awakened mind”?
* To draw spiritual nourishment from everything, as the bee takes only nectar and wastes no time on other things :: Well reasoned. The whole world becomes a garden.
- To work in large groups
- To store up food for hard times
- To keep away from human beings
R “For an awakened mind, the whole world is a world of spirituality. It extracts spiritual content from everything.”
@ the-learner-teacher
Q Father Heras held a degree in Spanish history, yet offered to teach Indian history, of which he then knew nothing. What does the article admire in this?
* With a great purpose before him, no task seemed too hard – he studied until he became a historian of repute :: Yes. A mission makes hardship light.
- He wished to avoid hard work
- He was ordered to do so by the principal
- He thought Indian history a small subject
R “It is when one has a great and over-riding mission in life that no task seems too difficult, no hardship too great.”
@ how-to-become-beautiful
Q The article likens insincere behaviour to flowing water. Why?
* Its course is decided by the ground it meets – the insincere person simply yields to each situation’s interest :: Exactly. Sincerity asks one to think and choose by principle.
- Because it is pure and refreshing
- Because it is strong and unstoppable
- Because it always finds the sea
R “This kind of behaviour is like that of flowing water. The contours of the surface of the earth determine its path. The one who is insincere is always ready to accede to the demands of a given situation.”
@ death
Q What, according to the article, gives death its gravity?
* It is not the end of life but the beginning of an eternal life of reward or retribution :: Yes. Only what has worth in the after-life will remain.
- It comes only in old age
- It is painful to those left behind
- It ends all a person’s plans
R “The gravity of the situation lies in death not being the end of life, but rather the beginning of a new, eternal life.”
