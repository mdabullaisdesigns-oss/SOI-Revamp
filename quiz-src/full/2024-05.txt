@ address-of-paradise
Q “Paradise lies under the feet of the mother.” To whom does the article say this saying is really addressed, and with what meaning?
* To the mother – she carries the great responsibility of shaping her child into a good human being :: Yes. Its usual reading, as only a demand on the child, misses this.
- To the child – obedience alone earns Paradise
- To the father – he must provide for the mother
- To teachers – they replace the mother
R “This Hadith actually addresses a mother… it is in the hands of the mother to create a passion for Paradise in a human being.”

Q Why does the article stress the first ten years of a child’s life?
* It is the formative period – the personality shaped then remains through later life :: Right. And children imitate their mothers most.
- Children learn nothing after ten
- Schooling ends at ten
- It is when children are easiest to feed
R “In this formative period, the personality that is formed within someone remains throughout the later age.”
@ a-lesson-in-simplicity
Q What did Maulana learn from finding that a summer fast he had dreaded passed easily?
* A task begun with trust in God is made easy by His help :: Yes. Fasting is a lesson in morale.
- Summer fasts are shorter than winter ones
- Fear is always justified
- Fasting should be avoided by the weak
R “No matter how difficult a task in God’s way may seem, if you start this work on God’s trust, God’s help will be with you.”

* So that he may wonder at the greatness of its Creator and come to know Him :: Well read. Not only to answer questions about creation.
- So that he may travel to other galaxies
- So that he may feel important

@ a-non-verbal-message
Q A student who had failed three times saw a stone worn hollow by clay pots at a well. What did he conclude?
* Constant effort lets even the weak wear down the strong – so he too could succeed by persevering :: Correct. He went on to earn a doctorate.
- Stones at wells should be replaced
- Clay is harder than stone
- He should give up his studies
R “The strong element had given way to the weak just through constant action. ‘Then why should I not succeed in my examinations if I too persevere?’”
@ three-types-of-people
Q How does the article describe the second of its three types – the occasional wrongdoer?
* He can be forgiven if he is a true believer, makes amends by good deeds and turns to God in penitence :: Yes. He is not obstinate in wrong.
- He is beyond all forgiveness
- He ranks highest of all
- He need do nothing at all
R “He too can be forgiven by God and share His blessings, provided he is a true believer… All he has to do then is turn to God in penitence.”
@ the-law-of-patents
Q An inventor’s patent lasts a limited time. Why does the article say the Creator’s right over creation is eternal?
* A human invention draws on nature and other people; the Creator made His creation alone, with no partner :: Right. And He sustains it every moment.
- Human laws do not apply in nature
- No one has challenged it
- Creation is older than any patent
R “Unlike man’s invention, there is no contribution by anyone in the Creator’s invention… The Creator’s monopoly over creation is eternal.”

@ silence
Q Besides increasing our own knowledge, what advantage does the article see in listening first?
* We learn the other person’s view, and so can speak to it persuasively and with consideration :: Exactly. “I have often regretted my speech, never my silence.”
- The other person tires and stops
- We need not speak at all
- We appear wiser than we are

@ water-as-spiritual-nourishment
Q The Prophet told a man not to waste water “even if you are on the banks of a flowing river.” What attitude does the article say leads to such care?
* Gratitude – one who truly values water as God’s gift asks, “If I cannot create water, how can I waste it?” :: Well reasoned. Spirituality is this thankfulness.
- Fear of a fine
- Dislike of water
- A wish to save money
R “A person who is truly grateful to God for the blessings of water will ask himself: ‘If I cannot create water, how can I waste it?’”
