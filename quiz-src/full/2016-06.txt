## 2016-06
@ lesson-from-london
Q What message does the editorial read in the election of a bus driver’s son as Mayor of London?
* Opportunities are now open to all who work for merit by peaceful means; complaint should give way to positive planning :: Yes. “Making the impossible possible.”
- Success depends on family connections
- Politics is the only road to progress
- London is unlike every other city
R “Abandon thinking along the lines of complaint and make plans for actions on a positive basis… Their goal should be to achieve competence and be meritorious.”
@ the-relationship-between-fasting-and-life
Q The Islamic creed begins with “There is no god” before “but God.” What general pattern does the article see in this?
* Half of what is asked of us is to abstain and half to act – and fasting trains the abstaining :: Right. It is the “brake” that makes the journey safe.
- Denial matters more than belief
- Every statement should begin with a negative
- Words matter more than deeds
R “There is a 50:50 ratio in all the actions man has to perform in life i.e. 50% of the actions require abstaining from something and the other 50% acting on something.”
@ the-importance-of-religion-in-peace-building
Q According to the article, which part of religion gives rise to conflict between communities, and which does not?
* Insistence on outward form causes clashes; the inner spirit – truthfulness, honesty, goodwill – does not :: Yes. Spirit is the real part; form calls for tolerance.
- Belief causes conflict; ritual does not
- Charity causes conflict; prayer does not
- Scripture causes conflict; custom does not
R “The spiritual content of religion does not create any problem. The problem is always created by the form of the religion. When the form is emphasized, differences, clash and confrontation ensue.”
@ the-importance-of-religion-in-peace-building
Q The article notes that in business a “customer-friendly” culture is adopted. What does it say is the essence of the social teaching of every religion?
* A human-friendly culture – goodwill towards all, even opponents :: Well read. “Follow one and respect all.”
- A culture of competition
- A culture of separation
- A culture of silence
R “Similarly the essence of the social teachings of every religion is a human-friendly culture.”
@ greed-till-the-grave
Q How does the article distinguish seeking wealth for one’s needs from seeking it for its own sake?
* Needs have a limit and can be satisfied; wealth as an aim has none, so contentment never comes :: Correct. “Greed for more and more distracted you… till you reached the grave.”
- Needs grow with age; greed fades
- Both end in contentment
- Neither can ever be satisfied
R “If it is to meet your needs that you seek wealth, when you have earned enough to meet your needs you will be content. But if you have made acquiring wealth for its own sake your life’s purpose, you will never be content.”
@ the-significance-of-iftar
Q What does the article say an iftar gathering ought to be?
* An occasion for spiritual exchange, turning a private experience into a shared one :: Yes. Not a party like any other.
- A display of the host’s generosity
- A meeting to discuss business
- A competition in cooking
R “Such a party should give us an opportunity for spiritual exchange, turning our individual experience into a social experience.”
@ the-life-after-death
Q On waking each morning the Prophet would say, “All praise and thanks are due to God who gave us life after death.” What does this teach, according to the article?
* Sleeping and waking are daily reminders of death and of rising again to give account :: Right. Ordinary acts become reminders of the Hereafter.
- Sleep is a kind of illness
- Mornings are the best time for prayer
- Dreams foretell the future
R “Going to sleep is like dying and waking up in the morning is like rising from the grave.”
@ the-life-after-death
Q Why, in the article’s account, does true faith lead a person to simplicity?
* Having seen God’s greatness he feels his own smallness, and modesty shows in how he lives :: Yes. “Simplicity is undoubtedly a part of faith.”
- Because he can no longer afford comfort
- Because luxury is forbidden to all
- Because others expect it of him
R “When man has discovered the greatness of God, his own existence in comparison appears quite insignificant… All this inevitably results in his preferring simplicity in everything.”
@ god-oriented-life
Q What does the article say a person gains who has truly found God, when he suffers a loss?
* A conviction that his greatest asset, God, is still with him – so loss no longer breaks him :: Exactly. Adversity brings its own good tidings.
- A promise that losses will be repaid at once
- Freedom from ever losing again
- Indifference to other people
R “Losses no longer matter; he never loses the feeling that his greatest asset, God, is still with him.”
@ fasting-and-feasting
Q The Prophet said the fasting person has two delights. What are they?
* One at breaking the fast, the other at meeting his Lord :: Well read. The first is a foretaste of the second.
- One at dawn, the other at noon
- One in giving, the other in receiving
- One in youth, the other in old age
R “The fasting person has two delights: one at the time of breaking the fast and the other at the time of meeting with his Lord God most High.”
