## 2013-05
@ non-violence-a-way-of-life
Q The editorial likens the world to a rose bush. What does it ask us to do?
* Accept what is favourable and avoid what is unfavourable, since the thorns cannot be removed :: Yes. This is what the author means by non-violence.
- Remove the thorns before picking the flowers
- Leave the flowers alone to avoid being hurt
- Plant a different bush with no thorns
R “No one is powerful enough to rid the world of all that is negative… This phenomenon of nature shows us the way to lead a realistic life; that is, to accept the favourable, while avoiding the unfavourable.”
@ non-violence-a-way-of-life
Q The author says non-confrontation “does not mean cowardice or accepting defeat.” What does he say it is?
* A strategy of buying time, so that energy goes into result-oriented work :: Well done. It is described as wise planning.
- A way of hiding one’s real aims
- A sign that one has no other choice
- A religious ritual
R “It is in actual fact, the same strategy which is known as ‘buying time’. That is to say, avoiding wasting one’s time in futile activities and directing all one’s energy towards result-oriented activities.”
@ the-concept-of-tawheed
Q Anthropologists hold that religion began with many gods and moved towards one. What is the Islamic view given in the article?
* Belief in one God came first, taught by Adam; polytheism crept in later :: Correct. The Prophets came to call people back to the One God.
- Both began together in different lands
- Monotheism began with the Prophet Abraham
- The order in which they arose cannot be known
R “According to Islamic belief, the concept of tawheed has existed since the beginning of human life on this earth… It was in later generations that this religious system began to change.”
@ the-concept-of-tawheed
Q How does tawheed differ from the pantheistic idea that everything is a form of one reality?
* All things are creations of the One God, not parts or partners of Him :: Exactly. God is alone in His being and in His attributes.
- It holds that God lives within natural objects
- It teaches that gifted people share in divinity
- It says the universe has no need of a creator
R “All other things of the universe… are the creations of this One God: they are in no respect constituents of, or partners in the divine godhead.”
@ a-price-for-everything
Q “No Niagara is ever turned into light and power until it is tunnelled.” What law of life is this quotation used to explain?
* Nothing is achieved without paying its price in focus and discipline :: Yes. We live in God’s world and must follow its laws.
- Nature should be left untouched
- Great power is always dangerous
- Success comes to those who wait
R “There is a price to be paid for every end in life: without paying that price, nothing can be achieved… No life ever grows until it is focused, dedicated, disciplined.”
@ accepting-defeat
Q Why does the article call accepting defeat the greatest form of realism?
* It shows a person where he really stands, and a journey can only begin from there :: Right. This is said to be the secret of Abraham Lincoln’s rise.
- It wins the sympathy of one’s opponents
- It saves a person from ever trying again
- It proves that success is a matter of luck
R “Once defeat is accepted, one is immediately in a position to start life’s journey afresh, for such a journey can only commence from where one actually is.”
@ a-sublime-character
Q Sultan Ghiyasuddin came to the Qazi’s court with a sword hidden in his clothes. What was it for?
* To punish the Qazi if he failed to judge justly out of fear of the king :: Well read. The king honoured him for ruling against him.
- To defend himself against the widow’s family
- To frighten the Qazi into a lenient verdict
- To surrender it as a sign of guilt
R The king said: “I brought this sword so that I could punish you in case you deviated from the path of justice… you gave the decision in accordance with justice and did not fear me.”
@ freedom-in-the-creation-plan-of-god
Q Why, according to the article, is this world imperfect and often unfair?
* It is a place of test, which requires free will; perfect justice belongs to the Hereafter :: Yes. “If it was a perfect world, there would be no test.”
- Human beings have spoiled a world that was once perfect
- Fairness is impossible in any world
- God leaves the world to run by chance
R “Strife is the price we have to pay for free will… Perfect justice and fairness towards even the smallest of deeds will only be achieved in the hereafter.”
@ assalamu-alaikum
Q Beyond its literal words, what does the greeting “Assalamu alaikum” promise the person greeted?
* That his life, property and honour are safe in the speaker’s hands :: Correct. The article calls it “a sacred covenant.”
- That the speaker will visit him again
- That the speaker shares his beliefs
- That the speaker asks for his help
R “This term thus signifies, ‘Be sure that your life, property, and honour are safe in my hands.’… It is not a mere ritual repetition of certain set words.”
@ social-harmony
Q Two farmers each had a boundary dispute. What did the second farmer do that the first did not?
* He left the boundary alone and spent twenty years strengthening his own position :: Exactly. He solved the problem “on other fronts.”
- He took the matter to a higher court
- He sold the disputed strip to end the quarrel
- He asked the village elders to arbitrate
R “The one who had wanted to settle the boundary dispute at the boundary was a loser; on the other hand, the one who tried to solve the problem on other fronts not only came to possess the disputed land but the whole field.”
