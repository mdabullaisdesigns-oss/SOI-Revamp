## 2015-08
@ as-you-sow-so-shall-you-reap
Q The article says no one finds complete fulfilment in this world. What does it take this general discontent to be?
* A reminder that our true destination lies beyond this life :: Yes. This world is for sowing; the next for reaping.
- A sign that people have too little
- A fault in human nature
- A result of modern living
R “This general discontentedness is a reminder to everyone that their destination lies somewhere else… that their desired world is not the world before death.”
@ as-you-sow-so-shall-you-reap
Q What does the article say about those who live with severe and lasting suffering?
* Their patient endurance will be accepted by God and is enough to win them Paradise :: Right. They also serve as reminders to others of this world’s passing nature.
- Their suffering shows God’s displeasure
- They are excused from every duty
- They can expect nothing in either world
R “They may not have been able to perform great deeds, but their patient and silent suffering will be accepted by God… will alone suffice to gain entry into Paradise.”
@ unnecessary-complaints
Q “A politician who complains about the media is like a ship’s captain complaining about the sea.” How does the article apply this to everyone?
* Difficulties are part of life, like waves; the sensible course is to learn to ride over them :: Yes. They train and strengthen us.
- People should avoid the sea
- The media should be ignored
- Complaints should be made in private
R “Instead of complaining about the waves that come our way, we should learn the art of successfully riding over or bypassing them.”
@ how-to-overcome-anxiety
Q The article sorts problems into three kinds by time. What does it advise for each?
* Present: solve by planning and action; past: forget it; future: it does not yet exist :: Correct. Anxiety is a way of thinking, not a physical fact.
- Present: wait; past: study it; future: fear it
- Present: forget it; past: solve it; future: plan it
- All three: ask others for help
R “If it is related to the present, you can solve it through wise planning… If it is related to the past… forget it… If the problem concerns the future, then it is not a problem at all, just a baseless fear.”
@ realization-of-god
Q Ali ibn Abi Talib said, “I realized God by having failed in my planning.” What does the article call this kind of experience, and what does it set beside it?
* A negative reminder of a power above us – beside the far more numerous positive reminders such as sunrise and rain :: Well read. Realisation grows like a tree.
- A punishment – beside the rewards of success
- A mistake – beside the lessons of books
- A rare event – beside the rarer miracles
R “This may be called a negative reminder. There are also positive reminders of the greatness of God and the number of positive reminders is far greater.”
@ jewish-muslim-relationship
Q Which examples from the Prophet’s years in Madinah does the article offer as a model for relations between Muslims and Jews?
* Dialogue among the faiths, visits to Jewish leaders and scholars, and standing in respect at a Jewish funeral :: Yes. “Was he not a human being?”
- Separate quarters for each community
- A ban on trade between them
- Avoiding all discussion of religion
R “These traditions about the Prophet of Islam give us a model for Muslim-Jewish relations.” The article adds the Quran’s guidance that “reconciliation is the best.”
@ turn-your-weakness-into-your-strength
Q “Man has been created weak,” says the Quran. How does the article see this weakness turning into strength?
* It teaches modesty and reliance on others, checks pride, and pushes the mind to develop :: Right. It acts like a “speed-breaker.”
- It excuses a person from effort
- It wins the pity of the strong
- It disappears with age
R “Weakness makes one realize: ‘I cannot do anything alone’… In this way, weakness, in terms of its results, becomes one’s strength.”
@ between-pride-and-modesty
Q A man wanted a house where his costly car need not stand beside a cheap one. What way of thinking does the article recommend instead?
* To see a car as a gift from God and from the labour of countless people, beside which one’s own payment is small :: Yes. This breeds modesty in place of pride.
- To buy a still more costly car
- To give up owning a car
- To hide one’s wealth from neighbours
R “He who considers the thing he has purchased from the market as a divine gift as well as a gift from humanity, and that his own contribution to it is nothing, will certainly develop modesty.”
@ dealing-with-differences
Q What does the Quran (49:6) ask believers to do on hearing a report about someone?
* Ascertain the facts fully before forming an opinion :: Exactly. Most complaints grow from hearsay and misunderstanding.
- Believe it if many repeat it
- Pass it on with a warning
- Confront the person at once
R “Ascertain the correctness of the report fully, lest you unwittingly harm others, and then regret what you have done.”
@ the-word-of-god
Q According to the commentary, when is it shown whether a person is truly a believer?
* In extraordinary circumstances, when faith calls for sacrifice :: Well read. There the inner person is revealed.
- In ordinary daily routine
- When others are watching
- In old age
R “The question of whether a man is a true believer is decided on the basis of how he conducts himself under extraordinary rather than ordinary circumstances.”
