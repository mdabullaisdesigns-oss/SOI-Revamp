## 2018-11
@ the-price-of-being-a-taker
Q In Chicago in 1893 a woman judged Swami Vivekananda by his simple dress. What did he tell her?
* That where he came from, character, not the tailor, makes a gentleman :: Yes. Politely said.
- That his clothes were costly at home
- That he would dress differently next time
- That she had no right to comment
R “In your country it is the tailor who makes a man a gentleman, but in the country from which I come, it is character which makes a man a gentleman.”
@ acknowledgment-of-others-basis-of-love
Q The article asks us to think about a car beyond the money paid for it. What does such reflection reveal?
* It is God’s gift in potential, made real by the labour of countless people – so we owe gratitude to God and to humanity :: Well reasoned. This is the root of the “love-all” outlook.
- That cars are a needless luxury
- That the buyer alone deserves credit
- That inventors were mostly lucky
R “Modern vehicles are God-given, in terms of potential. And since many countries and their citizens were involved in turning this natural potential into actual, these vehicles are also a gift from humanity.”

Q What did the Prophet say of one who does not acknowledge people?
* He cannot acknowledge God either :: Right. Gratitude is not divisible.
- He should be left alone
- He will be acknowledged by no one
- He needs more education

@ remember-everything-is-temporary
Q Shackleton’s men survived months stranded on drifting ice. What, according to the book quoted, lay beneath their optimism?
* The confidence that their situation was only temporary :: Yes. No difficulty lasts forever.
- A large store of supplies
- Regular contact with home
- Their leader’s strict discipline
R “Underlying the optimism of the party was the confidence that their situation was only temporary.”
@ the-period-of-old-age
Q What does the article call the positive gain of old age?
* The discovery of one’s helplessness, which leads to humility and to God – with wisdom to share :: Well read. Hence “the golden age.”
- Freedom from all duties
- The respect that age commands
- More time for rest
R “Old age has a positive aspect to it—a gain that only results with agedness—and that is the discovery of one’s helplessness.”
@ realization-of-god
Q By what familiar example does the article explain “conviction without observation”?
* Everyone is certain who his mother is, though no one witnessed his own birth :: Right. Certainty need not rest on sight.
- We trust maps of places we have not visited
- We believe in the wind though it is unseen
- We remember dreams we cannot prove
R “Everyone has complete conviction in one’s mother despite not having witnessed or observed one’s birth from her womb.”
@ a-believers-attitude-towards-his-neighbour
Q “He is not a believer who eats to his fill while his neighbour goes without food.” What does the article say this teaching shows?
* A believer cares about others’ hunger as about his own, and gives practical help :: Yes. Goodwill alone is not enough.
- Neighbours should eat together daily
- One should eat very little
- Charity is owed only to relatives
R “This shows that a Muslim is one who is concerned with others’ hunger and thirst as he is with his own.”
@ a-believers-attitude-towards-his-neighbour
Q According to the article, a faithful servant of God lives in one of two ways. Which?
* As a giver, or at least as a no-problem person :: Correct. “There is no third option.”
- As a leader or a follower
- As a teacher or a student
- As a host or a guest
R “For a man to be a really faithful servant of God, he must live either as a giver or as a no-problem person. There is no third option.”
@ the-material-and-the-immaterial
Q Why, in the article’s analysis, do some people prefer money to relationships?
* Money asks nothing in return, whereas people require give and take :: Well reasoned. But the choice is unnatural and leaves one divided.
- Money lasts longer than friendship
- People are harder to find than money
- Money brings more respect
R “With money, you can make contact on unilateral basis, but with human beings, you have to make contact on a bilateral basis, that is, on a give and take basis.”
@ the-straight-path
Q A train reaches its destination only while it stays on its rails. What does the article say takes a person “off the rails”?
* Becoming absorbed in something other than God – selfish desire, hatred, or devotion to a person or party :: Exactly. Any such detour misses the goal.
- Travelling too slowly
- Asking others for directions
- Stopping to rest on the way
R “One tends to stray from the straight path whenever one is obsessed with something, person or idea, other than God.”
