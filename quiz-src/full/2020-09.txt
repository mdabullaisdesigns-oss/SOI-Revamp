## 2020-09
@ virtual-meeting
Q A Hadith promises God’s love to those who meet one another for His sake. What does the editorial say of meeting online when people cannot gather in person?
* It too counts as gathering for God’s sake, if it serves the same purpose :: Yes. A modern substitute for companionship.
- It has no value compared with meeting in person
- It should be avoided where possible
- It counts only for the young
R “A virtual meeting too—if it is for a mission—is to love for the sake of God, and to gather for the sake of God.”

- “Father, let us ask others first”
- “Father, wait until I am older”
- “Father, such a dream cannot be true”
R “O my father, do as you are commanded; and God willing, you will find me steadfast.”
@ the-letter-and-spirit-of-eid-al-adha
Q What spirit does the article say the festival of sacrifice asks believers to renew?
* Readiness to give up what is dear for God’s sake: “my prayers, my sacrifice, my life and my death all belong to God” :: Well read. The form recalls the meaning.
- Pride in the size of one’s offering
- A wish to outdo one’s neighbours
- The pleasure of a public holiday
R “Believers reaffirm their belief in God and pledge themselves to parting with their precious belongings, if there is a need for it.”
@ the-purpose-of-religious-rituals
Q What does the article call the ideal form of religion?
* Ritual and inner spirit going hand in hand, each with its own value :: Yes. Form gives the spirit expression and identity.
- Spirit alone, with no outward form
- Ritual alone, performed exactly
- Neither, but philosophy
R “One is that in which spirit and rituals (or form) both have their distinct values. That is the ideal version of religion.”
@ coping-with-death
Q A reviewer found two scientific books on bereavement “useful but not elevating.” What does the article say can truly console the bereaved?
* Knowing that death begins the real life, and that everything belongs to God :: Correct. “What is there to lose and lament?”
- More detailed medical knowledge
- Keeping busy with work
- Avoiding all thought of the dead
R “It is only by realizing that death—far from being the end of life—is the beginning of real life that we can begin to cope with the distress and suffering that death leaves in its wake.”
@ blessing-in-disguise
Q The Prophet used to tell a sick person that the illness would be a means of purification. How does the article explain this?
* Illness reveals one’s weakness, removes pride, and turns the heart to God in prayer :: Well reasoned. Unbroken ease can make one insensitive.
- Illness cleanses the body of impurities
- Illness is a reward in itself
- Illness cancels all duties
R “When a sensitive person is afflicted with disease or loss, he discovers his weakness… the disease draws him away from other things and brings him closer to God.”
@ blessing-in-disguise
Q To what does the article compare the difficulties of this world, and what are they for?
* Speed breakers – so that man does not take this world for the real, final one :: Right. They turn his thinking towards the Hereafter.
- Walls – to stop him going anywhere
- Storms – to test his house
- Debts – to be paid off one by one
R “These problems act as speed breakers for human beings. These problems exist so that man does not consider the present world as the real world.”
@ is-life-worth-living
Q Even the most brilliant people leave the world with much undone. When, according to the article, does life begin to make sense?
* When one accepts that there is a life after death :: Yes. Then everything falls into place.
- When one achieves fame
- When one lives to a great age
- When one stops asking such questions
R “It is only after we accept that there is a life after death, an eternal existence, that we see some meaning in life.”
@ intrinsic-reality
Q Some say modern civilization has made religion out of date. How does the article answer?
* New comforts and machines change the outward forms of life, but religion deals with its lasting values, which they do not touch :: Exactly. A faster vehicle does not alter right and wrong.
- Religion must reject modern inventions
- Modern life has no values at all
- Religion matters only in villages
R “The relevance of religion is its concern with fundamental values rather than with social manifestations. Social phenomena undergo constant changes, whereas the values of life are immutable.”
@ religious-freedom-in-islam
Q A widow of another faith asked Caliph Umar for help; he offered her his faith and she declined. What did he do?
* Gave her more than she had asked, and prayed, “O God, bear witness that I have not exercised any coercion” :: Well read. “There shall be no compulsion in religion.”
- Withdrew his offer of help
- Asked her to think again and return
- Sent her to another official
R “Umar then gave her more than she had asked for… ‘O God, bear witness that I have not exercised any coercion on this lady.’”
