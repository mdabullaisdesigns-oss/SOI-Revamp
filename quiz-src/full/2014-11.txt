## 2014-11
@ now-or-never
Q A young man promised to give up a harmful habit, saying “I will do it.” What did the author tell him to say instead?
* “I have done it” :: Yes. The principle is “now or never.”
- “I shall try my best”
- “I need more time”
- “I will ask for help”
R “‘I will do it’ is a kind of luxury which we cannot afford. We have to take decisions at once.”
@ now-or-never
Q Fear of a cobra makes anyone act instantly. What does the editorial say a wise person does with this fact?
* Learns to call up the same alertness of mind without needing fear :: Right. That is what sets man apart from animals.
- Avoids every dangerous place
- Uses fear to motivate others
- Keeps himself always afraid
R “What differentiates a human being from an animal is that man should be able to activate the potentials in his mind without fear.”
@ working-together
Q The article compares a member of society to the driver of a steam engine standing by the fire. What is the comparison?
* He must control both his anger at others and his wish to run away, so that society keeps working :: Yes. Without this no “train” could run.
- He must keep others at a safe distance
- He must work harder than everyone else
- He must feed the quarrels that drive progress
R “Just as the engine driver controls both the fire which drives the engine and his own desire to escape from it, so must the individual in society tame both his own fury and a desire to simply run away.”
@ working-together
Q What keeps a family together through daily disagreements, and what must do the same work in a society?
* In a family, bonds of love; in society, a conscious, reasoned decision to agree to disagree :: Well read. One is instinctive, the other a deliberate effort.
- In a family, the father’s authority; in society, the law
- In a family, shared money; in society, shared trade
- In both, the absence of any differences
R “While it is an emotional bond that keeps families from disintegrating, it is a rational effort which cements society.”
@ two-types-of-psyche
Q The article contrasts the outlook “Islam is true” with “I am true.” What does each produce?
* The first, fear of God; the second, a false sense of superiority :: Correct. The article sees in this the difference between rise and decline.
- The first, pride; the second, humility
- The first, doubt; the second, certainty
- Both produce the same character
R “The first conception engenders the fear of God. The second promotes a false sense of superiority.”
@ call-of-the-judgement-day
Q How does a God-conscious person see each evening and each morning, according to the article?
* Evening as a likeness of the first call of the Day of Judgement, morning as the rising before God :: Yes. Going to bed is like lying in the grave; waking, like resurrection.
- Evening as rest earned, morning as work owed
- Evening as loss, morning as gain
- Both as matters of no meaning
R “Every evening therefore is an illustration of the scenario after the first call of the Day of Judgement. Every morning is an illustration of the scenario of the second call.”
@ happiness-other-name-for-contentment
Q The musk deer runs about searching for a scent that comes from itself. What does the article say this shows about happiness?
* It is an inner state of mind, not something to be found outside :: Right. Contentment with need, not pursuit of greed.
- It can be found only by travelling
- It belongs to animals more than to people
- It depends on what others think of us
R “Man searches for happiness outside of him, although happiness is inside him… Happiness is an internal phenomenon, it is not an external achievement.”
@ living-in-the-great-blessings-of-god
Q After naming animals for riding, the Quran adds: “He creates what you do not know.” How does the article understand these words?
* As pointing ahead to means still to come, such as modern industrial civilisation :: Yes. It was present in nature in potential form.
- As referring to creatures of the deep sea
- As a reminder that knowledge is forbidden
- As describing the angels
R “This blessing is the industrial civilization which was present in nature in the potential sense at the time of the revelation of the Quran.”
@ personality-development
Q When a negative thought arises, why does the article insist on dealing with it at once?
* Left alone, it sinks beyond conscious control and becomes part of one’s personality :: Well reasoned. Anger forgiven at once does not turn into revenge.
- It will be forgotten by morning anyway
- Others will notice it
- It grows weaker with time, so haste wastes effort
R “If there is a delay in this process… it will become such an integral part of the personality and even if man wants to detach it from his personality, he will not be able to do so.”
@ ashes-bear-witness
Q A crime in Delhi was uncovered months later from the ashes of the dead man. What larger truth does the article draw?
* Every deed leaves a record that cannot be wiped away, for the world is of God’s making :: Exactly. We are free to act, not to erase.
- The police always find the guilty
- Science has made crime impossible
- Only great crimes are ever recorded
R “As soon as a deed is done, it gets affixed to the ‘cosmic record’ and no one can ever rub it off thereafter.”
