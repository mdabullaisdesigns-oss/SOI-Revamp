## 2013-01
@ from-the-editors-desk
Q The Chief Editor says formal education “can give you a job, but it cannot teach you the art of living.” What, in his view, fills this gap?
* Informal education that instils spiritual and social values, such as a magazine can offer :: Yes. That is the stated purpose of the magazine.
- Longer years at university
- Professional training on the job
- Travel to other countries
R The editorial calls the magazine “an institution for informal education” whose purpose is “to promote spiritual values in society.”
@ the-only-solution-to-corruption
Q Why does the article say that four anti-corruption movements in India did not reach their goal?
* They made demands of the system, whereas corruption begins in the individual mind :: Exactly. The article asks for minds to be re-engineered, not systems.
- They did not draw large enough crowds
- They had no well-known leaders
- The laws they asked for were never written
R “It is not the system that is responsible for corruption; it is the human mind… Any movement that intends to achieve its goal must target individual minds, rather than the system.”
@ the-only-solution-to-corruption
Q “To shorten a line, you only need to draw a longer line next to it.” How does the article apply this image to corruption?
* Give people a goal higher than money – wisdom and spiritual growth – and money loses its hold :: Well done. Money becomes a need, not a goal.
- Punish small offences so that large ones look worse
- Pay officials more than they could take in bribes
- Shorten the time courts take to decide cases
R “We have to replace money as a goal with wisdom as a goal… to eradicate corruption, we have to give individuals a goal greater than money.”
@ the-fault-of-man
Q The article compares religion to nuclear energy. What is the point of the comparison?
* Both are sources of life as given by nature and God; the harm comes from human misuse :: Right. The fault lies with man, not with the gift.
- Both are too dangerous for ordinary people
- Both were discovered by modern science
- Both will one day be exhausted
R “It is wrong to blame religion for the evils which men have perpetrated in its name. This is the fault of man, not the fault of the divine religion which he corrupts.”
@ no-end-to-opportunities
Q What lesson does the article draw from the life of Abd al-Rahman, the Umayyad prince who fled Damascus and later ruled al-Andalus?
* When one opportunity ends another arises, but it must be seized with courage and perseverance :: Yes. He had nothing but “his own wits and perseverance.”
- Noble birth always brings a person back to power
- It is wiser to stay hidden when enemies are strong
- Success depends on having a large army
R “There is no end to the opportunities in this world. When one opportunity ceases to exist, another opportunity always arises elsewhere… It takes a person of courage to grasp opportunities.”
@ respecting-all-religions
Q Which formula does the article offer for harmony among people of different religions?
* Follow one and respect all :: Correct. Mutual respect is practical even where agreement is not.
- Accept every religion as equally true :: The article says this goes against each believer’s inner conviction and is not practical.
- Keep religion out of human life :: The article says the urge to believe is part of human nature and cannot be removed.
- Merge the religions into one shared creed
R “Mutual recognition of religion might not be practical, but mutual respect certainly is… a formula for maintaining religious harmony is—follow one and respect all.”
@ is-there-a-heaven
Q On what ground does the article call Stephen Hawking’s statement “There is no heaven” unscientific?
* Science can speak only of what is observable, and that is about 4% of the universe :: Exactly. Nothing can be asserted by science about the rest.
- Hawking was not trained in religion
- Other physicists have observed heaven
- The statement was made in a newspaper, not a journal
R The article says the only valid statement would have been: “according to known physical laws, in the 4% of the Universe subject to scientific study, there is probably no heaven.”
@ through-hardship-to-the-stars
Q Writing about Japan’s recovery after the 2011 earthquake and tsunami, what does the article say about adversity?
* It is not good in itself, but with the will to act it can bring out hidden potential :: Well read. The will is the deciding thing.
- It is always a blessing and should be sought
- It ruins nations that lack natural resources
- It matters less than complacency only in rich countries
R “By no means does this imply that adversity is itself beneficial. It is not. But it can be the spark which ignites the fuel of our souls… But for all this, there has to be the will to do so.”
@ lessons-from-nature
Q The article notes that a mountain stream does not try to remove the stones in its path. What should we learn from it?
* To go around obstacles and keep moving instead of colliding with them :: Yes. The stream “carves its way ahead around and alongside the stones.”
- To wait patiently until obstacles are removed by others
- To gather strength until obstacles can be broken
- To change our destination when the path is blocked
R The stream is “as if telling us not to collide with obstacles, but to continue on our way by keeping away from them.”
@ lessons-from-nature
Q According to the article, how does man’s following of the “universal model” differ from that of the stars, plants and streams?
* Nature follows it by compulsion; man has to adopt it consciously, of his own free will :: Right. That is the test of human freedom.
- Man follows it only in worship, nature at all times
- Nature gives and takes, man is asked only to give
- Man may follow it in part, nature must follow it fully
R “In the rest of the Universe, the model has been established compulsively, under the laws of nature, while we have to establish this model in our lives consciously, of our own free will.”
