## 2020-04
@ ramadan-a-month-of-rigorous-training
Q Patience and God-consciousness are needed all year. Why, then, does the article call Ramadan a “crash course”?
* For one month a strict discipline trains these qualities so that they stay part of one’s character the rest of the year :: Yes. Practice in refraining even from what one wants.
- Because the month is shorter than others
- Because its lessons are forgotten quickly
- Because only beginners need it
R “For one month, certain habits are enforced as part of a certain discipline, so that for the rest of the year, the person will have no difficulty in exercising the same control over all of their actions.”
@ blessings-of-god
Q A researcher is quoted: “The human brain is very resilient. Give it a chance and it will make every effort to repair itself.” How does the article say one gives it that chance?
* By keeping patient and calm after a setback, not disturbing the natural process of recovery :: Well read. Resilience is a law of nature.
- By working harder at once
- By talking the matter over repeatedly
- By changing one’s surroundings
R “When you suffer some kind of damage either internally or externally, keep your patience. Be empty-minded for a while.”
@ blessings-of-god
Q The body’s many organs work together without our effort. What does the article ask us to draw from this?
* Gratitude to the Creator, which keeps one from complaint and makes one a positive thinker :: Right. “To breathe is a luxury.”
- Pride in the human body
- Worry about possible illness
- Indifference, since it happens by itself
R “This realization makes us conscious of great blessings of the Creator… Hence, we should cultivate a feeling of gratitude for the Creator.”
@ idleness

* That the man lost his worth in Umar’s eyes :: Yes. Idleness wastes one’s best gifts.
- That the man was fortunate to be free
- That he should be given a pension
- That work is not for everyone
R “On learning that he does not work, he appears to me of no value.”
@ idleness
Q What does the article advise for someone who has no financial need to work?
* To occupy himself with higher aims and worthy causes rather than slip into idleness :: Well reasoned. A life without commitment decays.
- To enjoy his leisure fully
- To give away all he owns
- To travel constantly
R “If one is financially independent, one should turn one’s attention to higher things, pursue noble ends, serve worthy causes and keep oneself fruitfully occupied every day.”
@ guarantee-of-gods-succour
Q “If you persevere and fear God, their designs will never harm you in the least” (3:120). How does the article explain this in natural terms?
* Patience and humility bring an opponent back to a normal state, where his own conscience restrains him :: Correct. It is against human nature to oppose what is plainly right.
- Opponents grow tired with time
- Others will come to one’s defence
- Hardship makes one physically stronger
R “Adherence to the path of patience… and piety… form the best formula to bring an antagonist back to a reasonable frame of mind.”

@ ways-of-self-correction
Q A new believer left after learning only two verses: “Whoever has done the smallest particle of good will see it; whoever has done the smallest particle of evil will see it.” Why did he feel this was enough?
* They told him every deed is accountable to God, which kept him constantly on guard :: Well read. A self-correcting mechanism within.
- They were the shortest verses to memorise
- He was in a hurry to return home
- He had been told to learn nothing more
R “These verses tell us that man is accountable to God and every deed of man will be evaluated by God. Now I always keep this in mind.”
