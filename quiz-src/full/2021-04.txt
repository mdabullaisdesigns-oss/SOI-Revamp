@ paying-homage-to-my-teacher
Q Professor Majida Asad overcame many obstacles to her education and career. What does she advise when facing disagreement?
* Speak in a soft tone and present your case with full conviction – truthfulness wins over an opponent :: Yes. Gentleness with firmness.
- Raise your voice so as to be heard
- Avoid stating your view at all
- Leave the discussion at once
R “To tackle any situation of disagreement and confrontation, she advises one to speak in a soft tone, present one’s arguments with complete conviction.”
@ from-pride-to-arrogance
Q Why does the article say pride is never a harmless feeling of self-respect?
* Pride is comparative – it carries the thought “I am better than others,” and grows into arrogance :: Well reasoned. The very words attributed to Satan.
- Because it is tiring to maintain
- Because others quickly notice it
- Because it makes people work too hard
R “Being ‘proud’ is a comparative adjective. Pride always arises in comparison to someone else… there is a feeling that ‘I am better than others.’”
@ from-pride-to-arrogance
Q What does the article say is the only difference between pride and arrogance?
* The spelling – in reality they are the same :: Right. One becomes the other.
- Pride is private; arrogance is public
- Pride is brief; arrogance is lasting
- Pride is earned; arrogance is not
R “The only difference between pride and arrogance is that of spelling. In reality, there is no difference between the two.”

@ ideal-individual-ideal-society
Q Why does the article hold that an ideal person is possible in this world but an ideal society is not?
* A person can reform himself by his own will, but everyone is free, and some will always misuse that freedom :: Correct. So the individual, not society, is the target.
- Societies are too large to count
- Ideal people refuse to live together
- Laws are always badly written
R “Freedom means having more than one option. Because of this option, it will always be the case that some people will use freedom properly and some people will abuse it.”

* A life led by desire – anger, envy, greed – and a life led by principle – honesty, humility, forgiveness :: Right. Only the second is fully human.
- A life of work and a life of leisure
- A life in the city and a life in the village
- A life of learning and a life of trade
R “The life of every human being lies between two contradictory requirements: desire and principles.”

@ anger-management
Q What effect of anger, reported in a study the article cites, makes it unwise to act while angry?
* It makes us more impulsive and leads us to underestimate the chance of a bad outcome :: Exactly. Hence the advice to stay quiet until calm.
- It makes us forget what happened
- It makes us too cautious to act
- It improves our judgement of risk
R “Feeling anger can alter the way we view risks. Studies have shown that it can make us more impulsive and make us underestimate the chances of bad outcomes.”
@ the-word-of-god
Q “If God were to grant abundant provision to all His creatures, they would act insolently on earth” (42:27). What does the commentary draw from this?
* Provision is given in due measure, with knowledge of what each person can bear without becoming arrogant :: Well read. Excess can corrupt.
- Wealth is always a punishment
- The poor are dearer to God than the rich
- Effort has no bearing on provision
R “If people are given greater abundance than they can deal with equanimity, they will become obstreperous and arrogant.”
