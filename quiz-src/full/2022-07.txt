## 2022-07
@ lessons-from-the-volcanic-eruptions
Q Before volcanoes, floods and earthquakes, man and all his developments are helpless. What lesson does the article draw from this?
* Man is not the master of this world and should live in it with modesty and humility :: Yes. Such experiences are there to be learned from.
- Man should move to safer planets
- Nature is man’s enemy
- Science has failed mankind
R “These natural experiences teach that man is not the master of this world. In this world man must lead a life of modesty and humility.”
@ pages-from-maulanas-diary
Q The Maulana saw many talented, highly educated people fail in practical life. What does he say held them back?
* An exaggerated estimate of themselves, which made them look down on the tasks they were given :: Right. He calls each one “a case of overestimation.”
- A lack of money at the start
- Poor health in youth
- Jealous colleagues
R “The exaggerated assessment of their ability and talent did not allow them to do anything worthwhile. They could not adapt to the tasks they were assigned, because they considered them of lesser status.”
@ pages-from-maulanas-diary
Q A student lost two years to a spinal injury and thought his life was over. Which insight gave him the courage to return and come first?
* Even if a man’s past and present are destroyed, his future is still intact :: Well read. And: don’t use an excuse, even a good one.
- Time heals every wound
- Fortune favours the brave
- Others have suffered more
R “Don’t use an excuse, even if it is a good one; even if a man’s past and present are destroyed, his future is still intact.”
@ pages-from-maulanas-diary
Q A magazine cover described a fallen president as “A Man without a Country.” Why did the Maulana feel this was every man’s story?
* Death separates each person from whatever he has become lord of, and he goes on to the Hereafter with nothing :: Well reasoned. Every “country” is held only for a time.
- Everyone travels abroad at some time
- Borders change in every age
- Rulers are always forgotten
R “In this world, every man has a country of which he has become the lord. However, death separates every man from his country.”
@ history-speaks
Q King Roger II of Sicily invited the geographer Al-Idrisi to his court to make a map of the world. What does the article see in this as the way to esteem?
* Being useful to the world – having something of value that others need :: Yes. People seek out those who benefit them.
- Having powerful friends
- Travelling widely
- Demanding one’s rights loudly
R “They had something that others did not have, so people flocked to their sides.”
@ the-relationship-between-man-and-god
Q “I am near. I respond to the call of one who calls” (2:186). How does the article describe the relationship between man and God?
* Reciprocal – man offers realization, piety and thankfulness, and God bestows guidance in return :: Correct. “Let them, then, respond to Me.”
- One-sided – man receives and owes nothing
- Distant – reached only through others
- Uncertain – no one can know
R “This shows that the relationship between man and God is reciprocal: man offers God what he has, then God bestows His favours in return.”
@ character-builds-the-nation
Q To what does the article compare a dependable, tempered character in the building of a nation?
* Kiln-fired bricks, which can withstand storm and flood where unfired bricks collapse :: Right. Character is the building material.
- The paint on a finished house
- The roof that keeps out the rain
- The garden around a house
R “A character so tempered that it can be depended upon through thick and thin like the kiln-fired brick is what in the long run builds a nation.”
@ ethics-and-technology
Q When newly built structures fail, more technical training is often proposed. Why does the article say this will not cure the problem?
* The fault is moral, not technical – and a moral fault cannot be removed by technical improvement :: Exactly. It calls for the reform of individuals.
- There are already too many engineers
- Training takes too long
- Materials are the only problem
R “Corruption is a psychological evil, while lack of skill is a technical shortcoming. A psychological evil cannot be removed by technical improvement.”
@ seeking-the-right-alternatives
Q A passenger misses his train and a porter tells him to wait for the next. What is “the lesson that the platform teaches”?
* A missed opportunity is not the end; there is always a “next train” for one who waits patiently and stays alert :: Yes. A God-given second opportunity.
- Always arrive two hours early
- Never trust a watch
- Travel by another means
R “When we miss a train, there is always the comforting knowledge that there is that next train coming along. That is the lesson that the platform teaches.”
@ giving-as-god-wills
Q How does the article define real charity?
* That which is given for God’s sake alone, not for fame, self-esteem or worldly reward :: Well read. Giving as thanks for what one has been given.
- That which is given in the largest amount
- That which is given in public
- That which is given to relatives only
R “Real charity is that which is given for God’s sake alone, not for fame, self-esteem or worldly reward.”
