## 2019-02
@ know-your-rights-and-perform-your-duties
Q What “two-point formula” for a successful life does the editorial offer?
* Be content in material things and idealistic in spiritual matters :: Yes. This gives peace of mind and ends the habit of complaint.
- Be ambitious in wealth and modest in worship
- Demand your rights first and do your duties later
- Avoid both material and spiritual effort
R “As regards material things, remain contented, and in spiritual matters, be idealistic.”
@ spirit-of-islam
Q What does the article call the first principle for understanding Islam?
* To distinguish Islam from Muslims – judging Islam by its text, and Muslims by its teachings, not the reverse :: Right. The community is not the reference point.
- To study the history of Muslim empires
- To learn from whichever group is largest
- To compare it with other religions first
R “You have to judge Islam based on its original text. And, you have to judge Muslims in the light of Islamic teachings and not vice versa.”
@ the-work-of-nation-building
Q The article likens nation-building to planting an orchard. What is the point of the comparison?
* A nation grows only through long, patient nurturing of individuals, one by one – not through meetings and slogans :: Well read. “Build the individual and you build the nation.”
- A nation needs good soil and rain
- A nation should export fruit
- A nation grows best when left alone
R “The work of planting an orchard begins by obtaining seedlings and providing every single one with such favourable conditions as will enable it to develop its potential.”
@ the-emotion-of-anger
Q A man asked the Prophet for one piece of advice by which to manage all the affairs of his life. What was it?
* “Do not be angry” :: Yes. Respond positively even when provoked.
- “Do not travel alone”
- “Do not borrow money”
- “Do not speak in gatherings”
R “The Prophet replied, ‘Do not be angry.’ It means, don’t get angry even when provoked.”
@ the-emotion-of-anger
Q The article compares silence in a moment of anger to “shift time” when driving. What does silence do?
* Like changing gear, it turns the mind from a rash reaction towards a considered, positive response :: Well reasoned. “When you are angry, keep silence.”
- It hides the anger from others
- It makes the other person feel guilty
- It brings the matter to an end
R “At the time of anger, keeping silence is like changing gears of the mind… the result will be a well thought out action instead of a rash harmful reaction.”
@ greater-than-political-victory
Q The Quran calls the peace treaty of Hudaybiyyah a “clear victory.” Why, according to the article, is peace the greater success?
* Peace opens the door to every kind of opportunity, while political victory gives only control of a territory :: Correct. After the treaty the message spread rapidly.
- Peace costs less than victory
- Peace pleases one’s opponents
- Peace is easier to achieve
R “Political victory gives you only one thing and that is administrative control over a piece of land. But peace has much greater potential. Peace opens the door to multiple opportunities.”
@ indeciseveness
Q Why does the article compare decisions in life to catching a train?
* The train leaves on time whether we are ready or not – so one must prepare and decide in time :: Right. Wavering means being left on the platform.
- Trains are often late, so there is no hurry
- One should always travel with others
- The destination matters more than the journey
R “The train is no respecter of persons. It arrives on time and departs on time… we will miss the train of life and be left standing if we fail to get on board by taking timely and right decisions.”
@ total-involvement-in-a-task
Q In group work, people tend to leave the burden to others. What does the article say produces full commitment?
* Seeing the common good as one’s own good :: Yes. A deep sense of social consciousness.
- A larger personal reward
- Strict supervision
- Working with friends only
R “Only when one has come to think of the common good as one’s own good, of the profit of society as one’s own profit, will one become fully committed to collective work.”
@ spiritual-progress
Q A small child is pure by nature. How does the purity asked of an adult differ?
* It must be regained consciously, by his own effort, after the conditioning of his surroundings :: Well read. Self-attained purity is what is valued.
- It is the same, and needs no effort
- It is given by one’s family
- It cannot be regained
R “Now, he must make himself clean and pure spiritually by his own striving… It is this self-attained spiritual progress which is desirable in Islam.”
@ lessons-from-nature
Q Tiny organisms can do more harm than large, obvious dangers. What does the article compare to them in our lives?
* Small, repeated moments of negligence that quietly waste time and money and corrode character :: Exactly. Trivial for a day; enormous over a lifetime.
- Unkind remarks by strangers
- Rare but serious accidents
- Unexpected visitors
R “Easy going negligence can creep into our souls, like bacteria into the body, and if not pulled up short, can become an ingrained attitude.”
