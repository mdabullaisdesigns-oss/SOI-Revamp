## 2015-05
@ unintended-consequences-of-ones-actions
Q Why, according to the editorial, do actions so often bring results no one intended?
* People know their own ambitions but overlook the outside factors that may work against them :: Yes. Overestimating oneself is called man’s greatest weakness.
- People do not work hard enough
- Events are governed by chance
- Others deliberately spoil their plans
R “People are aware of their ambitions, but they do not take into account the external factors which may go against their plans.”
@ the-galaxy-speaks-about-its-great-creator
Q What response does the article expect from someone who could see the whole Milky Way at once?
* Wonder at the greatness of its Creator :: Right. Our galaxy is itself only a small part of the universe.
- Fear of being so small
- Pride in human science
- Indifference
R “The person will only be able to exclaim in wonderment: ‘The Creator is truly great, He made such a vast universe, of which our galaxy is only a tiny part!’”
@ happiness-is-a-state-of-mind
Q The article calls the shortcomings of life “shock treatment.” What does it mean?
* They are challenges meant to bring out a person’s inner abilities :: Yes. Taken as challenges, they make one positive.
- They are punishments to be endured
- They are illnesses needing a doctor
- They are accidents with no purpose
R “Inadequacies are like shock treatment meant to unfold a person’s inner treasure… take the differences as a challenge.”
@ ones-own-funeral-prayer
Q At a funeral a man asked what kind of prayer it was. The Imam answered, “Consider it as your own funeral prayer.” What did he mean?
* The prayer is truly said by one who sees his own death in the death before him :: Well read. It is a reminder, not a mere ritual.
- The man should leave the congregation
- The prayer had already been said for him
- The question was improper
R “The true funeral prayer is said by the one who sees his own death in the death of the deceased before us.”
@ focussing-on-the-future
Q A cricketer said he thinks only of the ball in front of him and forgets the last one. Why does the article call this good advice for life?
* Dwelling on the past – success or failure – divides one’s energy and harms the task ahead :: Correct. Past success breeds pride; past failure, distraction.
- The past is usually unpleasant
- Memory cannot be trusted
- The future is always better
R “If you remember the past, your energy will be divided into focussing on two phases of life… A wise person is one who completely forgets the past and focusses totally on the future.”
@ what-must-be-known
Q A visitor asked to call on a statesman who had died years before. What does the article take from the story?
* Not everything can be spelt out; without some prior knowledge a person cannot grasp what he is told :: Yes. One must already know “half.”
- Officials should brief travellers more fully
- News travels slowly between countries
- It is rude to ask questions abroad
R “Everything cannot be spelt out in words; there are some things that one has to know oneself… if one does not have half of it in one’s mind beforehand, then how can one grasp the whole picture?”
@ unlimited-capacities
Q What “law of nature” does the article mention about someone who loses a faculty such as sight?
* New capacities awaken in compensation, if the loss is taken with patience :: Right. Hence “differently-abled.”
- The other senses weaken as well
- The loss can always be reversed
- The person must depend wholly on others
R “If someone loses one part of his body, he develops new capacities in compensation… such a person takes his loss of vision in a positive sense, as a result of which his intellectual capacities are awakened.”
@ religious-extremism
Q With what does the article identify religious extremism?
* Religiosity reduced to outward form, once the inner spirit has been lost :: Yes. It arises in a community’s decline.
- Too much study of scripture
- Excessive charity
- Deep personal devotion
R “Religious extremism is but another name for religiosity based simply on form.”
@ religious-extremism
Q Where, says the article, must the renewal of such a community begin?
* With the reform of individual members :: Exactly. Institutions built by unreformed people only look grand.
- With a new government
- With larger organisations
- With grander buildings
R “Efforts to work with a community that has degenerated have to begin with the reform of individual members, and not in the form of collective efforts.”
@ untimely-death
Q A young cricketer died after being struck in a match. What message does the article say every death carries for the living?
* That their own chance still remains and should be used :: Well read. No death is “untimely” in God’s plan.
- That sport is too dangerous
- That life is unfair
- That the young should not make plans
R “For those who are alive, every death gives a message that they still have their chance and therefore must avail of it.”
