## 2014-08
@ the-emergence-of-a-new-india
Q The editorial says India is the world’s largest democracy in a “quantitative” sense. What does it ask to be added?
* A qualitative, value-based democracy, drawing on peace and spirituality :: Yes. These two are called India’s real identity.
- A larger number of political parties
- A stronger army
- A faster-growing population
R “The time has come to show the world that Indian democracy is value-based rather than simply power-based… Peace and spirituality make up the real identity of India.”
@ true-islam
Q How does the article relate the spirit of an Islamic practice to its outward form?
* Both are needed, but spirit is the real part; form allows concessions, spirit does not :: Right. Wiping over socks in the cold is the example of a concession in form.
- Form is the real part and spirit is optional
- Spirit alone is enough; form may be dropped
- They are equal in every respect
R “Spirit is the real part of Islam while the form is a relative part… Islam gives some concession in form, but there is no concession in spirit.”
@ true-islam
Q According to the verse quoted in the article, what makes an act of charity truly Islamic?
* That the giver’s heart trembles at the thought of returning to his Lord :: Yes. Charity without this spirit is not what the Quran describes.
- That it is given in public
- That it is a large amount
- That it goes only to relatives
R “Those who give to others what has been bestowed upon them with their hearts trembling at the thought that they must return to their Lord.”
@ creative-leadership
Q What separates creative leadership from the uncreative kind, in the article’s account?
* It looks beneath the problems for opportunities and plans before acting, where the other only protests :: Exactly. The test is the result, not the slogan.
- It commands a larger following
- It uses more forceful language
- It avoids all planning
R “Creative leadership is able to see what is beneath the surface. It is able to discover an opportunity amidst the jungle of problems.”
@ the-difference-between-al-deen-and-shariah
Q How does the article distinguish Al-Deen from Shariah?
* Al-Deen is the unchanging core given to every prophet; Shariah is its application, which has varied with circumstances :: Well read. Hence the place of ijtihad.
- Al-Deen is for scholars; Shariah for ordinary people
- Al-Deen changes; Shariah never does
- They are two names for one thing
R “Al-Deen refers to that part of divine guidance which has been given equally to all the prophets… Different Prophets have been given a different Shariah.”
@ the-challenge-of-satan
Q What made Satan ungrateful in spite of all he had been given, and how does he mislead people in the same way?
* One grievance filled his mind and blotted out every blessing; he magnifies people’s complaints likewise :: Yes. Feeling ungrateful is a signal to turn back to God.
- He was given less than the angels
- He did not know who had created him
- He was promised a reward and denied it
R “He generalized one event of complaint so much that he could not remember anything other than this grievance.”
@ way-to-happiness
Q The article tells of a man who made a great fortune and still ended in despair. What does it say brings peace of mind?
* Making God one’s concern and finding one’s real purpose – “in the remembrance of God hearts can find comfort” :: Right. Happiness cannot be bought.
- Giving all one’s money away
- Retiring early from work
- Avoiding illness through careful living
R “Finding God means to find one’s real purpose in life… ‘Surely in the remembrance of God hearts can find comfort’ (13:28).”
@ india-pakistan-relations
Q What is the “delinking policy” that the article recommends for India and Pakistan?
* Keeping disputed questions for negotiation while opening trade, education, travel and other ties :: Yes. The article points to post-war Japan as an example.
- Ending all contact until disputes are settled
- Handing the disputes to other countries
- Joining the two economies into one
R “The delinking policy in this regard means putting controversial issues on the negotiation table, and opening up all other relationships such as trade, education… tourism.”
@ suicide-is-not-an-option
Q How should a person regard a failure, according to the article?
* As a delay, not a final end – and so live in hope :: Correct. Hermann Hesse went on to win the Nobel Prize.
- As proof that the goal was wrong
- As a punishment
- As a reason to stop trying
R “If someone fails in doing something, he should take it as delay and not as final failure… One has to adopt the formula of wait and see.”
@ the-word-of-god
Q “We are closer to him than his jugular vein… each word he utters shall be noted down.” What does the commentary say makes this easier to grasp today?
* Known facts of recording in nature – thought, sound and action all leave traces :: Well read. So nothing a person intends, says or does is lost.
- The invention of printing
- The growth of written law
- The spread of schooling
R “The study of this world shows that there is an unerring system of ‘recording’ in operation here… these known facts are what make the Quran’s claim credible.”
