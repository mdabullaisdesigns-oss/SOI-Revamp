## 2015-06
@ you-cannot-hang-an-ideology
Q Why, according to the editorial, does force fail to end a movement built on an idea?
* An idea outlives its leaders; it can be answered only by a better idea :: Yes. Hence the call for a counter-ideology.
- Force is never used firmly enough
- Leaders are too well protected
- Ideas spread only in secret
R “It is not possible to bring an end to the influence of an ideology by force. Even if some leaders are hanged, new leaders will emerge and keep the ideology alive.”
@ from-planet-earth-to-planet-paradise
Q Stephen Hawking suggested mankind should settle on other planets. What does the article offer as the Quran’s view of man’s future home?
* The earth is a temporary abode; the lasting home to prepare for is Paradise :: Right. One must become a deserving candidate for it.
- Mankind will remain on earth for ever
- Another planet has been made ready for us
- The future cannot be known at all
R “The earth has been made as a temporary abode for human beings… Then the other abode for humans would be Paradise.”
@ theoretical-bossism-practical-bossism
Q What solution does the article propose for quarrels over “who is the boss,” for example at home?
* Be your own master in thought, and for practical matters be willing to let the other person lead :: Yes. Cooperation is a must in practical life.
- Decide by turns who gives the orders
- Let the elder always decide
- Avoid any shared work
R “In theoretical matters, take yourself as your own boss; but in practical matters, as per your convenience, accept the other person as the boss.”
@ a-counter-ideology-is-required
Q The article contrasts treating religion as something to be “followed” with treating it as something to be “imposed.” Why does the difference matter?
* Following turns a person to reforming himself; imposing turns him against rulers and so towards violence :: Well reasoned. The aim is the individual, not the state.
- Following is for the weak; imposing for the strong
- Following is private; imposing is more sincere
- There is no real difference in practice
R “If Islam is the subject to be followed, then the goal is to focus on oneself… But when Islam is considered a subject of imposition, the goal then becomes the external political system… Here lies the root of violence.”
@ a-training-course-in-thanksgiving-and-piety
Q Fasting is described as training with a dual purpose. What are the two?
* Gratitude for God’s blessings, and taqwa – holding back from what He forbids :: Correct. “The whole life of the believer is a life of fasting.”
- Health of the body, and saving of money
- Charity to neighbours, and hospitality to guests
- Memorising scripture, and learning its language
R “Fasting is training with a dual purpose. One is thanksgiving, the other Taqwa (righteousness or piety).”
@ god-and-man
Q What common mistake about God does the article describe?
* Imagining Him on the pattern of human beings – for example, as needing intermediaries or favouring one group :: Yes. Such beliefs are called a negation of His divinity.
- Thinking of Him as the Creator of all
- Believing that He hears prayer
- Holding that He is One
R “He visualises God in his image and vice versa. Human history is the story of this mistake and its consequences.”
@ news-of-promotion
Q A young man’s news of his promotion at work reminded the author of something greater. What?
* Entry into heaven – promotion from this imperfect world to the perfect one :: Right. So one should weigh one’s actions before they are weighed.
- The author’s own early career
- The reward of a long life
- The honour paid to scholars
R “Entry into heaven also is a promotion. People whose record in this imperfect world is found to be good will be promoted and allowed to enter into the perfect world of heaven.”
@ fasting-for-the-greatest-reward
Q How does the article describe the relation between Ramazan and the other eleven months?
* Ramazan is for preparation through study of the Quran; the rest of the year is for sharing its message :: Yes. That is why its reward is unlimited.
- Ramazan is for rest; the other months for work
- Ramazan is for charity; the other months for saving
- Ramazan is for travel; the other months for home
R “The month of Ramazan—is meant for preparation and the remaining eleven months are for spreading the message of the Quran to all mankind.”
@ clarity-of-expression
Q Why, according to the article, is it harder to be clear about ideas than about physical things?
* Physical things come already separate in nature; ideas lie mixed in the mind and must be sorted by analysis :: Well read. Objective, unprejudiced thinking is needed.
- Ideas are less important than things
- Words for ideas do not exist
- Physical things never change
R “In contrast to material objects… ideas do not exist separately. They exist in our minds mixed up along with other ideas.”
@ equality
Q On what basis, according to the article, did Islam teach human equality?
* The only distinction is between God and man; every human being is equally His servant :: Exactly. Distinctions among people fall away.
- All people have the same abilities
- All people should own the same amount
- All people belong to one nation
R “It stressed that the distinction was between God and man, and that there was no distinctions between human beings. Every human being, it taught, was a servant of God.”
