## 2018-05
@ u-turn-a-principle-of-life
Q A driver who had taken a wrong road turned back after five miles. What principle of life does the editorial draw from this?
* On finding one’s course mistaken, reassess and change direction without delay :: Yes. “When you cannot change reality, change yourself.”
- Once a course is chosen, keep to it whatever happens
- Never travel on unfamiliar roads
- Always let others choose the way
R “When they find that their journey is taking them down a blind alley, they need to revise their policy… they should change course without any further ado.”
@ u-turn-a-principle-of-life
Q A U-turn may involve some loss. How does the editorial weigh it?
* That loss is the lesser evil; going against reality is the greater :: Right. It is the policy of the wise.
- The loss shows the turn was a mistake
- Any loss must be avoided at all costs
- Losses should be recovered from others
R “Any such loss is the lesser evil, while going against reality is to opt for the greater evil.”
@ the-inner-diamond
Q A raw diamond loses much of itself in cutting before it becomes a jewel. What is the parallel for a human being?
* Each person is born as a potential that he himself must work to turn into reality :: Well read. Character does not develop by itself.
- People are valued by their wealth
- Hardship makes people smaller
- Only a few are born with any worth
R “Like a diamond, a human being is born in the form of a potential. It is for him to convert this potential into actuality.”
@ shocks-and-shock-absorbers
Q What does the article call the human equivalent of a vehicle’s shock absorber?
* Spirituality – positive thinking that keeps one steady through unpleasant events :: Yes. Its greatest enemy is complaining.
- Wealth
- A wide circle of friends
- Physical fitness
R “The human equivalent of a shock absorber in a vehicle is ‘spirituality’. A spiritual person is one who has an in built ‘shock absorber’.”
@ being-useful-to-society
Q In the parable of the torrent (13:17), the foam is cast away and what is useful remains. What aim does the article set for a person in society?
* To be a “giver” – or at the very least a no-problem person :: Correct. Otherwise one is the foam of the parable.
- To be the most admired person
- To keep apart from society
- To take as much as one gives
R “The higher aim should be to live as a ‘giver’ member of society or the least you should aim for is to be a no-problem person in society.”
@ the-price-of-realization
Q What does the article say is the price of a high level of God-realization?
* Making it the primary concern of life and treating all else as secondary :: Right. Nothing is gained without its price.
- Giving up one’s livelihood
- Many years of travel
- The approval of the learned
R “What is the price of a high level of realization? It is simply to make it the primary concern in your life and regard all other things as secondary.”
@ the-spirit-of-ramazan
Q How does fasting produce sympathy, according to the article?
* By making one feel hunger and thirst, it awakens awareness of others’ hardship and a will to share :: Yes. And care not to take another’s rightful share.
- By giving time to visit relatives
- By reducing the household’s expenses
- By gathering people at one table
R “It makes him aware of hunger and thirst. This understanding makes him aware of the difficulties of his fellow beings and inculcates in him a sense of sympathy.”
@ fasting-oriented-life
Q To what part of a vehicle does the article compare fasting, and why?
* The brakes – it trains one to restrain desires so that life’s journey stays on course :: Well reasoned. Without brakes the journey fails.
- The engine – it supplies energy
- The wheels – it keeps life moving
- The lamp – it lights the road
R “The position of fasting in human life is akin to the brakes in an engine… fasting is like applying brakes on the things forbidden by God.”
@ lailatul-qadr-the-night-of-destiny
Q The Prophet came out to tell people about the Night of Destiny, saw two men quarrelling, and the knowledge was taken from him. What lesson does the article draw?
* Even a quarrel in words can keep away divine blessing; a positive, conflict-free mind is needed to receive it :: Exactly. Positivity makes one worthy.
- Such knowledge is not meant to be shared
- The two men were to be punished
- Important news should be given in private
R “The two believers were merely arguing, but even fighting with words is enough to have the divine knowledge taken away… Positivity is the most important trait that makes a man worthy of receiving God’s blessings.”
@ a-door-not-a-grave
Q At a burial the author saw people chatting about weather, prices and politics. How does he say a grave should be seen?
* As an open door to the next world, reminding us of what awaits after death :: Yes. The real problem is the one beyond that door.
- As a place to be left quickly
- As the end of all concerns
- As a place for meeting acquaintances
R “The grave therefore is not really a grave, but rather, a door that has been opened for the deceased to enter the other world.”
