## 2015-01
@ road-to-happiness
Q What does the editorial name as the reason for the unhappiness found among rich and poor alike?
* Man seeks an explanation of life, and the world seems unexplained until the Hereafter is taken into account :: Yes. The answer he found was in the remembrance of God.
- People compare themselves with others
- Modern life moves too fast
- Wealth is unfairly shared
R “Man is born with an ‘explanation-seeking’ nature, but is compelled to live in a world that appears unexplained to him… the world Hereafter has to be taken into consideration.”
@ road-to-happiness
Q The editorial compares life to a train journey towards a known destination. What is the point of the comparison?
* One who is on the right path is glad at each stage, knowing he is nearer the goal of Paradise :: Right. Death becomes a gateway, not an end.
- Life passes too quickly to enjoy
- Fellow travellers cannot be chosen
- One should get down at every station
R “If you are on the right path… you will only be happy that every day and night you are coming closer to your goal which is none other than eternal Paradise.”

Q How, according to the article, can militancy carried out in the name of religion be ended?
* By a counter-ideology – patiently showing the peaceful teaching of Islam – not by the gun :: Yes. It is “ideology versus ideology.”
- By stricter punishment alone
- By closing schools of religion
- By ignoring it until it fades

@ patience
Q The Quran promises those who are patient a reward “without measure.” How does the article understand this reward?
* As the growth of one’s personality, which patience makes possible :: Well read. Patience keeps anger and hatred from blocking natural growth.
- As wealth in this world
- As a long life
- As victory over one’s enemies
R “In the above verse of the Quran, reward refers to personality development… the biggest source of personality development is patience.”
@ patience
Q What simple method does the article recommend when one is upset by an unwanted situation?
* Keep silent and empty the mind for thirty seconds :: Correct. The wave of reaction breaks and passes.
- Leave the place for a day
- Write down the complaint
- Speak at once and clear the air
R “You need to empty your mind for thirty seconds by observing silence. As a result, the anti-wave that had arisen would break and disperse.”
@ starting-from-scratch
Q Pelé’s first football was a sock stuffed with rags. How does the article read his poverty?
* As a challenge that awakened his drive – a blessing in disguise :: Yes. “Poverty is not deprivation. Rather, it is a challenge.”
- As a curse he escaped by luck
- As proof that talent needs money

@ the-people-of-the-book
Q In Quran 2:62 the Muslims are named together with Jews, Christians and Sabaeans. What does the article conclude?
* No community has a special claim; reward depends on true belief and righteous deeds :: Right. God regards a person’s thoughts and actions.
- All four communities are certain of salvation
- The verse applies only to ancient times
- The order of the names shows their rank
R “Belonging to a particular community gives no special status in the eyes of God… people will be judged according to their deeds and character.”
@ indifference
Q What contrast does the article lament?
* People speak warmly and at length of a son, an ancestor or a hero, yet have nothing to say of God :: Yes. It asks whether hearts have “withered of all faith.”
- People speak much and act little
- People praise the dead and neglect the living
- People know other lands better than their own
R “Alas! Those people who have volumes in praise of man, but not even a few words in praise of God.”
@ a-phenomenon-of-the-body-not-of-the-soul
Q Drummer Rick Allen lost an arm and returned to perform. What does the article say this shows?
* The inner energy of a person stays whole even when part of the body is lost :: Exactly. It calls this a law of nature, open to all.
- Modern medicine can replace any limb
- Music heals the body
- Only exceptional people can overcome loss
R “If a person’s hand or leg is cut off, his inner energy still remains intact—it does not get diminished.”
@ get-rid-of-depression
Q Why, according to the article, does the pursuit of material enjoyment end in boredom while intellectual and spiritual pursuit does not?
* The material domain is very limited, whereas the domain of thought and spirit has no boundary :: Well reasoned. Newton felt like a boy on the shore of a vast ocean.
- Material things cost too much
- Spiritual people need less sleep
- Boredom is a sign of illness
R “The domain of fulfilment in the material world is very limited; while, the domain of spiritual or intellectual fulfilment is so vast that words fail to express its endless boundaries.”
