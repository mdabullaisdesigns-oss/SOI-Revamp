## 2014-06
@ follow-reason-manage-emotions
Q When reason and emotional pressure pull in different directions, what does the editorial advise?
* Meet the rational demand, and handle the emotional pressure with tact :: Yes. Emotions can be managed; the laws of nature cannot be altered.
- Give way to family feeling first
- Postpone the decision until feelings settle
- Follow whichever is felt more strongly
R “Emotional pressures are always manageable but rational demands do not give man a second option.”
@ follow-reason-manage-emotions
Q A woman’s mother was angry whenever she returned late from a job she needed. What did the author suggest she say?
* “Mother, I am hungry right now. Please give me food and we shall talk later” :: Right. A gentle reply defused the moment and she kept her job.
- “My work is not your concern”
- “I shall leave the job tomorrow”
- Nothing at all, until the anger passed
R The author advised her to keep the job, which reason required, and to manage her mother’s anger politely – “in this way her problem was solved comfortably.”
@ god-oriented-life
Q The earth revolves around the sun. What is this a picture of, in the article?
* A life centred on God – which man must choose of his own free will :: Yes. The earth is compelled; man surrenders by choice.
- The shortness of human life
- The dependence of the weak on the strong
- The sameness of every year
R “Man must indeed revolve around God, just as the earth revolves around the sun… man, of his own free will, should surrender to God.”
@ god-oriented-life
Q What, according to the article, happens to a person who does not find God?
* The urge remains, so he gives the place of God to some created thing :: Well read. A person, a force of nature, an idea, or the self.
- The urge to worship fades away
- He becomes free of all dependence
- He turns naturally to science
R “It is possible for man not to accept the real God as God, but it is not possible for anyone to save himself or herself from granting the status of divinity to something other than God.”
@ message-of-ramazan
Q The Prophet declined an offer to turn a valley of Makkah into gold, preferring to be fed one day and hungry the next. Why?
* So that in hunger he might call on God, and when satisfied thank and praise Him :: Exactly. Each condition calls forth its own feeling of faith.
- Because wealth is forbidden to prophets
- Because gold was of no use in Makkah
- Because hunger is good for health
R “So that when I feel hungry, I can beseech You and remember You and when I am full and satiated, I can praise You and thank You.”
@ sanctity-of-human-life
Q The article traces a chain of killings among the sultans of Granada after 1302. What point does this history illustrate?
* Once the sanctity of life is broken, one murder opens the door to many :: Yes. This is why killing one person is likened to killing all mankind.
- Kingdoms fall when rulers are too gentle
- Spain was badly governed for centuries
- Royal families should be kept small
R “A person who commits such a crime gives licence to others in society… The murder of one human being opens the door to the killing of mankind.”
@ the-divine-way
Q Countless stars move at great speed without colliding. What does the article say this teaches about living in society?
* Each person must keep within the sphere God has set, and peace follows :: Right. Crossing limits brings clashes.
- People should have as little to do with each other as possible
- Speed and energy matter most
- The strong should lead and the rest follow
R “If everyone moves in his respective sphere, a state of peace is automatically established in society. But when people cross their limits… society will witness clashes and confrontations.”
@ fasting-as-a-spiritual-culture
Q What does the article say fasting symbolises beyond a month of going hungry?
* A lifelong culture of dedication – simple living and high thinking, free of distraction :: Yes. The believer is “a man of mission.”
- A yearly test of physical endurance
- A way to save on food
- A custom kept for the sake of community
R “It symbolizes a lifelong culture, that is, a culture of dedication… the adoption of the well-known formula — simple living, high thinking.”
@ embellishment-of-deeds
Q How, according to the article, does Satan lead people into wrongdoing?
* By making evil look attractive – dressing false arguments as sound ones :: Correct. He works on the mind, not by force.
- By physically compelling them
- By removing their free will
- By hiding the truth from prophets
R “He does it by allurement… It is to present a baseless argument as a strong argument in favour of an action… They find constructive words for their destructive activities.”
@ second-expulsion
Q What does the article compare to the forbidden tree, and what does it see as the sign that the limit has been crossed?
* A life of luxury beyond real need; global warming :: Well reasoned. The earth’s resources allow need, not greed.
- Scientific curiosity; space travel
- Large families; overcrowding
- City life; the loss of villages
R “A life of luxury was, for man, like the forbidden tree of Paradise… Global warming is like a proclamation by nature that this target of man is never going to be fulfilled in this present world.”
