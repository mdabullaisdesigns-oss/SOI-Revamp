## 2020-01
@ restoration-of-religion
Q When centuries of soot were cleaned from Michelangelo’s frescoes, their original colours proved far brighter than anyone had thought. What does the article compare with this?
* True religion, whose original form has been dimmed by later additions and needs restoring :: Yes. Then it is seen to be quite different from what was supposed.
- The fading of human memory
- The decline of the arts
- The ageing of old buildings
R “In very much the same way, the nature of true religion has become distorted over the centuries… it is in need of restoration to its original form.”
@ restoration-of-religion
Q How does the article contrast religion in its pure form with religion in a distorted form?
* Pure religion springs from the heart and breeds humility and unity; distorted, it becomes empty rites and a source of pride :: Well read. The spirit is lost while the form remains.
- Pure religion is simple; distorted religion is complicated
- Pure religion is old; distorted religion is new
- Pure religion is private; distorted religion is public
R “Religion in its pristine form breeds humility in its followers, fostering human unity; but when religion becomes distorted, it becomes a source of pride.”
@ signs-of-god-scattered-in-the-universe
Q Most of the earth’s water is salty and undrinkable. Which natural process does the article point to as a sign of God?
* Evaporation by the sun, which leaves the salt behind and returns the water as fresh rain :: Right. Nature’s own desalination.
- Rivers washing salt back to the sea
- Ice forming at the poles
- Springs rising from deep rock
R “Heated by the sun, seawater evaporates. The vapour rises up in the atmosphere, leaving behind the salt that the water had contained.”
@ make-god-your-supreme-concern
Q Many speak of God-realization (marifah) without attaining it. Why, according to the article?
* They wish for it but will not pay its price – giving themselves to it completely :: Yes. “Knowledge gives you a part of it only when you give yourself to it completely.”
- It is granted only to scholars
- It requires great wealth
- It comes only in old age
R “The reason is that they wish to attain the realization of God but they are not willing to pay the price… The price of God-realization is total surrender.”
@ make-god-your-supreme-concern
Q Can God-realization be handed down from father to son, in the article’s view?
* No – it is personal and comes only through one’s own effort; otherwise it is merely traditional belief :: Correct. A lifelong journey of discovery.
- Yes, in devout families
- Yes, if the father is learned
- Only to the eldest child
R “Marifah… is not something to be inherited… Whatever one receives without personal struggle will be just a kind of traditional belief rather than a living marifah.”
@ the-flawless-universe
Q Science has found the universe to be well planned and well managed – even “intelligent.” What does the article say follows from this?
* That it is the work of an intelligent Creator :: Right. No other explanation fits.
- That it needs no explanation
- That it will run forever
- That it was shaped by human observers
R “Accepting the universe as an intelligent universe means also that this universe is the creation of an intelligent Creator.”
@ appearance-and-reality
Q Two people sit down to the same meal. What distinguishes the one the article calls “seeing” from the one it calls “blind”?
* He recognises the food as God’s gift, the product of the whole universe, and feels wonder and gratitude :: Well reasoned. The other sees only shop-bought goods.
- He eats more slowly
- He knows the price of each item
- He has cooked the food himself
R “When he drinks milk, or puts a piece of fruit into his mouth, he feels that it is a unique blessing from God.”
@ appearance-and-reality
Q What does “faith in the unseen” mean, as the article explains it?
* Realising that what seems to come from human hands comes in truth from God :: Yes. Looking through appearance to reality.
- Believing whatever one is told
- Ignoring the visible world
- Expecting miracles daily
R “Faith in the unseen means the realization that the things which we seem to receive from the hands of men, come in actuality, from the hands of God.”
@ procession-of-death
Q We see or hear of a death almost daily. Why, says the article, do we fail to take in what it means?
* We are absorbed in worldly concerns and think of people only in worldly terms, not in relation to the eternal world :: Exactly. No living picture of the Hereafter is before us.
- Because death is rare
- Because it happens far away
- Because the young are not told of it
R “We see man in relation to this ephemeral world, but fail to see him in relation to the next eternal world.”
@ the-word-of-god
Q The commentary speaks of two ways of denying the Hereafter. What are they?
* Denying it in words, and denying it in practice by living for this world alone :: Well read. In effect the two are the same.
- Denying it in youth and in old age
- Denying it in private and in public
- Denying it in prose and in verse
R “One group rejecting it by word of mouth and the other doing so in practice.”
