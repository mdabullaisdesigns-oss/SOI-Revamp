## 2015-09
@ living-with-an-open-mind
Q The editorial says we should answer a remark about our faults as we answer a mirror. What does that mean?
* Accept it without taking offence and put the matter right :: Yes. Taking offence is the great obstacle to learning.
- Look away from it
- Return the remark in kind
- Ask for a second opinion
R “When a mirror tells you have dirt on your face, you don’t get angry. Rather, you will accept it as true and promptly wash your face.”
@ the-importance-of-stability
Q What happens to a tree that is dug up and replanted again and again, and what does the article compare this to?
* Its roots shorten until it cannot grow – like a person who keeps changing his place and never builds a history :: Right. Stability is the condition of growth.
- It grows stronger – like a person who travels widely
- It flowers sooner – like a person who learns quickly
- Nothing changes – place does not matter
R “A person who frequently changes his place of work is one who has had a ‘break in his history’… a person without a history is like a rootless tree.”
@ the-importance-of-stability
Q Which saying of the Prophet does the article quote in support of constancy?
* “The best deeds are those which are done regularly, even if they are small” :: Correct.
- “Seek knowledge even if far away”
- “Actions are judged by intentions”
- “Tie your camel, then trust in God”
R “The Prophet of Islam said: ‘The best deeds are those which are done regularly, even if they are small’ (Ibn Majah).”
@ two-types-of-people
Q The article says one group “keeps the markets of the world turning over” while another “turns over new pages in human history.” What marks the second group?
* They work for a worthwhile cause without seeking reward or credit :: Yes. They remember responsibilities, not rights.
- They are better paid
- They are more highly educated
- They work alone
R “Those who are destined to perform great deeds in life are those who do not seek reward for what they have done; the very fact that they have done something is sufficient reward for them.”
@ friendliness-and-separation
Q Some hold that the Quran discourages friendship with people of other faiths. How does the article read the verses cited (60:1, 60:4)?
* They concerned a particular wartime incident, not relations in times of peace :: Well reasoned. Relations with others rest on the laws of peace.
- They apply to all people at all times
- They forbid only business dealings
- They were addressed to other communities
R “What is stated in this chapter… applies for times when war prevails between the followers of Islam and others and not when peace has been established.”
@ world-of-hope
Q Newton lost his father before birth and grew up largely alone. What does the article draw from his story?
* Even hard experiences hold a plus point – his solitude made him a deep thinker :: Yes. “This world is a world of hope.”
- Genius is inherited
- Children do better without parents
- Unhappy people cannot succeed
R “No matter what experiences we go through—even seemingly negative ones—there is always a positive aspect or plus point contained in them.”
@ has-the-age-of-oppression-returned
Q Urged to join the fighting among Muslims of his day, Abdullah ibn Umar refused. What reason did he give?
* God had forbidden him to shed his brother’s blood; the fighting of the Prophet’s day had been to end persecution, which was over :: Right. He would not fight to create strife.
- He was too old to fight
- He supported the other side
- He was waiting for a better leader
R “God prevents me from doing so, for He has forbidden me from spilling my brother’s blood… In the Prophet’s day we fought until there was no more oppression.”
@ the-question-of-justice
Q How does the article describe the duty of an ordinary person, as against a ruler, with regard to justice?
* An ordinary person must act and speak justly in his own sphere; one given authority must also establish a just system :: Yes. Responsibility follows capacity.
- Both must set up courts
- Neither is responsible for justice
- The ordinary person must enforce justice on the ruler
R “The responsibility of an ordinary person… is to act justly at all times within the sphere in which he operates. But someone whom God has blessed with authority must… also establish a just system.”
@ the-weakness-of-man
Q From Adam’s eating of the forbidden fruit, what weakness of man does the article say Satan learnt to exploit?
* Wanting the whole and not being content with a part – which leads to ingratitude :: Exactly. In this world no one is given everything.
- Fear of the unknown
- Love of sleep
- Forgetfulness of names
R “Man, because of his disposition, will want the whole, and will not be content with only a part of it… As a result, mankind forever lives in ingratitude.”
@ social-living
Q What does the article say about a person who, when an opportunity is already before him, keeps praying for one?
* It is like asking for sunshine while the sun is shining; the moment calls for thanks and use of what is given :: Well read. Opportunity comes unannounced.
- He shows admirable persistence
- His prayer will bring a better opportunity
- He should stop praying altogether
R “When a person gets an opportunity and instead of recognizing it and utilizing it, he prays for an opportunity from God, he is going against the law of nature. Such a moment is a moment of being thankful to God.”
