## 2014-07
@ winners-and-losers-all
Q After India’s 16th general election, how does the editorial describe the proper role of the losing party?
* A supporting role in national development, not opposition for its own sake :: Yes. In a democracy both sides are winners.
- To withdraw from public life until the next election
- To block the new government wherever possible
- To form a rival government
R “The role of the opposition is not that of a political rival; rather, it is a role of participation… it is destined to perform a supporting role.”
@ winners-and-losers-all
Q How does the motto of democracy differ from that of kingship, in the editorial’s words?
* “I am here to serve the land,” not “I am here to rule the land” :: Right. Victory is a responsibility, not a cause for pride.
- “I am here to reward my supporters”
- “I am here to change the constitution”
- “I am here for life”
R “In the kingship era, the motto was: ‘I am here to rule the land’, but in a democracy the motto is quite different. It is: ‘I am here to serve the land.’”
@ the-spirit-of-ramazan
Q What link does the article draw between fasting and dua (supplication)?
* Fasting builds patience and a humble heart, from which sincere prayer rises :: Yes. “Real fasting is that which induces real dua.”
- Dua is only accepted during the fast
- Fasting replaces the need for dua
- Dua shortens the hours of the fast
R “Patience is the ground on which the blessed tree of dua is grown… Fasting increases the spiritual capability of a man’s heart.”
@ the-spirit-of-ramazan
Q Why is Ramazan called “the month of sympathy”?
* For some hours the well-off share the hunger and thirst the poor know daily :: Correct. Fasting teaches what basic needs are.
- Because people greet each other more often
- Because the sick are excused from fasting
- Because quarrels are forbidden by law
R “For a few hours, the rich also come to live in the same conditions in which a poor man lives.”
@ true-face
Q A neighbour said he could not accept Islam: the saint’s religion seemed beyond his strength, and the common people’s made him ashamed. What does the article conclude?
* Both are distortions; Islam in its original sources is simple, natural and appealing :: Well read. Later additions hid its true face.
- Islam is meant only for saints
- Ordinary believers cannot practise religion
- The neighbour was looking for excuses
R “Islam is a simple and natural religion… If all the artificial veils are lifted and Islam’s true face is seen, it will be recognized as the voice of human nature.”
@ zakat-the-prescribed-charity
Q In what spirit should a person give Zakat, according to the article?
* As handing others their rightful share of what God has given, without any sense of doing a favour :: Yes. All one owns is from God.
- As a generous gift for which thanks are due
- As a payment to be returned with interest
- As a tax owed to the ruler
R “When a man gives alms to others, he should do so with the knowledge that they have a rightful share in his wealth… He is doing no more than giving others their due.”
@ the-significance-of-iftar
Q Why should iftar be a simple meal?
* A lavish spread turns attention to taste and loses the gratitude and reflection the moment should bring :: Right. “A lavish iftar kills the true spirit of fasting.”
- Rich food is unhealthy after a fast
- Simple food is cheaper for the host
- The Prophet disliked eating in company
R “Simplicity saves us from distraction and will make us concentrate on the spiritual aspect of fasting.”
@ qualitative-or-quantitative-islam
Q What danger does the article see in measuring religion by counted rituals?
* It breeds complacency – a person feels his duty is done and stops fearing God :: Exactly. The Companions never felt their deeds were enough.
- It makes worship too difficult
- It discourages people from attending the mosque
- It leads to disputes over numbers
R “Having quantified his deeds, he feels that he has complied with what was expected of him and therefore becomes complacent.”
@ ramazan-and-eid-ul-fitr
Q The last night of Ramazan is called the “night of reward.” How does the article say it is best spent?
* In prayer, looking back over the month, and making fresh resolve :: Yes. Not in shopping and entertainment.
- In preparing the Eid feast
- In visiting the markets before they close
- In sleep, to be fresh for the morning
R “The ‘night of reward’ is best utilised in more prayer and supplication, in introspection of the whole month of Ramazan that has just passed and in making fresh resolutions.”
@ the-relationship-between-fasting-and-life
Q To what part of a vehicle does the article compare fasting, and why?
* The brakes – restraint over desire is what lets the journey be completed safely :: Well read. Half of life’s actions consist in abstaining.
- The engine – it supplies energy
- The wheels – it keeps life moving
- The lamps – it shows the way at night
R “The position of fasting in human life is akin to the brakes in an engine… so that he may travel successfully on the path of God.”
