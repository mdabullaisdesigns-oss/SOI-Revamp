@ aspiring-for-more-and-settling-for-less
Q After studying people who live in frustration, what cause does the editorial identify?
* Unrealistic goals that can never be reached :: Yes. The remedy: aspire for more, but be content with less.
- Too little ambition
- Lack of education
- Unkind employers
R “There is no other reason for this, except that of having unrealistic goals.”
@ aspiring-for-more-and-settling-for-less
Q Why does the editorial recall that Nehru first tried the law before entering public life?
* To show that when one option fails, there are others – take the next and try again :: Right. “Don’t give up, plan once again.”
- To show that law is a poor profession
- To show that success comes from family name
- To show that one should never change course
R “In this world, there are always several options and if one option does not work, one should take another and try it out.”
@ principles-for-success
Q “Even a bullock cart will do better than a jet plane which has no sense of direction.” What principle is meant?
* Direction matters more than speed – first make sure you are heading the right way :: Well read. Motion alone gets nowhere worth going.
- Slow travel is safer
- Old methods are the best
- Machines cannot be trusted
R “It is direction that is all important… Starting out in the right direction is the first important principle of success.”
@ principles-for-success
Q The article says success is reached by the ladder, not the lift. What does this mean?
* Progress comes step by step, paying the price at each stage; there are no buttons to press :: Yes. One accepts loss before gain.
- Tall buildings should be avoided
- Help from others should be refused
- Only the young can succeed
R “The climb is up the ladder one step after another, you cannot make a success of your life by the lift expecting it to do everything for you.”
@ dont-live-in-suspicion
Q Why does the article compare the habit of suspicion to smoking?
* It harms the person himself and also pollutes the atmosphere for everyone, destroying trust :: Well reasoned. It is a social, not just a private, evil.
- It is expensive
- It is found mostly among the old
- It is easily given up
R “The habit of suspicion is like smoking. It not only ruins the health of the individual who has this bad habit, but it also pollutes the whole of the social atmosphere.”
@ the-simple-formula-of-maarifat
Q Every day something we wanted fails to happen. How can such moments lead to God-realization, according to the article?
* Each reminds us of our limits – and where man’s limit ends, God’s domain begins :: Correct. Open to everyone, educated or not.
- By making us try harder next time
- By teaching us to want nothing
- By showing us whom to blame
R “Whenever man reaches his boundary, he should realize that immediately after this, the boundary of God starts.”
@ forgiveness
Q What does the article say forgiveness awakens in the other person, where tit-for-tat arouses the ego?
* The conscience :: Right. And conscience plays a positive role in disputes.
- Fear
- Curiosity
- Pride
R “If you follow the tit-for-tat policy, it arouses the ego of the other person, whereas if you follow the policy of forgiveness, it will activate the other person’s conscience.”
@ forgiveness
Q “If forgiveness is a full stop, revenge is punctuated by commas.” What is meant?
* Forgiveness ends an unwanted situation; revenge keeps extending it :: Yes. A chain of action and reaction.
- Forgiveness is brief; revenge takes planning
- Forgiveness is silent; revenge is spoken
- Forgiveness is final only in books
R “Forgiveness means ending an unwanted situation, while taking revenge means endlessly extending it.”
@ principles-of-islam
Q A Companion explained taqwa to Umar by asking how he walks a path with thorny bushes on both sides. What is the point of the image?
* Living with caution and restraint – gathering oneself in so as not to be caught in life’s snares :: Exactly. The path of avoidance, not entanglement.
- Clearing every thorn before moving on
- Staying at home to be safe
- Walking as fast as possible
R “Umar Farooq replied, ‘I gathered my clothes close to me and moved ahead cautiously’. The Companion said, ‘This is the meaning of taqwa.’”
@ death
Q When, according to the article, is a funeral prayer truly meaningful?
* When another’s death makes you remember and prepare for your own :: Well read. As if praying at your own funeral.
- When it is attended by a great crowd
- When it is performed slowly
- When it is followed by a meal
R “A truly meaningful funeral prayer is one in which someone else’s death provokes you to remember your own impending death.”
