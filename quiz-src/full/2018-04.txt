## 2018-04
@ stephen-hawking-is-no-more
Q The editorial names a “human contribution” of Stephen Hawking. What was it?
* He showed that a person with a severe disability can achieve what others do, using the mind and technology :: Yes. He ended the “disabled theory.”
- He founded hospitals for the paralysed
- He invented the wheelchair he used
- He refused all help from others
R “He put an end to the ‘disabled theory’, as Dr. Hawking was a living example who proved that one who has a challenging disability can overcome it.”
@ stephen-hawking-is-no-more
Q Why does the editorial find the search for a single theory of nature significant for religion?
* A universe governed by one unified law is like a scientific affirmation of the concept of one God :: Well reasoned. Harmony points to oneness.
- It shows the universe has no beginning
- It proves science and religion never meet
- It makes prayer unnecessary
R “It is this explanation which is the equivalent of a scientific affirmation of the concept of one God.”
@ the-concept-of-social-welfare-in-islam
Q How does the article describe zakat “in spirit” and “in its external form”?
* In spirit an act of worship; in form a social service :: Right. Not merely a tax.
- In spirit a tax; in form a gift
- In spirit a loan; in form a duty
- In spirit a custom; in form a law
R “In spirit, zakat is an act of worship, while in its external form, it is the carrying out of a social service.”
@ religion-a-divine-not-a-social-phenomenon
Q Nuclear energy is judged as a fact of nature, not by how one country uses it. What does the article conclude about understanding Islam?
* It should be understood from the Quran and Hadith, not from the conduct of any group of its followers :: Yes. Theory must be told apart from practice.
- It should be understood from newspapers
- It should be understood from the history of empires
- It cannot be understood by outsiders
R “Now one begins to look at Islam in the light of Quran and Hadith, instead of in the light of the practice of Muslims.”

- A large inheritance

@ god-is-the-greatest
Q The phrase Allahu Akbar is heard and said some three hundred times a day. What does the article say this repetition is meant to achieve?
* To make God’s greatness a constant part of one’s consciousness, which makes a person modest :: Right. It is “the essence of faith.”
- To help in counting the prayers
- To call others to attention
- To mark the passing hours
R “God’s greatness should be an integral part of his consciousness in such a way that under no condition should he lose awareness of it.”
@ duty-to-parents
Q Besides repaying what parents have given, what further benefit does the article see in serving them?
* It teaches one to look on all people with love and so to serve humanity at large :: Well read. Home is the first school of service.
- It secures a share of their property
- It earns the respect of neighbours
- It frees one from other duties

@ in-times-of-crisis
Q People in sudden danger commonly cry out to God. What does the article take this to show?
* That awareness of God is woven into human nature :: Yes. The call to God turns this potential into reality.
- That fear makes people irrational
- That people copy what others do
- That danger is always a punishment
R “This proves that the concept of God is interwoven in human nature. Every human being is potentially aware of God’s existence.”
@ acceptance-of-our-prayer-requests
Q A young man felt his prayers went unanswered. With what comparison does the article reply?
* A student cannot dictate his examination paper; God decides each person’s test and knows what is best :: Exactly. So pray: “Give me what is best for me.”
- A farmer who must wait for rain
- A traveller who has lost his way
- A merchant who must bargain
R “A student appearing for an examination cannot demand that the examination paper should be as he wants it to be… If our supplications do not appear to be accepted, we must know that this is what God wills.”
@ an-expert-in-gems

* Hidden talents need recognising – by honest self-awareness or by a sincere well-wisher :: Well reasoned. Both are rare, so much potential is lost.
- Talents always show themselves in time
