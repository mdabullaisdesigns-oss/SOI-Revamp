## 2020-08
@ in-gods-country
Q If man lives in “God’s country” and must live by God’s scheme, how is he to know what that scheme requires?
* Through the prophets, whose guidance is gathered in the Quran :: Yes. They set it out plainly for all.
- Through trial and error over many generations
- Through the customs of each land
- Through the opinion of the majority
R “It was to provide an answer to this question that God raised up His prophets… The Quran is a collection of this prophetic guidance.”
@ freedom-of-choice
Q Resources in nature’s keeping cause no havoc; in human hands they can. What remedy does the article propose?
* Man should learn to use them as nature does – applying nature’s model with self-discipline :: Well read. Discipline, not freedom alone, brings peace.
- Leave all resources untouched
- Give control to a single authority
- Develop stronger weapons for defence
R “Man must learn how to utilize these resources in the way nature utilizes them. The model of nature must be applied to human life.”
@ humans-on-trial
Q Why, according to the article, has man alone been given freedom?
* So that he may choose an upright life of his own free will and so earn the highest credit :: Right. Not to live without restraint.
- So that he may rule over other creatures
- So that he may enjoy himself as he likes
- Because he could not be controlled
R “Its purpose is that man should lead a morally upright life of his own free will, thus demonstrating that he is of the highest moral character.”
@ humans-on-trial
Q Freedom opens two doors for man. What decides which he passes through?
* Whether freedom makes him arrogant, or he stays humble and binds himself to God’s will :: Yes. Success or failure lies in the response.
- Whether he is born rich or poor
- Whether he lives long or briefly
- Whether others approve of him
R “If, on receiving freedom an individual becomes arrogant and insolent, it will mean that he has failed the test. If on the other hand, he remains modest and humble… he will have made the right use of his God-given freedom.”
@ the-reality-of-life
Q The article likens the seriousness the Prophet’s message demands to a particular moment. Which?
* A traveller at night whose torch suddenly shows a snake in his path :: Well read. Not a matter for idle theory.
- A student opening an examination paper
- A merchant counting his day’s takings
- A sailor sighting land
R “It requires every man to take a serious view of his situation—even more so than a traveller in the night whose torch reveals a black snake slithering menacingly in front of him.”
@ the-existence-of-evil
Q By what single principle does the article explain human history, including the presence of suffering?
* The principle of “test” – man is placed here to be tried :: Correct. Neither pure determinism nor pure freedom explains it.
- The principle of chance
- The principle of the survival of the strongest
- The principle of endless repetition
R “According to Islam, there is only one correct principle to interpret human history and that is the principle of ‘test’.”
@ the-existence-of-evil
Q Why, in the article’s reasoning, did the possibility of evil have to be allowed?
* Without real freedom to do wrong, choosing good would earn no credit – and freedom can be misused :: Well reasoned. A small price for something very precious.
- Because evil makes life interesting
- Because good cannot exist without equal evil
- Because the Creator overlooked it
R “Man can be granted the credit for a good deed only on the condition that, despite having the opportunity to indulge in bad deeds, he chooses of his own free will to act virtuously.”
@ find-your-true-calling
Q Moses was raised in Pharaoh’s palace, yet could not remain there. What does the article suggest such blocked paths may mean in our own lives?
* That God has a higher purpose in view and is prompting us to rethink and re-plan :: Yes. So setbacks can be received with gratitude.
- That we chose wrongly and must give up
- That others are working against us
- That effort is useless
R “If God prevents a task from happening the way you had planned it, it is a clue that He wants you to do something else.”
@ mans-uniqueness
Q In contrast to every other creature, what unique thing does the article say man must do?
* Live by truth he has discovered for himself, with self-imposed discipline :: Exactly. Others obey nature’s law without choosing.
- Build cities and machines
- Speak many languages
- Live longer than other creatures
R “In contrast to other living beings, man must do something unique—and that is, to live in self-discovered truth.”
@ the-word-of-god
Q The verses recall that man is created from a mere drop, yet becomes “openly contentious.” What lesson does the commentary draw?
* Remembering his humble origin, he would never be arrogant, and would be grateful for all he has been given :: Well read. Every benefit he enjoys was provided for him.
- That man is of no importance
- That disputes should be settled by force
- That animals are superior to man
R “If he were to keep in view the reality of his origin, he would never allow himself to become arrogant in this world.”
