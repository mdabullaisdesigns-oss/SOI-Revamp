## 2020-12
@ facebook-a-study-club
Q The editorial calls social media potentially “a study club.” What decides whether it turns out so for a given person?
* The choice he makes – to use it to learn from others, or to spread complaints and unverified news :: Yes. Everything can be used either way.
- The number of friends he has on it
- The country he lives in
- The device he uses
R “Everything in this world could be used to develop oneself positively or can be used to spread negativity. One should make responsible choices.”
@ the-beautiful-names-of-god
Q People asked which name of God to recite for protection in the pandemic. How does the article say the names of God should be understood?
* Not as mysterious formulas to chant, but as attributes to understand and call on with meaning :: Right. “Known doors of God’s mercy.”
- As words whose power lies in their sound
- As secrets known only to a few
- As charms to be written and worn
R “It is not about chanting, it is about understanding and discovering the attribute and beseeching God to help us in a particular situation.”

@ the-explanation-of-meaningfulness-of-the-universe
Q Everything in nature is found in pairs. What does the article reason from this about human life?
* An imperfect life before death must have its pair – a perfect life after death :: Yes. Otherwise man alone would be left incomplete.
- Every person must marry
- Every good has an equal evil
- Every question has two answers
R “Human life must have a pair, that is, a perfect life after death must be the pair of an imperfect life before death.”
@ homeschooling
Q A family whose talk had centred on complaints was advised to make their home a “homeschool.” What changed?
* Parents and children began learning and reading together, and positive discussion replaced complaint :: Correct. Each came to understand his role.
- The children were sent away to study
- The family stopped speaking at meals
- They moved to another city
R “Now the atmosphere of negative talk and complaining in their house has become a thing of the past. Instead, topics related to positive thinking and God-realization are being discussed.”

@ the-key-to-success
Q A villager dug such deep foundations that he had too little material left to roof his house. What does the story illustrate?
* Failure often comes not from lack of resources but from using them badly :: Yes. Planning must match one’s means.
- Deep foundations are always a waste
- Villagers should not build in brick
- Friends give poor advice
R “Failure often results not so much from a lack of resources as from available resources having been under-exploited or wrongly used.”
@ the-key-to-success
Q “Success is wholly a thing of the mind.” What two things does the article say bring success?
* The determination to reach one’s goal, and the best use of the resources at hand :: Exactly. Will first, then wise use.
- Good fortune and influential friends
- Great wealth and long experience
- Speed and secrecy
R “Success comes to those who have the determination to achieve their goals and put the resources they have at their disposal to the best possible use.”
@ on-pride-and-arrogance
Q How does the article describe the stance of a proud person towards truth?
* Instead of bowing before truth, he wants truth to bow before him :: Well read. Admitting a fault would lower his status.
- He searches for truth day and night
- He accepts truth only from the learned
- He keeps the truth to himself
