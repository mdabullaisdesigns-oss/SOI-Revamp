## 2014-02
@ this-is-not-the-time
Q A young man on his way to catch a train for a job interview would not let his friend chase boys who had thrown stones. What does the article draw from this?
* One who is serious about a goal has no time for distractions – and the believer’s goal is the Hereafter :: Yes. Patience keeps the journey on course.
- Young people should avoid quarrels in public
- Children should never be punished
- Travel plans matter more than friendship
R “One who is serious about the Hereafter does not want to get involved at all, in issues that will divert him from his goal.”
@ the-final-destination
Q Maurice Wilson wrote in his diary that he would reach the summit of Everest on his birthday, and died on the mountain. Why does the article tell his story?
* To show how people feel sure of “tomorrow” while moving, unaware, towards death :: Well read. “Life is absolutely uncertain, yet people imagine it to be absolutely certain.”
- To warn against mountaineering
- To praise the courage of explorers
- To show that planning always fails
R “Everyone thinks that he is advancing towards the peak of success… In actual fact, everyone is heading towards a destination where there will be no one to welcome him other than Death.”
@ differences-between-men-and-women
Q According to the article, how does the Quran describe the relation between men and women?
* As a pair that complete each other – equal in respect, different in roles :: Correct. Nature works on complementarity.
- As identical in every function
- As rivals for the same roles
- As independent of each other
R “Nature functions on the principle of complementarity… ‘Men and women are equal in respect and different in roles’.”
@ humility-and-sincerity-in-prayer
Q What, says the article, most easily pulls the mind away during prayer – and what guards against it?
* Negative thinking; it is guarded against by patience :: Yes. That is why patience is mentioned together with prayer.
- Tiredness; guarded against by sleep
- Noise; guarded against by solitude
- Hunger; guarded against by eating first
R “The most powerful thing that can shift one’s focus away from something is negative thinking… Offering prayer with humility and utter sincerity is possible only for the person who before prayer has developed positive thinking.”
@ from-the-spiritual-tree
Q On seeing an unfamiliar African tree, what thought came to the author?
* That it is as if “Made by God” were written on everything, so that God may be recognised through His creation :: Right. The craftsmanship itself announces the Maker.
- That African trees are finer than others
- That trees ought to be labelled with their names
- That nature grows without any design
R “It is as if God wrote the words ‘Made by God’ on everything and then kept Himself hidden from people’s vision so that they could recognize Him from the things that He has created.”
@ lessons-from-jesus-christ
Q Why, according to the article, does the Quran tell of many earlier prophets and not only of the Prophet Muhammad?
* So that believers may find guidance in how each prophet dealt with his situation :: Yes. “These are whom God guided aright, so follow their guidance.”
- To show which prophet was the greatest
- To correct the dates given by historians
- To record the history of ancient kings
R “It was so that the Prophet of Islam and his ummah might learn valuable lessons from the ways in which these prophets dealt with the situations in which they found themselves.”
@ lessons-from-jesus-christ
Q The article places Jesus’ words “Love your enemies, do good to those who hate you” beside a verse of the Quran. Which one?
* “Repel evil with good and he who is your enemy will become your dearest friend” :: Exactly (41:34). The same teaching is found in both.
- “For you your religion; for me mine”
- “There is no compulsion in religion”
- “Every soul shall taste death”
R “The same teaching is echoed in the Quran: And good deeds and evil deeds are not alike. Repel evil with good and he who is your enemy will become your dearest friend.”
@ dominion-as-a-test
Q “We alternate these days among mankind,” says the Quran. How should a person respond to gaining or losing power, in the article’s view?
* Without pride when in power and without bitterness when out of it, for both are tests :: Yes. Attention should stay on one’s own responsibilities.
- By holding on to power at any cost
- By protesting until power is restored
- By avoiding public life altogether
R “When he is granted power, he should not suffer from pride and arrogance. And when man finds himself in a state of subjugation, he should not fall prey to negative psychology.”
@ life-is-a-tightrope-walk
Q What does “tightrope walking” mean in what the article calls the dictionary of wisdom?
* Keeping a wise balance between one’s desires and outer realities :: Right. Leaning to either side brings a fall.
- Taking risks to entertain others
- Avoiding every decision
- Living without any desires
R “You have to maintain a wise balance between your personal desires and external realities. This is the real art of life.”
@ divine-signs
Q At a book fair the author saw a dog searching for food, unaware of the books around it. What did the sight suggest to him?
* That most people move among God’s signs without noticing them or asking life’s great questions :: Well read. People are “lost in the creation, and not in the Creator.”
- That animals should be kept out of public places
- That books are of little use to the hungry
- That knowledge belongs only to scholars
R “God’s signs… are visible everywhere, but most of us human beings move around in blind ignorance of them.”
