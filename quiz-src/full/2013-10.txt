## 2013-10
@ where-the-muslim-brotherhood-went-wrong
Q The editorial quotes the Prophet: “As you are, so will be the government upon you.” What does it conclude from this saying?
* Government grows out of society, so minds must be prepared first; a system cannot be imposed :: Yes. This was, in the author’s view, the Brotherhood’s mistake.
- A ruler should be obeyed whatever he does
- Good people always get good rulers at once
- Power should be taken first and society reformed later
R “The government always emerges from society. It cannot be imposed from outside… If your society is not ready to accept your system, you have to wait and engage yourself in the task of preparing the minds of people.”
@ where-the-muslim-brotherhood-went-wrong
Q Sir Arthur Keith wrote that the Egyptians “were conquered not by the sword, but by the Koran.” Why does the editorial recall this?
* To show that peaceful sharing of the message achieves what force cannot :: Well done. “The power of the Quran is greater than the power of the gun.”
- To show that Egypt was never ruled by Muslims
- To praise the early armies of Islam
- To argue that the Quran is a political manifesto
R The editorial offers the formula “dawah activism and political status-quoism,” and says the Quran is “a book of ideology,” not a political manifesto.
@ desire-to-seek-god
Q A returning astronaut wanted to kiss the earth. How does the article contrast this with the response of a true believer?
* The believer looks past the gift to the Giver and offers his deepest feelings to the Creator :: Exactly. He “rises above creation and discovers his Creator.”
- The believer feels nothing for the earth
- The believer prefers heaven to the earth
- The believer avoids travel beyond the earth
R “The one who does not believe thinks that the beauty of created things is actually intrinsic to these things… a true believer sees the beauty of the Creator in the beauty of creation.”
@ ritual-must-arise-from-the-spirit
Q A man prayed in the mosque with every outward form correct, yet the Prophet sent him to pray again. Why?
* His prayer was mechanical and lacked the spirit of modesty :: Right. Form without spirit is not worship.
- He had prayed at the wrong time
- He had missed a part of the ablution
- He had prayed too quickly for others to follow
R “However punctilious the worship in form, without the true spirit the performance of the ritual is invalid.”
@ ritual-must-arise-from-the-spirit
Q During the first Haj, pilgrims worried that they had performed rites in the wrong order. The Prophet said, “There is no harm.” Where did he say real harm lies?
* In dishonouring a person :: Yes. Where the spirit is present, a slip in form is tolerable.
- In missing a day of the pilgrimage
- In asking too many questions
- In performing Haj more than once
R “There is no harm. There is no harm. Real harm lies in the dishonouring of a person.”
@ de-conditioning-the-mind
Q By what process, according to the article, can a conditioned mind be de-conditioned?
* Honest introspection – accepting others’ criticism and being one’s own strictest critic :: Correct. The article calls these external and “self-hammering.”
- Moving to a new country
- Reading as widely as possible
- Avoiding people who disagree
R “De-conditioning of the mind can only be done by one process alone, and that is introspection… Either one accepts the ‘hammering’ of others or one becomes one’s own worst critic.”
@ importance-of-positive-thinking
Q Prophet Abraham advised his son to “change the threshold” after meeting one wife and to “maintain” it after meeting another. What made the difference?
* The first complained of her hardships; the second was grateful in the same conditions :: Yes. A great mission needs people free of complaint.
- The first was poor; the second was wealthy
- The first was a stranger; the second was a relative
- The first was idle; the second worked hard
R “For the great mission… the people who are suitable are only those who have the highest level of positive thinking, and who are free from all complaints.”
@ no-fear-of-accountability
Q Most people believe in life after death. Why, says the article, does this belief so rarely affect how they live?
* Each group has added a self-made belief that its own salvation is assured :: Well read. The article finds this among Muslims as much as others.
- They think the Hereafter is very far away
- They doubt that deeds are recorded
- They have never been taught about it
R “When other self-made beliefs are added to this belief, then believing in the life after life is almost as good as not believing in it at all.”
@ opportunities-will-never-cease
Q How does the article advise us to look on failure?
* As experience that can make us wiser and open a new path :: Right. When one opportunity ends, another appears.
- As a sign to give up the goal
- As proof that plans are useless
- As a punishment to be endured
R “When faced with failure, one must consider it as an experience and then move on to a new path… Failure can open even bigger doors of success for you.”
@ on-old-age
Q In what sense does the article call old age “a prior notice”?
* It is a reminder that death and accountability before God are near :: Yes. The most unfortunate is one who draws no lesson from it.
- It warns the young to respect their elders
- It tells a person to stop working
- It announces the end of one’s duties
R “Old age is a prior notice of one’s death… Old age can shake up a person and make him conscious of the Hereafter.”
