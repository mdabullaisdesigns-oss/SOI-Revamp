## 2017-06
@ no-thought-crime-in-islam
Q In the Prophet’s time some opponents spoke ill of him and his mission. What response does the article recall?
* The poet Hassan ibn Thabit was permitted to answer them in words :: Yes. “Words are responded to by words, and not by sword.”
- They were punished by the community
- They were forbidden to speak in public
- They were sent away from the town
R “One of the Muslims of the time, Hassan ibn Thabit, was given permission by the Prophet to respond in a literary method to the opponents.”
@ developing-ones-personality
Q A Hadith speaks of a black spot appearing on the heart after a wrong deed. What happens, according to the article, if a person keeps repeating the wrong without introspection?
* The heart gradually becomes insensitive, and truth no longer influences it :: Right. Repentance erases the spot; repetition spreads it.
- The spot fades by itself with time
- Others begin to notice the spot
- The person becomes more cautious
R “If he keeps committing one bad deed after another and does not introspect… then, gradually, his heart becomes insensitive.”
@ developing-ones-personality
Q Why does the article insist that a negative thought be turned into a positive one “at the very first instance”?
* Once it sinks from the conscious into the unconscious mind, it becomes part of the personality and cannot be removed :: Well reasoned. Delay hands control away.
- Because negative thoughts are forgotten quickly
- Because other people may come to know of it
- Because positive thoughts come only in the morning
R “If you delay in doing this, the thought will travel into your subconscious mind, and then… enter your unconscious mind… no matter how much you may try to separate it from yourself, you will not succeed.”
@ mans-uniqueness
Q Man alone has been given full freedom. What, then, is his test?
* To submit to God and live with self-discipline by his own choice, despite that freedom :: Yes. Every gift carries a responsibility.
- To use his freedom to master other creatures
- To give up his freedom altogether
- To discover how many living beings exist
R “The test for man is for him to bow down before God despite the freedom that he enjoys.”
@ the-sense-of-repentance
Q The Quran says God will change the evil deeds of some people into good ones (25:70). How does the article explain this?
* Sincere repentance creates a strong resolve to reform, so the wrong deed becomes a turning point towards good :: Exactly. The mistake becomes a starting point.
- Wrong deeds are simply erased from the record
- Good deeds are counted twice
- People forget what they did
R “If he does this, the evil deed that he has done becomes a factor that leads him to goodness.”
@ right-starting-point
Q The chiefs of Makkah offered to make the Prophet their ruler. What does the article learn from his refusal?
* The real work is to change individuals’ thinking and character, not to seek power :: Correct. That is the natural sequence.
- Rulers must come from outside the town
- Power should be taken only by force
- The offer was not generous enough
R “The goal of an Islamic movement is not government or political power. Instead, the real focus… is bringing about change in an individual’s character.”
@ the-quranic-concept-of-history
Q “Man is only the manager of his own person.” What does the article mean by this?
* A person is responsible for his own faith and just dealings, not for controlling the course of events :: Well read. History is in God’s hands.
- A person should take no interest in others
- A person should manage only his money
- A person cannot change himself
R “Man is responsible only for controlling his own self: his principal objectives should be to remain on monotheism and see to it that he is just in all his dealings.”
@ knowing-the-known
Q Gravity existed long before Newton described it. What human weakness does this example illustrate?
* We use countless blessings without trying to understand them or acknowledge their Giver :: Yes. Taking things for granted is a kind of ignorance.
- We forget the names of scientists
- We are afraid of new discoveries
- We trust books more than experience
R “Man has a habit of taking things for granted… They avail of all this natural bounty without acknowledging their Giver.”
@ prayer-for-good-health
Q What does the article say makes a supplication (dua) true?
* It rises from the depths of the heart, out of realization of God :: Right. Not mere repetition of memorized words.
- It is said in a loud voice
- It is repeated a fixed number of times
- It is made only in times of illness
R “The true dua is one that is based in and emerges from marifat or God-realization… not simply something that is repeated mechanically on the tongue.”
@ eid-that-brings-people-together
Q Fasting in Ramazan symbolises a responsible life in this world. What does Eid-ul-Fitr symbolise?
* The reward God will give in the Hereafter for a life lived responsibly :: Yes. And, shared with neighbours, it becomes a festival of humankind.
- The end of all duties for the year
- The beginning of the harvest
- A victory over other communities
R “Eid-ul-Fitr denotes the reward that will be given by God Almighty in the Hereafter in return for our good deeds.”
