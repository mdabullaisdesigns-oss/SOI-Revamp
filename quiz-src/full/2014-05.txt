## 2014-05
@ ready-to-respond-every-moment
Q The editorial advises using every event as a “point of reference.” What does this mean?
* Turning each experience into an occasion to remember God and pray – for instance, a hurt into a prayer to be spared hurt in the eternal life :: Yes. In this way one receives divine blessings every moment.
- Keeping a written record of each day
- Comparing one’s life with that of others
- Asking advice before every decision
R “When you undergo a hurtful experience in life, you can make it a point of reference and pray to God to save you from the hurtful experience of eternal life.”
@ ready-to-respond-every-moment
Q In what way does the editorial liken the mind to a mobile phone?
* It must be recharged again and again, by constant reflection, to stay aware of God :: Right. Otherwise distraction takes over.
- It can hold only a limited amount
- It should be switched off at night
- It works best when connected to others
R “A mobile phone becomes inactive when its battery gets exhausted and so we need to recharge it. The same is also true for our mind.”
@ the-secret-of-unity
Q Muslims stand united in congregational prayer, yet often lack unity outside the mosque. How does the article explain this?
* Unity in ritual meets no differences; real unity means staying together in spite of differences :: Exactly. That needs conscious tolerance.
- Prayer is led by one imam; society has many leaders
- People forget prayer once they leave
- Unity is possible only in small groups
R “Real unity can only be established when differences are tolerated. Remaining united despite differences is the formula for true unity.”
@ the-veil-of-causation
Q Every event appears to have a natural cause. What, says the article, is the test this sets for man?
* To see the hand of God behind the cause, which is only a veil :: Yes. This discovery is what the article calls faith.
- To find a cause for every event
- To deny that causes exist
- To control events through science
R “Man needs to develop his consciousness so that he can see the hand of God behind every causal event. He should discover that in these events the cause is just a veil and not the reality.”
@ starting-from-scratch
Q A successful tailor said he had reached his position “by climbing a ladder and not by coming up to it in a lift.” What did he mean?
* Progress comes step by step through long learning and hard work; there is no button to press :: Correct. The article applies this to nation-building too.
- He had refused all help from others
- He distrusted modern machines
- He had begun life in a tall building
R “There are no buttons which you can just push and automatically reach your goals. You can only make progress step by step.”
@ fate-of-the-truth-teller
Q An Eskimo who described New York to his village was named “the Liar.” What comparison does the article make?
* The prophets, who told of an unseen world, were likewise called madmen and liars :: Well read. People could not picture what they had never seen.
- Travellers always exaggerate
- Simple people are easily deceived
- City life corrupts those who visit it
R “Just as the Eskimo was made to see a hitherto unknown world, so God gave the prophets a glimpse of the world that lies beyond death… people dubbed them madmen and liars.”
@ abasement-and-destitution
Q The article says a community can feel abased while having numbers and resources. What causes this feeling?
* False pride in a superior status and past glory, which makes whatever it has seem too little :: Yes. The condition is psychological, not actual.
- Real poverty hidden by statistics
- Unfair treatment by other nations
- A lack of able leaders
R “Muslims consider whatever they have got, to be less than what they imagine they should have, because of their false pride.”
@ the-discovery-of-our-helplessness
Q Why does the article call the discovery of one’s own helplessness “the greatest discovery”?
* It turns a person to God in the kind of prayer that is sure to be answered :: Right. All power is with God, all need with man.
- It frees him from having to work
- It teaches him to depend on other people
- It shows that life has no purpose
R “This discovery gives him great prospects for remembering and beseeching his Lord… God’s mercy reaches him in return faster than the speed of light.”
@ the-second-forbidden-tree
Q What does the article name as the “forbidden tree” of this world?
* Violence :: Yes. To kill one person is like killing all mankind (Quran 5:32).
- Wealth
- Travel
- Argument
R “There is also a forbidden tree in this world and that is violence.” Habil’s words are given as the model: “Even if you raise your hand to kill me, I will not raise my hand to kill you.”
@ the-second-forbidden-tree
Q The article speaks of “passive violence.” What is it, and how does it rank beside active violence?
* Regarding others as oppressors and hating them – a sin of the same magnitude :: Well read. The difference is only outward.
- Refusing to defend oneself – a lesser fault
- Watching violence without helping – no fault
- Speaking harshly – a virtue when deserved
R “Passive violence is that wherein you regard others as oppressors and then show hatred towards them… Both these types of violence are sins of the same magnitude.”
