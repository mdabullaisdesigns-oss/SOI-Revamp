## 2017-12
@ intellectual-freedom-in-islam
Q What, according to the article, happens in a society that curbs freedom of expression?
* People go on thinking but hide their thoughts, so the society fills with hypocrisy and creativity dries up :: Yes. Thinking cannot be stopped; only honesty is.
- People stop thinking altogether
- People become more united
- People turn to study instead
R “If curbs are placed on freedom of expression, people’s thinking cannot be stopped, the only thing that will take place is that their thinking will not come to their lips and pens.”
@ intellectual-freedom-in-islam
Q “May God bless the man who sends me the gift of my own shortcoming.” What quality of a believer do these words of Umar express?
* Eagerness to have his errors pointed out, so that he may correct them :: Right. Accepting truth at the cost of prestige is called the greatest worship.
- Patience with those who flatter him
- A wish to find fault with others
- Indifference to what people say
R “The highest point of this attribute is that man is always prepared, rather he eagerly waits for someone to point out to him any shortcoming of his.”
@ monotheism-oneness-of-god
Q How does belief in the oneness of God shape a person’s character, according to the article?
* It makes him humble before God, yet gives him dignity, confidence and respect for all as equals :: Well read. Submission and freedom together.
- It makes him withdraw from other people
- It makes him proud of his knowledge
- It makes him anxious about the future
R “Belief in Tawheed makes a person humble and modest… Submission to one God results in individual freedom and dignity, for, all human beings become equal.”
@ avoidance-of-conflict
Q Differences between people are inevitable. What does the article ask of someone who has a goal in life?
* Not to let differences grow into conflict, and to stay focused on the work :: Correct. Friction wastes the effort the goal needs.
- To remove everyone who differs
- To give up the goal when opposed
- To settle every difference by debate first
R “While carrying out of his goals, he should not allow differences to escalate into conflict.”
@ trials-and-tribulations-in-life
Q What does the article say is special about a believer’s prayer in a time of trial?
* It comes in new, heartfelt words he had not known before, born of discovering his helplessness :: Yes. Not formal, oft-repeated phrases.
- It is longer than at other times
- It is said together with many people
- It follows a fixed wording exactly
R “It is not the formal duas and oft-repeated recitations that appear on his tongue, rather these are original and new supplications.”
@ freedom-of-expression-and-dissent-in-islam
Q Caliph Umar announced a limit on dowers, and an old woman objected, citing the Quran. How did he respond?
* He accepted her correction openly :: Right. “A woman has quarreled with Umar and has bested him.”
- He ordered her to be silent
- He promised to consider it later
- He asked the scholars to answer her
R According to another account, Umar said: “May God forgive me, everyone knows better than Umar, even this old lady.”
@ prayer-from-the-heart
Q Why does the article call prayer “the ultimate degree of realism” rather than wishful thinking?
* It acknowledges things as they are: man’s complete dependence and God’s absolute power :: Well reasoned. Prayer puts us in our true place.
- Because prayers are always answered at once
- Because it follows fixed timings
- Because it is practised by many people
R “Prayer, then, is not the wishful thinking or illusions of the pious but is actually acceptance of things as they are.”
@ no-half-measures
Q A writer who studied great men found two qualities common to them all. Which?
* Curiosity and discontent – never satisfied with second best :: Yes. These kept them striving higher.
- Wealth and good connections
- Caution and contentment
- Physical strength and good health
R “All of the men he had studied had been filled with curiosity and discontent.”
@ cleanliness-in-islam
Q The article says ablution (wudu) before each of the five daily prayers amounts to what?
* A half-bath five times a day – showing how central cleanliness is to faith :: Correct. “Keeping oneself clean is one half of faith.”
- A symbolic act with no cleansing value
- A practice meant only for Fridays
- A custom for travellers
R “As a cleansing process, wudu is the equivalent of a half-bath. In this way, the devout Muslim takes a half-bath five times daily.”
@ universality-of-the-quran
Q At the end of prayer the worshipper turns right and left saying “Peace and blessings of God be upon you.” What does the article see in this?
* A daily lesson in wishing well to the whole world :: Exactly. God is “Lord of the worlds,” not of one people.
- A greeting meant only for those praying alongside
- A sign that the prayer was too long
- A farewell to the mosque
R “One of the aims of salath (prayers) is to instill in believers, feelings of well-wishing for the whole world.”
