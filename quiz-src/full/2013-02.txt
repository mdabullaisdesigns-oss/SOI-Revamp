## 2013-02
@ from-the-chief-editors-desk
Q The editorial recalls Swami Vivekananda’s reply to a lady in Chicago who judged him by his clothes. What was his point?
* It is character, not tailoring, that makes a gentleman :: Yes. “A gentleman is, as a gentleman does.”
- Indian clothes are finer than Western ones
- Monks should not be judged at all
- Americans care too little about religion
R He said: “In your country the tailor makes a man a gentleman; but in my country, character makes a man a gentleman.”
@ from-the-chief-editors-desk
Q Why, according to the editorial, do crimes flourish in a rights-conscious society and stay in check in a duty-conscious one?
* A rights-conscious person knows only his own needs; a duty-conscious person thinks of the needs of society :: Exactly. Laws alone – “paper activism” – cannot do this.
- A rights-conscious society has too few laws
- Duty-conscious people fear punishment more
- Rights-conscious societies are poorer
R “A rights-conscious person only knows his/her own needs, whereas a duty-conscious person thinks about the needs of society.”
@ religion-and-scientific-reasoning
Q Light, gravity and magnetism cannot be seen directly; they are known by their effects. What does the article conclude from this about religious truths?
* Inference is as sound as direct observation, so reasoning from design to a Designer is scientific :: Well reasoned. The same method is used in science itself.
- Religious truths need no reasoning at all
- Science has nothing to say about unseen things
- Only what a microscope or telescope shows is real
R “Man was compelled to accept that indirect, or inferential argument, was academically and intellectually as sound as direct argument. This change… has, in the present age, made divine reasoning truly scientific.”
@ silence-is-golden
Q What, according to the article, happens when a person observes silence?
* He stops conversing with the outer world and begins conversing with his inner world :: Right. Man is never silent “for himself.”
- His mind becomes empty and at rest
- He loses touch with reality
- He gives others room to speak
R “Remaining silent as far the external world goes, he starts conversing with his internal world… He eschews superficiality and engages himself with deeper realities.”
@ likeness-of-god
Q How does the article argue from man’s experience of himself to belief in God?
* Man knows will, sight, hearing and an “I” in himself, so he can conceive a Being who has these to a perfect degree :: Yes. Believing in God is likened to believing in one’s own self.
- Man is weak, so he needs to imagine a protector
- Man is part of God and shares His powers
- Man cannot know himself, so he cannot deny God
R “Man’s experience of himself, his attributes, his characteristics, make it possible for him to apprehend the eternal Being who possesses these very attributes… to a superlative degree.”
@ present-world-and-the-next
Q The article speaks of a farmer who wants to reap on the very day he sows. What does this picture warn against?
* Seeking in this world the rewards that belong to the next, and so losing both :: Correct. Today is for action; tomorrow is for the harvest.
- Working without rest
- Trusting the weather instead of God
- Saving too much for the future
R “The world of today is a place for action, and the world of tomorrow is the place for reaping the harvest… Man desires to achieve in the present world, what is to be achieved in tomorrow’s world. This is the reason he loses both.”
@ the-human-potential
Q After studying more than a hundred disasters, which word did the Disaster Research Centre find best described how people behave?
* Heroism :: Yes – “heroism, not panic or shock.”
- Panic
- Shock
- Resignation
R The report said human beings are “amazingly controlled and resilient in the face of adversity. Perhaps heroism, not panic or shock, is the right word.” The article asks us not to waste time lamenting losses.
@ is-islam-against-democracy
Q Before the battle of Uhud the Prophet preferred to stay and defend Madinah, while most companions wished to go out. What did he do, and what does the article take from it?
* He went with the majority – showing consultation and majority opinion at work in Islam :: Well done. The Quran says believers’ affairs are decided by mutual consultation (42:38).
- He kept to his own view, showing a leader must decide alone
- He delayed the decision until revelation came
- He let each companion act as he wished
R “Sometimes the Prophet accepted the opinion of the majority, even though it was against his personal opinion. One such example is found at the time of the battle of Uhud.”
@ consistent-character
Q Steel can be trusted to hold up a bridge because its properties never change. What parallel does the article draw for human society?
* Society is stable only when people show a consistent, reliable character :: Exactly. A man’s worth lies in keeping his character under all conditions.
- Society needs strong rulers as a bridge needs steel
- People, like materials, cannot change their nature
- Modern society depends mainly on engineering
R “If people do not show consistency, reliability and predictability in their character, human society will crumble.”
@ a-refusal-to-suffer
Q Thelma Thompson came to love the Mojave desert she had wanted to leave. What had changed?
* Only her own attitude – she chose to see “the stars” rather than “the mud” :: Yes. Nothing outside her had changed.
- The climate became milder
- Her husband was posted elsewhere
- The local people learned English
R “Neither the desert, nor the natives, nor any other element in the situation had changed. Whatever change had taken place was inside the mind and heart of Thelma Thompson.”
