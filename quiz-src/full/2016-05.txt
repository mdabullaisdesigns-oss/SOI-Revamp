## 2016-05
@ my-motherland
Q Asked whether it is lawful to call one’s country “motherland,” how did the author answer?
* It is a matter of culture and sentiment, not of religious law; love of one’s homeland is natural :: Yes. Making it a legal question is a form of extremism.
- It is forbidden
- It is a religious duty
- Only scholars may use the word
R “It must be regarded as an issue of culture and not of the law… Love for one’s nation, or patriotism is a natural fact.”
@ my-motherland
Q The Prophet once answered a questioner, “Seek the fatwa from your heart.” What does the editorial take this to mean?
* Matters not addressed in the Quran and Hadith are to be settled by one’s own sincere conscience :: Right. With a pure intention.
- Scholars’ opinions are never needed
- The heart is wiser than revelation
- Questions should not be asked
R “Matters not mentioned directly in the Quran and Hadith should be decided through the dictates of one’s own nature or conscience.”
@ happiness
Q Why, according to the article, does happiness always seem to lie in the future?
* Man seeks perfection but lives in an imperfect world; complete happiness belongs to Paradise :: Yes. Finding joy in small things does not satisfy his nature.
- People do not work hard enough
- Happiness is only an idea
- The past was happier than the present
R “Man by nature is a perfectionist, born in an imperfect world. It is this contradiction that creates problems.”
@ zakat-the-prescribed-charity
Q Zakat is described as “an annual reminder.” Of what?
* That everything a person has belongs to God and others have a rightful share in it :: Correct. A selfish stance is a wrong stance.
- That the year has ended
- That taxes are due to the state
- That wealth should be hidden
R “Zakat is an annual reminder to man that everything he possesses belongs to God, and that nothing should, therefore, be withheld from Him.”
@ spiritual-development
Q On a very hot day the electricity returned and the fan began to turn. What did this bring to the author’s mind?
* The believer’s passing from the hardship of this world to the joy of Paradise :: Well read. A material event became a spiritual one.
- The importance of electricity
- The discomforts of city life
- The cleverness of inventors
R “The period of worldly suffering will instantly end and the joyous period of Paradise shall begin… The material incident was converted into a spiritual one.”
@ fasting-as-a-spiritual-culture
Q The Arabic word for fasting, sawm, means abstinence. How does the article connect this with a “man of mission”?
* One devoted to a mission gives up all that distracts from it; fasting is training in that spirit :: Yes. Simple living, high thinking.
- A man of mission never eats in public
- Abstinence is the mission itself
- Missions should last only a month
R “When one involves himself in a mission, he abstains from all diversions… Abstaining from food and water for a limited period is symbolic training.”
@ between-two-options
Q Many people feel their abilities were never appreciated. What two options does the article say they have?
* To work within what others have built, or to create one’s own world by one’s own effort :: Right. There is no third option.
- To complain, or to remain silent
- To wait, or to move abroad
- To seek praise, or to give up
R “One is to accept an establishment made by someone else. The other is to create one’s own world… In this world, no one else can do your work.”
@ patience-a-matter-of-wisdom

- “Why has this happened to me?”
- “I shall win it all back”

@ undiscovered-reality
Q Why does the article call death “an undiscovered reality,” though everyone knows of it?
* People treat it as news about others and never apply it to themselves :: Exactly. They live only in the present.
- No one has returned to describe it
- Doctors cannot define it
- It happens too rarely to notice
R “People see or hear of this regularly, but everyone takes it as external news. No one applies it personally.”
@ the-return-to-religion
Q A scientist is quoted as saying, “We know more and more about less and less.” What point does the article draw from this?
* Science gives only partial knowledge of reality and cannot by itself reach the ultimate truth :: Well reasoned. Hence renewed interest in religion.
- Science has stopped making progress
- Scientists know less than their forebears
- Specialisation should be banned
R “It is now an accepted fact among the scientific community that science gives us but a partial knowledge of reality.”
