## 2018-02
@ in-singapore-the-enraged-go-to-the-fragment-room
Q The editorial describes “rage rooms” where people smash things to relieve stress. What does it recommend instead when one is angry?
* Stay completely silent and carry on with positive work, so the energy of anger feeds creativity :: Yes. Anger is “a great natural gift” to be used wisely.
- Shout in a place where no one can hear
- Take a long holiday at once
- Tell everyone how you feel
R “All you need to do when you get angry is to keep mum. Your total silence will automatically initiate a natural process within you and the released ‘anger energy’ will be diverted to a positive target.”
@ the-culture-of-spirituality
Q Milk does not exist ready-made in a cow; it is produced by an inner process. What point about spirituality does this make?
* It is not found ready-made outside – a prepared, thinking mind must produce it from its experiences :: Right. “A product of your own factory.”
- It can be bought from an expert
- It is found only in beautiful places
- It comes without any effort
R “Spirituality is not a ready-made thing. It is a product of an intellectual process… Rather you must develop your own mind and soul.”
@ when-things-lose-their-novelty
Q The umbrella and the hand pump once caused amazement and are now ordinary. What lesson does the article draw?
* Familiarity dulls wonder – we should look at God’s creation as if seeing it for the first time :: Well read. Like one cured of blindness.
- New inventions are soon replaced
- Old things were better made
- People were once easily fooled
R “We should not lose our sense of awe and gratitude simply because familiarity has taken the edge off our awareness.”
@ understanding-the-quran
Q Ali was asked whether he had any inspiration beyond the Quran. What did his answer show?
* Reading the words is one thing; grasping the deeper meaning needs reflection and God-given understanding :: Yes. The Quran is an ocean to be explored in depth.
- That he possessed secret writings
- That only Arabic speakers can benefit from the Quran
- That the Quran need only be recited
R “To understand the words of the Quran all one has to do is read it. But true understanding of Quranic meanings can only come from deep thought and contemplation.”
@ constant-choices
Q Which experiences does the article call the real “test papers” of life?
* Situations demanding a moral choice, especially dilemmas where the right course is hard to see :: Correct. Routine matters test nothing.
- School and college examinations
- Routine daily tasks
- Contests with other people
R “Some other types of experience… place us in the dilemma of not being able to tell which the right course is… It is such experiences which are the real ‘test papers’ in life.”
@ constant-choices
Q If a person finds himself in rank and affluence, what does the article say he must guard against?
* Arrogance, pride and miserliness :: Right. As poverty calls for patience and gratitude.
- Impatience and despair
- Idleness and boredom
- Too much generosity
R “One may… find oneself in a position of rank and affluence, in which case it is arrogance, overweening pride and miserliness which have to be guarded against.”
@ an-exemplary-tale
Q Mr Khan was appointed almost without an interview because his former professor already knew his work. What moral does the article draw?
* Make yourself genuinely useful and able, and opportunities will seek you out :: Well reasoned. The world runs on give and take.
- Good connections matter more than merit
- Interviews serve no purpose
- Private colleges choose carelessly
R “If you want employment, you must make yourself useful… Then you will see that you don’t need to pursue employment instead employment will pursue you.”
@ mans-quest
Q Man longs for a perfect world but lives in an imperfect, passing one. What does the article reason from this?
* Another, perfect and lasting world must follow; this one is a place of trial :: Yes. So one should not try to build heaven on earth.
- Man’s longing is a mere illusion
- Perfection can be reached here with effort
- The world was once perfect and has declined
R “This world must be inconclusive. Another world must follow to compensate for the inadequacies of the present one.”
@ political-power-not-an-objective
Q When armed tribes reached the borders of Madinah at the time of Al-Ahzab, what did the Prophet and his Companions do?
* Dug a trench, which prevented a battle from taking place :: Right. An example of averting war.
- Marched out to meet them in the open
- Left the city by night
- Sent for help from distant allies
R “The Prophet and his Companions dug a deep trench between them, thus successfully preventing a battle from taking place.”
@ wisdom
Q “Be an idealist in your own home, and as soon as you step out of your home, become practical.” What does this mean?
* Expect the highest of yourself, but only what is realistically possible of others :: Exactly. Doing the opposite leads to failure.
- Be strict with family and easy with strangers
- Dream at home and work outside
- Keep your ideals private
R “We need to be realist when it comes to others and idealist when it comes to our own selves.”
