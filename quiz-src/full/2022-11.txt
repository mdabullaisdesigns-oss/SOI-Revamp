@ realize-your-potential
Q The editorial opens with Helen Keller, blind and deaf from early childhood. According to her words, how is character developed?
* Not in ease and quiet, but through the experience of trial and suffering :: Yes. Trial strengthens the soul.
- Through comfort and long rest
- Through wealth and good connections
- Through avoiding every difficulty
R “Character cannot be developed in ease and quiet. Only through experience of trial and suffering can the soul be strengthened, ambition inspired, and success achieved.”

- Treat others as they have treated you

@ disadvantage-turned-to-advantage
Q Gandhi’s shyness once left him unable to speak in court. What lasting benefit did he say it brought him?
* It taught him economy of words, so that a thoughtless word hardly ever escaped him :: Right. A weakness became a strength.
- It kept him away from public life
- It made him a faster writer
- It won him the sympathy of judges
R “Its greatest benefit has been that it has taught me the economy of words… a thoughtless word hardly ever escaped my tongue or pen.”

@ the-word-of-god
Q The chapter speaks of those “who are uncharitable even over very small things.” How does the commentary describe such a person?
* He will not give even what costs him nothing substantial – a matchstick or his good wishes :: Exactly. Belief in the reckoning is what makes one considerate.
- He gives only to his relatives
- He gives large sums but in secret
- He gives only when asked twice
R “He will not even give to others such things as will cause him no substantial loss—even if it be only matchsticks or his good wishes.”
