## 2015-07
@ dates-on-a-calendar
Q The editorial says each day brings opportunities that “do not loudly announce themselves.” What must one keep in mind while using them?
* Others have interests too, so one advances by cooperation and by avoiding clashes :: Yes. Success never comes through conflict.
- Opportunities belong to whoever takes them first
- It is safer to let them pass
- Only large opportunities are worth taking
R “Success in your life can happen only by cooperating with others, never through conflict… A smart person carefully avoids clashing with other people’s interests.”
@ finally-in-the-grave
Q “No matter how big a palace you live in, you can occupy just one room at a time.” What does the article conclude from such remarks?
* Take from the world only what is needed and make the Hereafter one’s aim :: Right. Nothing gathered here is taken along.
- Build smaller houses
- Share one’s palace with others
- Wealth should be passed to one’s children early
R “Wise is the one who takes from the things of the world only that which is necessary and makes the Hereafter the focus of his efforts and hopes.”
@ losing-the-opportunity
Q What principle does the article say should replace “my community, right or wrong”?
* Judging the community’s conduct by the teachings of the Quran, not defending whatever it does :: Yes. Islam and Muslims are to be distinguished.
- Judging other communities more strictly
- Refusing to discuss the community at all
- Leaving judgement to political leaders
R “Islam needs to be understood according to the Quranic teachings and the Muslim community should be judged in this light rather than by the principle—‘my community, right or wrong’.”
@ peace-based-empire
Q What does the article observe about violence in the world of nature, and what does it infer?
* Nature works by cooperation and harmony; deliberate violence is found only among humans and brings only destruction :: Well read. “Violence can destroy, but not build.”
- Nature is violent, so violence is natural to man
- Nature is indifferent, so nothing can be learnt from it
- Nature rewards the aggressive
R “Violence occurs only in the human world, not in the world of nature… only the peaceful method which nature abides by produces positive results.”
@ ramazan-and-warfare
Q A Companion explained taqwa by asking how one walks along a path lined with thorny bushes. What was the answer?
* One gathers one’s clothes close and goes carefully on one’s way :: Yes. Taqwa is this cautious attitude.
- One cuts the bushes down
- One turns back
- One runs through as fast as possible
R “‘I gathered my clothes close to me and went on my way’. Ubayy ibn Kaab said, ‘It is this cautious attitude which is called taqwa.’”
@ ramazan-and-warfare
Q With what words did the Prophet greet the new moon of Ramazan?
* A prayer that it come “with peace and security” :: Correct. The article calls Ramazan the month of patience and peace.
- A call to prepare for battle
- A warning of hardship to come
- An order to store food
R “O God, let the moon of the month of Ramazan appear on us with peace and security.”
@ airport-of-the-hereafter
Q In the article’s image, what awaits passengers at “the airport of the Hereafter”?
* Some will be stopped for questioning, others welcomed with greetings of peace :: Yes. And the time of landing is not announced.
- All will be sent back to begin again
- All will pass through without any check
- Only the first to arrive will be admitted
R “Some will be stopped for questioning while others will be welcomed and led into Paradise… The time will come without any announcement.”
@ acceptance-of-supplications
Q A young man complained that his prayers for his problems went unanswered. How does the article answer him?
* Worldly conditions are like an examination paper set by God; one keeps asking and leaves the choice to Him :: Right. He knows what is truly good for us.
- He had not prayed often enough
- Prayers for worldly needs are forbidden
- His problems were a punishment
R “The ideal prayer would be ‘O God! Please grant me whatever is best for me!’ and then to willingly accept whatever is given.”
@ lailatul-qadr-the-night-of-destiny
Q Which prayer did the Prophet teach Aisha for the Night of Destiny?
* “O God, You are the One who forgives and You love to forgive, so forgive me” :: Yes. Seeking forgiveness shows one’s smallness before God.
- “O God, grant me wealth and long life”
- “O God, give me victory over my enemies”
- “O God, make this night last”
R “O God! Certainly You are the One who grants forgiveness for sins. You love to forgive, so forgive me.”
@ lailatul-qadr-the-night-of-destiny
Q The Prophet came out to tell people which night it was, saw two men quarrelling, and the knowledge was taken from him. What lesson does the article draw?
* Conflict, even in words, shuts out divine blessings; they come to a positive mind :: Well reasoned. Free of hatred and revenge.
- Good news should be kept secret
- Quarrels are best settled in public
- The night is the same every year
R “Even fighting with words is enough to have the divine knowledge taken away… Positivity is the most important trait that makes a man worthy of receiving God’s blessings.”
