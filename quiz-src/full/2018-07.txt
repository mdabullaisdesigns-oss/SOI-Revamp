## 2018-07
@ the-prophet-of-wisdom
Q What definition of wisdom does the editorial give?
* The ability to discover the relevant by sorting out the irrelevant :: Yes. Clarity of thought follows from it.
- The possession of much information
- The ability to speak well
- The experience that comes with age
R “Wisdom is the ability to discover the relevant by sorting out the irrelevant.”
@ the-prophet-of-wisdom
Q In early Makkah the Kabah was in the hands of those who opposed the Prophet’s message. How does the editorial describe his response?
* He did not react; he saw the people gathered there as an audience and used the opportunity to convey his message :: Well read. Avoid the unwanted; plan on what is available.
- He stayed away from the Kabah altogether
- He demanded control of it first
- He waited until the gatherings ended
R “The Prophet of Islam never reacted to this situation. He studied the situation and found that this assembly was a potential audience for his message.”
@ spiritual-role-of-women
Q When the Prophet returned shaken from the cave of Hira, how did Khadija help him?
* She reassured him that no harm would come to one so truthful and kind to others :: Right. Gentleness and understanding calmed him.
- She sent for the elders of the town
- She advised him to tell no one
- She asked him to return to the cave
R “She did her best to assure him that no harm would come to him as he always spoke the truth, helped the poor and those in distress, and invariably treated others with respect.”
@ spiritual-role-of-women
Q The emperor Akbar, insulted in court by his teacher, wished to punish him. What did his mother, Maryam Makani, tell him?
* That history would remember an emperor with all power who forgave :: Yes. She calmed him and the man was spared.
- That the teacher should be sent away
- That an emperor must never be insulted
- That the court should decide
R “She told him that his pardon would go down in history; that history would remember that ‘an emperor, having all the power at his disposal, forgave an offender’.”
@ destiny-and-freewill
Q You choose to walk, yet you need the earth, gravity and air to do so. What does this example show about freedom and destiny?
* Man is free in his will, but can act only within an “infrastructure” set by the Creator :: Well reasoned. A balance between the two.
- Man has no freedom at all
- Man is free of every limit
- Freedom belongs only to the strong
R “In terms of using his will, he is completely free. But he is helpless in the sense that he cannot use his free will without the infrastructure that has been established by the Creator.”
@ no-acknowledgement-of-god
Q When the Prophet Solomon received a blessing, what did he say?
* “This is by the grace of my Lord” :: Correct. Gratitude is acknowledging the real Giver.
- “This is the fruit of my effort”
- “This is my inheritance”
- “This is the gift of my people”
R “When the Prophet Solomon received a worldly blessing he immediately said: Haza min fazli rabbi, which means, ‘This is by the grace of my Lord.’ (27: 40)”
@ happiness-only-in-the-hereafter
Q The article recalls Charlie Chaplin, who made the world laugh yet was unhappy within. What does it conclude about this world?
* It is made for action; lasting happiness belongs to the Hereafter :: Yes. Here one can only work towards it.
- Happiness depends on fame
- Comedians are sadder than other people
- Happiness comes from laughing more
R “The Creator has created the present world for action. Here only action to achieve happiness is possible and not happiness per se.”
@ the-rational-basis-of-rituals
Q Ritual without spirit is “like a nutshell without a kernel.” What value does the article still see in it?
* It keeps religion in mind and serves as a regular reminder of the spirit :: Right. Form symbolises the inner content.
- None at all
- It is equal to ritual with spirit
- It replaces the need for spirit
R “This minimum role of rituals can be described as a reminder of spirit through routine.”
@ after-death
Q Man alone has a sense of “tomorrow” and dies with ambitions unfulfilled. What does the article infer from this?
* That another world must follow in which human hopes, and justice, find fulfilment :: Well read. Otherwise man alone would be left incomplete.
- That man should have fewer ambitions
- That animals are happier than man
- That life should be made longer
R “This indicates that there is a world that will come after this present one, a world where human beings will find complete fulfilment of their wishes and hopes.”
@ the-word-of-god
Q Why, according to the commentary, was belief declared only after seeing the punishment of no avail?
* Acceptance of truth has value when reached through reason, not when forced by what is seen :: Exactly. The test is to recognise truth by its arguments.
- Because it was spoken too softly
- Because too many people declared it together
- Because it came from the wealthy
R “Only that admission of truth is desirable and useful which is arrived at on the basis of arguments.”
