## 2014-10
@ hajj-fifth-pillar-of-islam
Q What, according to the article, are the rites of Hajj – the circuits, the run between Safa and Marwah, the sacrifice?
* A symbolic re-enactment of stages in the life of Prophet Abraham and his family :: Yes. “It is a tradition of your father, Abraham.”
- Customs taken over from the traders of Makkah
- Exercises to prepare pilgrims for travel
- Rules laid down by later scholars
R “The rituals of Hajj are a symbolic re-enactment of the various stages of the life of the Prophet Abraham… so that the believer repeats the lessons from this history in his own life.”
@ hajj-fifth-pillar-of-islam
Q Besides memorising prayers and rules, what does the article say will make a pilgrim’s Hajj “a living Hajj”?
* Knowing the history that runs from Hajira and Ishmael to the Prophet and his Companions :: Right. Then the rites become a deep spiritual experience.
- Performing Hajj several times
- Travelling with a large group
- Staying longer in Makkah
R “If pilgrims read this history, they will know the historical background of the worship they perform. In this way, their Hajj will become a living Hajj.”
@ the-greatness-of-god
Q Astronomers have found a black hole heavy enough to swallow the solar system. How does the article say a thoughtful person should receive such discoveries?
* As a means of realising God’s greatness and his own helplessness :: Yes. Science has “enlarged the canvas of God realization.”
- As a reason to fear the future
- As matters for specialists only
- As proof that the universe has no order
R “These facts remind man of his state of extreme helplessness and the state of Omnipotence of God.”
@ the-moment-of-truth
Q Who are the true believers, in the article’s description?
* Those who bow to truth on the strength of argument, while nothing compels them :: Correct. In the Hereafter, belief will no longer be a choice.
- Those who believe once they see God’s power
- Those who are born into a believing family
- Those who never ask for reasons
R “The true believers are those who believe in God on the basis of argument, who bow to truth while there is no other incentive at work.”
@ the-islamic-state-of-is-is-not-an-islamic-state
Q How, according to the article, did Abu Bakr become head of the community after the Prophet’s death?
* Through open consultation and the consensus of the Companions :: Yes. No one may proclaim himself ruler.
- By declaring himself caliph
- By inheriting the position
- By the support of the army
R “Open consultation took place between the Companions. Through the consensus of the Companions, Abu Bakr al-Siddiq became the head of the state of Madinah.”
@ divine-food
Q The article speaks of “extracting” spiritual food from material things, with a glass of water as the example. What does it mean?
* Reflecting on how fitly a thing serves us until it leads to appreciation of God’s wisdom :: Well reasoned. “Man cannot live by bread alone.”
- Blessing food and drink before taking them
- Eating and drinking very little
- Studying the chemistry of what we consume
R “You have to develop your mind to such an extent that you are able to extract the spiritual content from material things.”
@ hajj-lifes-fulfilment
Q Which lesson of Hajj does the article single out when it speaks of people of many languages and cultures performing the rites together?
* Living together as one community in spite of differences, and a sense of human equality :: Right. It is “a complete course of life’s training.”
- That one language should replace the others
- That travel broadens the mind
- That the pilgrimage should be made easier
R “Hajj is a great training course which, despite differences and disagreements, teaches us to live together as one community.”
@ a-common-human-weakness
Q Why, says the article, are people so ready to believe ill of those who have risen to prominence?
* Because of jealousy – a reluctance to accept another’s superiority :: Yes. This was Satan’s fault towards Adam.
- Because prominent people are usually guilty
- Because the truth is hard to find out
- Because people love justice
R “Man’s greatest weakness is his reluctance to accept the superiority of another.”
@ a-matter-of-wisdom
Q What lesson does the article draw from the story of the Prophet Ayyub (Job)?
* No loss in this world is final; with patience and hope in God one may recover and gain more :: Exactly. His family and wealth were restored twice over.
- Wealth is a danger to faith
- Suffering is always a punishment
- One should not own many possessions
R “God… has devised the system of this world in such a way that here no loss or deprival is final.”
@ peace-and-justice
Q “If you put the cart before the horse, you will never reach your destination.” In the article, which is the horse and which the cart?
* Peace comes first; justice and rights are worked for through the opportunities peace opens :: Yes. This is presented as a law of nature.
- Justice comes first; peace follows from it
- Rights come first; duties follow
- Wishes come first; plans follow
R “One must try to establish peace at any cost… Peace will then open the doors of opportunities. And, by availing these opportunities, one should work towards achieving justice.”
