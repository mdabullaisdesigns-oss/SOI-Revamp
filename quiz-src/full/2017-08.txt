## 2017-08
@ welcoming-the-71st-independence-day
Q The editorial holds up Charles De Gaulle as an example. What lesson does it draw from him?
* A nation that missed its first chance can re-plan and succeed, if its leaders accept the personal cost :: Yes. He lost popularity, but France grew strong.
- A leader should guard his popularity above all
- A nation should never change its first plan
- Military strength alone makes a nation great
R “In life it often happens that individuals or nations are unable to avail of the first chance. This is why they have to plan again for a second time.”
@ purification-tazkiya
Q Every person is born on an upright nature but is shaped by his surroundings. What, then, is tazkiya (purification)?
* De-conditioning oneself – finding and removing what the environment added – to return to one’s true nature :: Right. It is the process of self-correction.
- Withdrawing from society for good
- Learning the rules of ritual cleanliness
- Acquiring new habits from one’s elders
R “Tazkiya or purification is for one to properly understand this problem and to de-condition himself and thereby make himself once again, a person on true nature.”
@ purification-tazkiya
Q Who, according to the article, can carry out this self-correction for a person?
* No one else – it is each person’s own responsibility, through introspection :: Correct. It begins with searching one’s own mind.
- A teacher who knows him well
- His parents
- The community as a whole
R “This task of self-correction or de-conditioning is not something that someone else can do for another. It is each one's own responsibility.”
@ conversion-to-god
Q The sun, the cow and the tree each convert one thing into another. How does the conversion asked of man differ?
* It is conscious and spiritual – turning observation into knowledge of God, and resentment into forgiveness :: Well read. In nature it is instinctive and material.
- It is quicker than in nature
- It needs special equipment
- It happens only in old age

@ working-in-unison
Q Japanese shipbuilders finished a tanker in eight months, against sixteen and twenty-four elsewhere. What did the survey find to be the deciding factor?
* Teamwork – workers and management functioning in complete unison :: Yes. Unity is the key to organisational success.
- Cheaper materials
- Longer working hours
- Newer machinery
R “A survey conducted by Western experts shows that the deciding factor is teamwork.”
@ working-in-unison
Q What price does the article say individuals pay in such a system of joint effort?
* Their own personality has less room to stand out – a sacrifice a successful nation’s members accept :: Well reasoned. Group activity is not “a springboard to individual glory.”
- They earn lower wages
- They lose touch with their families
- They cannot learn new skills
R “The only flaw in this system of joint effort is that the personality of the individual has little opportunity to flourish. But it is only a nation which is made up of individuals who are prepared to make this personal sacrifice which can ever aspire to success.”
@ concealing-others-faults
Q If you come to know of a fault in someone, which two courses does the article approve?
* Keep it to yourself, or speak to the person privately with compassion :: Exactly. “A believer is like a mirror for another believer.”
- Warn others about him, or avoid him
- Announce it publicly, or write to him
- Ignore him, or report him
R “You could keep this fact in your heart and not speak about it to anyone. Or, you could meet this person yourself, and in a spirit of compassion and genuine concern for reform, speak to the person about it.”
@ intellectual-crusades
Q Why does the article say the approach suited to the freedom struggle needed to change after Independence?
* The relationship was now between Indians and Indians, which calls for duty-consciousness rather than demands for rights :: Yes. A better society is a duty-conscious one.
- Because the old leaders had retired
- Because rights no longer mattered to anyone
- Because foreign countries required it
R “In the post-Independence period, the equation was between Indians and Indians. Now it has become essential to change our strategy from being rights-based to being duty-based.”
@ intellectual-crusades
Q At Geneva airport the author spoke to a traveller about “cow culture.” What did he mean?
* Converting negative experiences into positive thinking, as a cow turns non-milk into milk :: Right. She resolved to give up the complaint culture.
- Living close to the land
- Keeping to a simple diet
- Honouring animals in ritual

@ the-word-of-god
Q Neighbouring plots fed by the same water bear fruits of different taste (13:4). What human parallel does the commentary draw?
* The same guidance is offered to all, yet people benefit from it very differently :: Well read. One is enriched; another takes little.
- People of different lands need different guidance
- Good soil is a matter of luck
- Sweet fruit grows only with more water
R “The same ‘seeds’ of guidance when ‘sown’ in different individuals will produce outcomes as different as the sweet and sour fruits of our analogy.”
