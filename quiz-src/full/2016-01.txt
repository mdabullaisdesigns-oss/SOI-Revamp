@ discovery-of-god
Q Hans Bethe was overjoyed to learn why the stars shine. What contrast does the article draw with believers?
* A small discovery thrilled the scientist, yet many who say they have found God show no such joy :: Right. Without that joy, the discovery has not really been made.
- Scientists are happier people than believers
- Believers should avoid looking at the stars
- Discovery matters less than obedience
R “If there are no traces of the joy of discovery, then the discovery itself has yet to be made.”
@ pollution
Q How does the article describe man’s position with regard to the natural world?
* He is a beneficiary, free to use it but not to upset its balance – he cannot create even a leaf :: Yes. Polluting it is called suicidal.
- He is its owner, free to do as he likes
- He is its servant, with no right to use it
- He is separate from it, and unaffected
R “Man is permitted to exploit natural resources but without destroying the natural order of things… Man’s freedom ends where his mismanagement of nature begins.”
@ the-importance-of-reason
Q The article says reason can have a good or a bad outcome. On what does this depend?
* Used with sincerity and modesty it builds a person; joined to ego it makes him arrogant :: Well read. It must also be developed by study and reflection.
- On how much schooling one has had
- On whether one reasons alone or with others
- On the subject one reasons about
R “When used with sincerity and modesty, it serves as a constructive and healthy agent of one’s personality. But if one is a victim of egoism, then reason makes him an arrogant person.”
@ the-life-force
Q A creeper that had been cut down and cemented over broke through the floor and grew again. What does the article see in this?
* The force of life, which persists and reappears just when it seems to have ended :: Correct. It “will claim its right to exist.”
- The poor quality of the cement
- The carelessness of the owner
- The danger of plants near houses
R “Even when uprooted, it continues to exist, albeit dormant… When people conclude that… life must have ended, that is just when it rears its head from the debris.”
@ reading-the-signs
Q A good driver who sees a ball roll into the road stops at once. Why, and what is the wider lesson?
* He foresees the child behind the ball – in life, too, one must read what a sign implies :: Yes. And keep to one’s own “lane.”
- The ball might damage the car – avoid small losses
- The law requires it – obey every rule
- Others are watching – guard your reputation
R “The really good driver stops, not on account of the ball, but on account of the child that he sees with his mind’s eye.”
@ the-price-of-peace
Q What does the article name as the price of peace?
* Tolerating loss :: Right. It takes courage, and leaves one free to rebuild.
- Winning the argument first
- Paying compensation
- Avoiding other people
R “What is the price of peace? Tolerating loss!… To patiently accept and tolerate loss is not defeatism.”
@ speech-management
Q What test does the article suggest before one speaks?
* Whether what one is about to say rests on evidence and will do good; if not, stay silent :: Yes. An allegation is not the same as a supported statement.
- Whether it will impress the listeners
- Whether others have already said it
- Whether it can be said quickly
R “Begin by examining whether what you have to say is supported by logic. If you have good reason to speak, then open your mouth, else remain silent.”
@ success-out-of-failure
Q When Joseph was thrown into the well, what does the Quran say was revealed to him?
* That he would come out to a new life and rise so high his brothers would not know him :: Exactly. His worst moment was about to become his best.
- That his brothers would soon be punished
- That he should stay silent about his dream
- That he would return home the next day
R “God revealed to Joseph that his worst moments were about to become his best. Where antagonists intended to end his life, a whole fresh chapter unfolded.”
@ the-age-of-civilisation
Q Why does the article call the age of civilisation “still incomplete”?
* Man has learnt to turn matter into technology, but not yet to settle differences by peaceful reasoning :: Well reasoned. War is as outdated as striking stones to make fire.
- Many countries are still poor
- Machines often break down
- Not everyone can read
R “Though man learnt to convert matter into technology, he failed to discover the art of managing differences.”
