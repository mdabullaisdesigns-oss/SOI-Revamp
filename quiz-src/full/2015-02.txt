## 2015-02
@ condemnation-of-attack-on-charlie-hebdo-in-paris
Q In condemning the attack in Paris, what does the editorial say the Prophet and his Companions did when they themselves were ridiculed?
* They punished no one and went on sharing their message in a positive way :: Yes. The editorial calls the killings gravely un-Islamic.
- They left the city where they were mocked
- They asked the rulers to silence their critics
- They answered every insult with an insult
R “Even during the Prophet’s time there were several instances of ridicule, however the Prophet and his Companions neither punished such persons nor asked anyone to do so.”
@ between-lesser-evil-and-greater-evil
Q The article distinguishes personal life from life in society. How do the choices differ?
* Personally one may aim at the greatest good; in society the wise choice is often the lesser of two evils :: Right. “The wisdom to know the difference.”
- In society one may demand the ideal; privately one must compromise
- There is no difference between them
- In both, one should refuse anything imperfect
R “In one’s personal life, one can try to opt for the greater good. But, in society one has to be ready to opt for what seems to be the lesser evil.”
@ between-lesser-evil-and-greater-evil
Q What does the article identify as the “greater evil” to be avoided?
* Negative thinking, which paralyses the mind and halts progress :: Yes. Unfavourable conditions are the lesser evil.
- Unfavourable outward circumstances
- Lack of political power
- Being fewer in number
R “Unfavourable situations in terms of the result, are lesser evils. While, negative thinking, in terms of the result, is the greater evil.”
@ intellectual-freedom-in-islam
Q The Caliph Umar said, “May God bless the man who sends me the gift of my own shortcoming.” What attitude does the article commend through this saying?
* Eagerness to have one’s mistakes pointed out and to accept the truth :: Exactly. The article calls accepting the truth the greatest act of worship.
- Patience with people who talk too much
- Generosity in giving presents
- Suspicion of all praise
R “The highest point of this attribute is that man… eagerly awaits for someone to point out to him any shortcoming of his so that he may immediately accept it.”
@ intellectual-freedom-in-islam
Q Why does the article say accepting the truth is so great an act?
* It demands the sacrifice of one’s prestige and sense of greatness :: Well read. And it is possible only where people may speak freely.
- It takes a great deal of study
- It is rewarded with public honour
- It brings an end to all disagreement
R “This is the sacrifice of one’s prestige; of losing one’s greatness. To lose one’s sense of greatness for the sake of truth is an occasion when man earns his entry into heaven.”
@ reaction-to-provocation
Q In the Prophet’s time, how were those who spoke against Islam answered?
* With words – the poet Hassan was asked to reply in verse :: Yes. “Countering words with words.”
- With banishment from the city
- With a fine
- With silence, by order
R “The Prophet appointed a poet called Hassan to give a befitting answer in verse to the offenders, poetry being the main mode of public expression.”
@ the-seeker-finds
Q What does the article conclude about a person who lacks what is truly worth having – fear of God, remembrance, sincere piety?
* He has not really sought it, for God gives to those who ask :: Right. “Those who seek will always find.”
- God has decided to withhold it from him
- It is given only to a chosen few
- It comes only in old age
R “If one does not have what is desirable in life, it means that one has not sought it… He gives only to those who ask.”
@ the-straight-path-for-social-living
Q The Quran speaks of the Arabs being rescued “from the brink of an abyss of Fire.” What does the article take this to mean?
* The endless chain of revenge wars between the tribes :: Yes. Each defeat bred a new war.
- The heat of the desert
- A volcano near Madinah
- Poverty and famine
R “Because of this culture of seeking revenge, the Arab tribes were constantly engaged in a chain of wars. This chain of wars is referred to in the Quran as an ‘abyss of fire.’”
@ waiting-for-a-highway
Q What habit of mind does the article describe with the image of “waiting for a highway”?
* Putting off effort until ideal conditions arrive, and missing the opportunity already at hand :: Correct. “Everyone is waiting for the train, which will never arrive.”
- Travelling too much
- Relying on public transport
- Planning too carefully
R “Don’t wait for the moment; instead every moment try to discover the opportunity that is already present, and avail it with wise planning.”
@ dichotomous-thinking-is-wrong
Q The Kabah in Makkah had become a gathering place for all the tribes. How did the Prophet respond, according to the article?
* He left the problem aside and used the gathering as a chance to convey his message :: Well reasoned. This was the “third option.”
- He stayed away from the Kabah altogether
- He called on his followers to clear it by force
- He moved the gathering to another town
R “The Prophet ignored the problem and he tried to use the assembly for his mission of communicating the message of God to mankind.”
