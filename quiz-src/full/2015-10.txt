@ spirituality-a-universal-religion
Q How does the editorial describe spirituality?
* As a conscious discovery by the thinking mind of a reality beyond the material :: Yes. It is called the highest intellectual achievement.
- As a passing mood of ecstasy
- As the suppression of all thought
- As a gift given only to mystics
R “Spirituality is a phenomenon of intellectual development. By applying the mind, one discovers that there is a non-material reality beyond material existence.”
@ the-role-of-muslims-in-diaspora

* In harmony with local people, earning their living by hard work and ready to help defend the country :: Right. As givers, not takers.
- Apart from the local people, by their own laws
- As guests supported by the king
- As traders who soon returned home

@ keeping-calm-in-the-face-of-adversity
Q Facing 20,000 soldiers with a handful of men, Napoleon walked forward unarmed and won them over. What does the article take from this?
* One who stays calm in danger can judge the situation and find a way other than confrontation :: Well read. Panic prevents a proper assessment.
- Courage always defeats numbers
- Armies are loyal only to the strong
- Battles are won by speeches
R “Whatever a man’s resources, to deal effectively with a situation, he must be able to make a proper assessment. And he will not be able to do this if he panics in the face of danger.”
@ two-types-of-people
Q How does the article distinguish a negative person from a positive one?
* One is a victim of circumstances and laments them; the other rises above them and helps shape history :: Correct. Negative thinking rests on the outer world, positive thinking on one’s inner self.
- One is poor; the other rich
- One is silent; the other speaks out
- One is young; the other experienced
R “A negative person is a victim of history, while a positive person helps shape history.”
@ going-places-on-home-ground
Q Paul Dirac was an unremarkable engineering student who became one of the great physicists. What does the article conclude?
* Everyone has a field of his own in which to excel; failure in one is no reason to lose hope :: Yes. Mathematics was his “home ground.”
- Teachers cannot recognise genius
- Engineering is harder than mathematics
- Success depends on the right college
R “Like Dirac, everyone has a domain of their own to excel in. Failure in one field is no reason to lose hope.”
@ the-universe-is-not-a-machine
Q The article tells of robots that went out of control. What argument does it build on such accidents?
* A mere machine goes wrong; the faultless order of the universe over billions of years points to conscious control :: Well reasoned. “Each in its own orbit runs.”
- Machines will one day replace people
- The universe is a larger robot
- Technology should be abandoned
R “Had the universe just been a mechanical object like the robot, there would definitely have been accidents similar to those mentioned above.”

@ death-at-everyones-doorstep

* Death comes to everyone at an appointed moment; wise is the one who prepares before it :: Yes. “Successful is the one who awakens” in time.
- Public men should retire early
- Lecturing is dangerous in old age
