## 2026-09
@ the-gift-of-criticism
Q Umar ibn al-Khattab called the person who points out his shortcomings a giver of a “gift.” Why?
* A weakness one is unaware of cannot be corrected, so whoever reveals it is helping one grow :: Yes. Criticism is a mirror, not an insult.
- Because criticism is pleasant to hear
- Because gifts were customary among leaders
- Because critics are always right
R “A weakness of which you are unaware cannot be corrected. The person who makes you aware of it is, therefore, helping you in your self-development.”
@ the-gift-of-criticism
Q What question does a sincere seeker of truth ask about a criticism?
* “Is what has been said true?” – not “Who has said this?” :: Right. If true, he accepts it; if not, he answers calmly.
- “Who has said this?”
- “How can I reply sharply?”
- “Who else heard it?”
R “A sincere seeker of truth does not ask, ‘Who has said this?’ He asks, ‘Is what has been said true?’”
@ a-glimpse-of-paradise

@ discovering-god-everywhere
Q Asked for proof of God, a Sufi answered, “The leaves of a tree.” What was his reasoning?
* The same soil, water and sunlight yield utterly different plants and fruits – pointing to a designed system behind them :: Well read. From creation to the Creator.
- Leaves are too many to count
- Trees are older than people
- Leaves fall every year
R “Leaves and fruits grow from the same earth. They receive the same water and the same sunlight. Yet their results are astonishingly different.”

@ death-is-not-extinction
Q The article likens this life and the Hereafter to a farmer’s two seasons. Which is which?
* This life is the season of sowing; the Hereafter is the season of harvest :: Yes. After death there is no more sowing.
- This life is the harvest; the Hereafter is for sowing
- Both are seasons of harvest
- Neither has any connection with the other
R “Our worldly life is the season of sowing; the Hereafter is the season of harvest.”
@ save-yourself-first
Q Why does the article say the first victim of an angry reaction is oneself?
* Anger destroys one’s own peace and judgement, and replaying the incident repeats the harm many times :: Right. “Save yourself.”
- Because the other person always strikes back
- Because anger is punished by law
- Because others stop listening
R “He harmed you once; by repeatedly remembering the incident, you harm yourself many times.”
@ the-art-of-non-provocation
Q Opponents mocked the Prophet with the name “Mudhammam.” What principle does the article draw from his reply that they were abusing Mudhammam, “while I am Muhammad”?
* Do not take every negative remark personally – an insult becomes a quarrel only when it finds a willing participant :: Yes. He deprived the insult of its power.
- Always answer mockery with wit
- Change one’s name when insulted
- Insults should be reported to the elders
R “It demonstrated a profound principle of life: do not personalize every negative remark… An insult becomes a quarrel only when it finds a willing participant.”
@ the-power-of-perseverance
Q In the Chinese story, what was the old man’s answer to those who laughed at his plan to dig away two mountains?
* The mountains cannot grow, but with each day’s digging – by him, his sons and theirs – they must grow smaller :: Exactly. A problem has limits; perseverance need have none.
- That he would hire many workers
- That the mountains were smaller than they looked
- That he would move house if he failed
R “The mountains cannot grow any bigger, while with every day’s digging they will become a little smaller. If we continue, one day they must disappear.”
@ a-drop-reveals-character
Q When, according to the article, is a person’s real character most clearly seen?
* In small moments – when there is nothing to gain, no one watching and no fear of being judged :: Well reasoned. And character can be reformed by self-examination.
- In public speeches
- In great decisions only
- In what others say about him
R “It becomes especially visible when there is no advantage in doing good, no audience to appreciate us and no fear of being judged.”
