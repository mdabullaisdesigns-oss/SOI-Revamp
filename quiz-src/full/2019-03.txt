## 2019-03
@ the-existence-of-god
Q Building on Descartes’ “I think, therefore, I am,” what does the editorial say?
* “I am, therefore, God is” – a creature’s existence points to a Creator :: Yes. Simple logic applied to one’s own being.
- “I doubt, therefore, nothing is certain”
- “I see, therefore, I believe”
- “I work, therefore, I am”
R “By applying the same logic, one can say, ‘I am, therefore, God is.’”
@ the-existence-of-god
Q How does the editorial use subatomic particles to answer the question, “How can one believe in a God who is unseen?”
* They show that something can exist without being visible :: Right. Being seen is not the test of being real.
- They show that matter is an illusion
- They show that scientists are often wrong
- They show that everything can be seen with the right tools
R “A subatomic particle, although not visible, does exist. This shows that for something to exist, it is not necessary that it should be visible.”
@ the-eternal-journey
Q What does it mean, in the article, to look at every affair “in its eternal context”?
* To value only what will count in the Hereafter, and treat as worthless what will be worthless there :: Well read. This is the outlook of faith.
- To plan carefully for old age
- To study the history of past nations
- To ignore present duties
R “We will consider everything which will become worthless in the next world as worthless now, no matter how great a worldly price it may seem to command.”
@ real-loss
Q The article calls this world “a preview of the Hereafter.” How should its pleasures be understood?
* As a drop that points to an ocean – meant to increase longing for the lasting joys to come :: Yes. The loss is to mistake the sample for the whole.
- As rewards already earned in full
- As temptations with no purpose
- As all the happiness there is
R “So that he may see one drop of water, and realize that it is indicative of a vast ocean beyond.”
@ self-development
Q “No steam or gas ever drives anything until it is confined.” What does the article apply this to?
* A life grows only when it is focused, dedicated and disciplined :: Correct. There is a price for every end.
- Engines need regular repair
- People work best under pressure from others
- Freedom is harmful to the young
R “No life ever grows until it is focused, dedicated, and disciplined.”
@ the-moment-of-truth
Q When drought cut the Ivory Coast’s electricity, a businessman said he had never realised how hot it was. What does the article take from this?
* People live in an artificial sense of ownership and freedom; death will show how things really stand :: Well reasoned. Freedom was a test, not a right.
- Hot countries need more power stations
- Businessmen should work from home
- Comfort makes people healthier
R “When death comes, it will dawn on an individual, all of a sudden, that he had just been fooling himself: he had been given freedom as a test whereas he had thought it was his right.”
@ in-gods-country
Q “Madam, you are in Russia now, not America.” What wider point does the article make with this remark?
* Man lives in a world made by God, not by himself, and must live by God’s scheme for it :: Right. The prophets came to make that scheme known.
- Travellers should keep their opinions to themselves
- Every country is the same at heart
- One should never leave one’s homeland
R “Man, then, is not in his own country: he is living in the country of God.”
@ morning-is-the-best-time
Q Why, according to the article, is the mind at its freshest in the morning?
* During sleep it sorts and files the day’s impressions, leaving it clear – and there are few distractions :: Yes. The best hours for reading, reflection and planning.
- Because the air is cooler
- Because breakfast gives energy
- Because the day’s news has not yet arrived
R “During sleep, the mind sorts out the observations and thoughts and stores them in their respective files… his mind has been cleared of all thoughts and is blank once again.”
@ one-sided-reporting
Q What habit does the article call “one-sided reporting”?
* Telling only what the other person did, while leaving out one’s own part in the matter :: Well read. It is unfair and solves nothing.
- Reading only one newspaper
- Speaking for too long
- Repeating what others have said
R “Every person has the habit of recounting an incident in such a manner that only the involvement of the other person is apparent, whilst remaining silent about their own involvement.”
@ all-that-glitters-is-not-gold
Q A necklace bought as gold proved to be brass on the goldsmith’s touchstone. What does the story warn of?
* Deeds we think of as gold will be tested on God’s touchstone, and only the genuine will have value :: Exactly. Self-satisfaction is no proof.
- One should never help strangers
- Gold should be bought only from shops
- The poor are often dishonest
R “In the next world, God will judge everybody’s actions on His own touchstone. The value of gold will only be attached to those actions which are proved to be made of gold.”
