## 2021-03
@ deconditioning-the-mind
Q The editorial uses an onion to picture “deconditioning.” What does the image convey?
* Society’s influences cover our pure nature like layers, to be peeled away one by one :: Yes. Until the original nature is uncovered.
- The mind has no centre at all
- Learning always brings tears
- Each person has many personalities
R “This conditioning is like a series of coverings over our true nature, similar to the peels of an onion. Deconditioning is a process… of these layers, one by one, until the true, pure nature is uncovered.”
@ deconditioning-the-mind
Q According to the editorial, in which two ways can a person be deconditioned?
* By accepting others’ harsh criticism with an open mind, or by examining himself just as strictly :: Right. “Hammering” from outside or from within.
- By travel abroad, or by long solitude
- By reading widely, or by fasting
- By changing friends, or by changing work
R “Either one accepts the ‘hammering’ of others or one becomes one’s own worst critic by ‘self-hammering’.”

@ a-faultless-universe
Q The universe is unimaginably vast, yet works in complete harmony. What does the article conclude from this?
* It is governed by a single, all-powerful Creator – otherwise it would fall into chaos :: Yes. The smallest deviation would upset the whole.
- It is too large to have any order
- Its harmony is only apparent
- Its parts are independent of one another
R “This perfect harmony in the universe is a proof that it is functioning under an All-Powerful Creator. Had this not been so, the whole universe would be reduced to ultimate chaos.”
@ the-art-of-thinking
Q A ship approaching the shore shows its mast first and then its hull. What does the article use this to show?
* That discoveries come from thinking about what we see – here, that the earth is not flat :: Correct. Like Newton and the falling apple.
- That ships were once built with tall masts
- That the sea is higher far from land
- That eyesight weakens with distance
R “If the earth was flat, the entire ship would have appeared in the distance… The truth is that all discoveries are made solely by thinking.”
@ the-art-of-thinking
Q “Dogs may bark, but the elephant goes on.” What principle of right thinking does the saying illustrate?
* Do not be drawn into every provocation; keep to your own constructive course :: Right. Avoidance is the better option, not cowardice.
- The strong may ignore the law
- Small people should stay silent
- Noise always signals danger
R “Instead of wasting your time being provoked into fighting, you should stick to the path of positive action and avoid negative action.”
@ the-art-of-thinking
Q Everyone has his own idea of what is ideal. What does the article call the only practical solution in dealing with others?
* A policy of adjustment :: Well reasoned. Insisting on one’s personal ideal leads to conflict.
- Persuading everyone to agree
- Living entirely alone
- Letting the strongest decide
R “The achievement of the personal ideal is impossible. The only practical solution is to adopt the policy of adjustment with others.”
@ the-story-of-khadija
Q Khadija had been the wealthiest lady of Makkah. What did she do during the years when the believers were driven out to live in tents?
* She chose to stay with the Prophet and bore hunger and hardship patiently, without complaint :: Yes. Her wealth had already been given to the cause.
- She remained in her house in the city
- She travelled to another land
- She asked her relatives to end the ban
R “Khadija could have stayed on in Makkah. But she chose to stay with the Prophet in these times of trial and difficulty… But she did not complain.”
@ ask-maulana
Q A man moved a full-grown tree into his courtyard to save time, and it withered. What did he say he had learned?
* “I am in a hurry, but God isn’t” – nature works by gradual growth, not sudden leaps :: Exactly. Realism is the root of patience.
- “Large trees need more water”
- “Courtyards are too small for trees”
- “Labourers cannot be trusted”
R “‘I am in a hurry, but God isn’t.’ This story informs us about the law of nature, which is based on gradual development and not on sudden leaps.”
@ ask-maulana
Q In a factory the Maulana saw a flywheel reverse its direction within seconds. What did it suggest to him about anger?
* If a machine can change direction so quickly, a person can train himself to switch from rage to silence at once :: Well read. Silence lets anger subside.
- That machines are superior to men
- That anger should be released quickly
- That factories are stressful places
R “When matter (or machine) has the quality to change its direction within seconds, then a human being should have this ability manifold.”
