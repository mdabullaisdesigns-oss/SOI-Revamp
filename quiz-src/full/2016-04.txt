## 2016-04
@ the-problem-of-intolerance
Q How does the editorial describe intolerance?
* As differences of opinion that have not been managed :: Yes. Managed, they lead to development; unmanaged, to breakdown.
- As a problem peculiar to one country
- As something only governments can cure
- As the natural end of every disagreement
R “Intolerance is only an improper name for differences of opinion… In truth, intolerance is a phenomenon of unmanaged differences.”
@ the-problem-of-intolerance
Q What does the editorial ask of those who want to do something about intolerance?
* To educate people in the value of tolerance, not merely protest about it :: Right. Talking alone is an escape from duty.
- To demand new laws
- To withdraw from public life
- To answer intolerance in kind
R “Those who want to do something about the issue of intolerance must not protest against it in speech or writing, but educate the masses.”
@ running-after-two-hares
Q “If you run after two hares, you will catch neither.” What kind of mind does the article say achievement requires?
* A single-focused mind fixed on one goal :: Yes. A divided mind becomes “Jack-of-all, Master of none.”
- A mind busy with many projects at once
- A mind free of any goal
- A mind that changes goals often
R “Super-achievement needs super endeavour done with a single-focused mind.”
@ the-importance-of-time
Q Molvi Lutfullah never attended an English school, yet wrote a book in English that was published in London. How did he learn the language?
* By memorising ten words every night without fail for eight years :: Well read. “Little by little becomes great.”
- By living in England for a decade
- By employing an English tutor
- By translating Persian poetry
R “During those eight years, not even a single night passed without having committed to memory ten words of the English language.”
@ the-importance-of-time
Q “Take care of the minutes, for the hours will take care of themselves.” What principle does the article draw from Lord Chesterfield’s advice?
* The whole is made of parts; great success is the sum of small ones :: Correct. Five wasted minutes an hour add up to weeks.
- Small things do not matter
- Hours matter more than minutes
- Time cannot really be saved
R “Taking care of the parts is just as good as taking care of the whole… All great success ultimately boils down to an accumulation of small successes.”
@ mans-helplessness
Q The article says experience teaches man two things about his position in the world. What are they?
* That he is only a “taker,” and that there is another Being who is only a “giver” :: Yes. From this follows praise and thanks to God.
- That he is master of nature, and answerable to none
- That he is alone, and must rely on himself
- That he is unlucky, and others are fortunate
R “Experience shows man that in this world he is only a ‘taker’ and secondly that there is another Being Who is only a ‘giver’.”
@ sound-speech
Q What does the article say makes a person able to speak soundly and straight?
* Fear of God, which makes him sincere and honest so that his words match his thoughts :: Right. Such speech reaches people’s hearts.
- Training in public speaking
- A wide vocabulary
- A loud and confident voice
R “Fear of God makes one highly sincere, responsible and honest. It is these qualities that makes one able to speak in a sound manner… There is no difference between his thinking and his speaking.”
@ the-treasure-of-god-realization
Q How does the article regard the discoveries of modern science?
* As uncovering the “words of God,” giving believers far more to reflect on :: Yes. Science has widened the canvas of God-realisation.
- As a threat to faith
- As of use only to specialists
- As replacing the need for reflection
R “Modern science, in its reality, is another name for discovering these words of God… Now science has provided a huge library of unlimited data for the seekers of Ma’rifat.”
@ salvation
Q According to the article, how does receiving in the next world differ from receiving in this one?
* Here things are given as a test, deserved or not; there, only according to what one has earned :: Exactly. Now is the time for action.
- Here nothing is given; there everything is
- Here the good are rewarded; there all are
- There is no difference
R “In the world before death, man has been given a great number of things… whether or not he deserved them. But after death, the criterion of receiving will only be a matter of his just desserts.”
@ increasing-in-faith
Q A scientist said a day without a new discovery in nature felt wasted. What does the article say this means for a believer?
* Faith should grow daily through fresh discovery of God; faith that does not grow is hardly faith :: Well reasoned. God is a never-ending reality.
- Believers should become scientists
- Faith is complete once it is declared
- Discovery belongs to science alone
R “Faith is another word for discovery of God… Faith that does not grow is not really faith at all.”
