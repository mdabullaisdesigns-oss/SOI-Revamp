## 2016-07
@ right-here-right-now
Q Why does the editorial call the outlook “right here, right now” unworkable?
* Human nature longs for the eternal, so a life planned for the present alone leaves a person under strain :: Yes. The post-death period must be taken into account.
- The present is always unpleasant
- People cannot remember the present
- Planning is impossible in modern life
R “According to human nature, anything less than eternity cannot truly be man’s goal… The right attitude towards life is that which takes into account the post-death period.”
@ secret-of-happiness
Q To what does the article compare the troubles of this world?
* A furnace that purifies ore – hardship forges a strong and shining personality :: Right. One must “learn to be happy without happiness.”
- A storm that destroys the harvest
- A prison with no door
- A debt that grows each year
R “The troubles of the world act like the furnace required for the purification of ores… hardship and difficult experiences forge the personality of an individual.”
@ negative-thinking-is-un-islamic
Q When trouble comes, what does the article say the Quran asks a believer to do?
* Look within for his own fault and correct it, not blame others :: Yes. “Whatever misfortune befalls you is due to your own doing.”
- Identify who is responsible and oppose them
- Wait for others to apologise
- Accept that nothing can be done
R “Whenever any trouble comes upon you, the only thing you must do is introspect to discover the fault within yourself which is the real cause of your troubles.”
@ negative-thinking-is-un-islamic
Q How does the Quran explain the Muslims’ setback at the Battle of Uhud, according to the article?
* By their own failing – their courage failed and they disagreed among themselves :: Well read. It does not dwell on the enemy’s plots.
- By the greater numbers of the enemy
- By betrayal from an ally
- By bad weather on the day
R “‘…when your courage failed you and you disagreed among yourselves…’ (3:152). This verse is a clear lesson to the Muslims that they were not to lay the blame of their defeat in the battle on others.”
@ eliminate-bad-habits
Q What law of nature does the article draw from the form of the creed, a negation followed by an affirmation?
* Something must be given up before something better can be gained :: Correct. Truth cannot be held along with falsehood.
- Every gain is followed by a loss
- Nothing can ever be given up
- Habits form by themselves
R “It is an abiding law of nature that only after relinquishing something will one be able to acquire something else.”
@ belief-in-god
Q The article compares a stuffed tiger in a museum with a live tiger in the jungle. What do they stand for?
* Inherited, lifeless belief in God, and conscious belief that stirs awe in the whole person :: Yes. Where there is no awe, belief is only traditional.
- Science and religion
- Knowledge from books and from travel
- Past greatness and present weakness
R “A traditional belief in God is a lifeless belief… A conscious belief in God arises from discovering God and all His powers… his whole personality is shaken.”
@ when-you-make-a-mistake
Q What does the article say of someone who readily says “O God, forgive me” but will not say “Brother, forgive me”?
* That such reluctance goes against the spirit of faith :: Right. “Sorry, I was wrong” restores relations at once.
- That he is right to keep the two apart
- That forgiveness from people is not needed
- That he should wait for the other to speak first
R “A true man of faith will say to another: ‘Forgive me,’ just as easily as he says to God: ‘O Lord, forgive me.’”
@ wonder-of-wonders
Q The chemicals in a human body are worth only a few dollars. What does the article ask us to see in this?
* The wonder that God has made a living, thinking being, beyond price, from matter of no value :: Yes. People marvel at a conjurer but not at this.
- That human life is cheap
- That science can now make a human being
- That the body matters less than money
R “Out of this four hundred rupees worth of matter, God has created a human being so wonderful that no price can be set upon him.”
@ patience-the-superior-solution
Q Why, according to the article, does the Quran link gratitude with patience?
* Life is full of hardship, and only one who bears it patiently can remain thankful :: Exactly. Patience also keeps the mind clear to respond wisely.
- Because the patient receive more than others
- Because the two words sound alike in Arabic
- Because gratitude is owed only after long waiting
R “It is only when a man patiently endures worldly problems that it is possible for him to express his feeling of gratefulness to God.”
@ the-secret-of-success
Q A paratrooper climbed along the outside of a speeding train to reach the guard and save a fallen comrade. What does the article take from his act?
* Success comes to one who forgets himself in single-minded pursuit of the goal :: Well read. He thought only of the fallen soldier.
- Rules should always be followed first
- Trains ought to be made safer
- Courage is a matter of training alone
R “To succeed in life, one must do what Kailash Chand did. One should put aside all thoughts about oneself and relentlessly pursue one’s goal.”
