## 2013-03
@ living-with-a-positive-mind
Q A Christian scholar asked whether Islam has a teaching like “Love your enemy.” Which idea from the Quran (41:34) did the author give in reply?
* Return good for evil, and the enemy may become your dearest friend :: Yes. The verse treats an enemy as a potential friend, to be won by a positive response.
- Keep away from enemies until they apologise :: The verse asks for a positive response from your side first, not waiting.
- Treat enemies exactly as they treat you :: The verse says good and evil deeds are not equal; it asks for good in return for bad.
- Enmity is natural and must simply be endured :: The article says friendship is natural and enmity is unnatural, born of misunderstanding.
R The author quoted 41:34 – “Do good deeds in return for bad deeds; then you will see that the one who was once your enemy has become your dearest friend” – and concluded that your enemy is your potential friend.
@ living-with-a-positive-mind
Q The article uses the example of a cow turning grass into milk. What is it meant to show?
* That a person can convert negative experiences into positive thinking :: Well done. This “art of conversion” is called the gist of prayer and spirituality.
- That simple living is better than modern life
- That animals are closer to nature than man
- That good results need a long time
R “If a cow can convert the grass that it eats into milk, why cannot man, with a mind a billion times more developed… convert negativity into positivity?”
@ all-praise-is-due-to-god
Q According to the article, what is the one thing man can offer to God?
* Acknowledgment of His greatness and bounties :: Right. God is too great for man to give Him anything else.
- Wealth spent in His name
- Long hours of ritual
- Knowledge gathered over a lifetime
R “God the Almighty is too great for man to give Him anything. The only thing man can offer in His presence is acknowledgment.”
@ a-15-billion-year-plan
Q Why, according to the article, did the Creator bring the universe into being through a long process of cause and effect rather than by a sudden miracle?
* So that man could develop his thinking and creativity, giving rise to science and civilization :: Exactly. A gradual, law-bound world is one man can study and learn from.
- Because a miracle would have been beyond the laws of nature
- So that the earth would have time to cool
- So that the prophets could arrive in the right order
R The article says that had the universe been created suddenly, “creativity could not have been developed in man. No science, no civilization would have come into existence.”
@ duty-consciousness-not-rights-consciousness
Q “One person’s duty is another’s right.” What follows from this, in the article’s view?
* If everyone fulfils their duties, everyone’s rights are taken care of automatically :: Yes. That is why the stress is placed on duty.
- Rights must be won before duties can be asked for
- Duties and rights can never be balanced
- Only governments can secure people’s rights
R The article says, “if we all follow the principle of being duty conscious, automatically our rights will be taken care of,” and that rights should be asked for from God.
@ how-the-quran-was-compiled
Q At the time of the Prophet’s death, in what state did the Quran exist?
* Complete in the memories of companions in its present order, and fully written down on separate materials :: Correct. The order lived in memory; all the parts existed in writing.
- As one bound volume kept in the mosque at Medina
- Only in memory, with nothing yet written
- Written in full, but memorized by only a few scribes
R “The order existed in memory, not in writing, though all the parts existed at that time in written form.”
@ how-the-quran-was-compiled
Q The article describes Zaid ibn Thabit’s work under Abu Bakr as “more of collection than of compilation.” Why?
* He gathered the written pieces and cross-checked them against the memorized Quran before writing it out in order :: Well reasoned. The written and oral forms were matched first.
- He chose which verses were to be included
- He translated the verses for new Muslims
- He arranged the chapters in a new order of his own
R The scattered written pieces were “used to crosscheck countless memories in oral tradition”; once the two matched, Zaid wrote the verses down in their correct order.
@ the-psychology-of-success
Q American researchers compared winning and losing wrestlers of equal strength and training. What separated them?
* Their state of mind: winners were positive, relaxed and confident :: Right. The difference was psychological, not physical.
- Their diet and hours of sleep
- The quality of their coaches
- Their years of experience
R The report said: “Losers tended to be more depressed and confused before competing, while the winners were positive and relaxed.”
@ islam-and-politics
Q The Prophet said “Perform your prayer as you see me performing it,” but gave no such instruction about government. What does the article conclude from this?
* Worship has a fixed pattern, while politics is to be shaped by the social conditions of the time :: Yes. The article calls this “situational politics.”
- Politics is forbidden to believers
- The Medina state must be copied in every age
- Rulers should always be religious scholars
R “Islamic ibadah has a set pattern forever but Islamic politics has no such eternal norms… a kind of situational politics that is based on social realities.”
@ patience-instead-of-reaction
Q The Quraysh mocked the Prophet with the name “Muzammam” (the condemned). How did he respond?
* Calmly – he said their abuse was aimed at “Muzammam,” whereas he was Muhammad :: Exactly. He refused to be provoked.
- He asked his companions to punish them
- He left Makkah the same day
- He answered them with equal insults
R The Prophet said: “They abuse a person by the name of Muzammam. Whereas I am Muhammad.” The article concludes that Islam does not teach one to be easily provoked.
