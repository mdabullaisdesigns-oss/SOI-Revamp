## 2015-03
@ differences-of-opinion
Q A Hadith calls differences of opinion “a mercy.” What one condition does the editorial set for expressing them?
* They should rest on accepted evidence, without allegations against the other person :: Yes. Then they bring new aspects of a subject to light.
- They should be kept private
- They should be voiced only by scholars
- They should be settled by a vote
R “Those who disagree with each other should do so on the basis of accepted evidence and must abstain from hurling allegations against others.”
@ peace-for-the-sake-of-peace
Q The article compares peace to rain. What is the point?
* Rain only waters the field; the farmer must sow and work – peace gives opportunity, not the goal itself :: Right. Hence “peace for the sake of peace.”
- Peace, like rain, comes only in season
- Peace, like rain, cannot be predicted
- Too much peace, like too much rain, does harm
R “Peace is like rain. Peace only provides you with opportunities. It is upon people to avail these opportunities through wise planning.”
@ gender-equality
Q How does the article reconcile equality between men and women with the fact that they often do different work?
* Equality of status goes together with a division of labour based on ability :: Yes. Such differences exist between any two people.
- Equality means everyone must do the same tasks
- Different work shows that equality is impossible
- Work should be assigned by age
R “The formula should be ‘Gender equality while maintaining division of labour’… division is based on ability and not on gender.”
@ gender-equality
Q What does the article recommend as the practical way to secure women’s equal standing in society?
* Giving girls quality education and training :: Correct. Then equal status follows “without making any formal demands.”
- Passing more laws
- Holding public campaigns
- Reserving half of all posts
R “We need to provide quality education to girls, give them the best training… women will be able to attain an equal status in society.”
@ the-answer-is-very-much-here
Q For centuries people held that the sun went round the earth. Why does the article recall this?
* To show how a fixed way of looking can keep people from seeing an answer that is already there :: Well reasoned. Here the fixed idea is “right here, right now.”
- To show that science is often wrong
- To praise the scholars of the ancient world
- To prove that the earth is unimportant
R “People are obsessed with wrong ideas… It is this difference in outlook between the reader and the book that has created the problem.”
@ eligibility-for-paradise
Q What precedent does the article see in Adam’s removal from Paradise?
* Paradise is earned by one’s deeds and self-restraint; it is no one’s birthright :: Yes. Not even for the Prophets.
- Paradise, once lost, cannot be regained
- Paradise is reserved for Adam’s descendants
- Paradise was only a symbol
R “No one will be given Paradise magically or on the basis of recommendations from anyone. Paradise is not a birth-right even for the Prophets.”
@ big-or-beneficial
Q By what measure, says the article, should a “big” achievement be judged?
* Whether it is of real benefit to people, not whether it brings personal glory :: Right. Marie Curie and Helen Keller are the examples.
- How dangerous it was
- How widely it was reported
- How much it cost
R “Big dreams must be creative and beneficial for humanity. They should not merely be a source of sensational news and personal glory.”
@ gods-neighbour
Q What sign does the article look for in someone who has truly found God?
* An inner life visibly touched and changed – not just claims and public fervour :: Yes. A discovery so great cannot leave a person unmoved.
- Wealth and success
- Many followers
- Long speeches about religion
R “They display religious fervour in public, but there are no signs of their souls being illuminated with the light of God.”
@ why-has-god-given-man-freedom
Q Stars, rivers and animals follow God’s will without choice. Why has man alone been given freedom?
* So that he may choose to live uprightly without compulsion, and so earn the highest credit :: Exactly. The freedom is a test.
- So that he may live as he pleases
- So that he may rule over other creatures
- So that he need answer to no one
R “Its purpose is that man should lead a morally upright life of his own free will… without being subjected to any external force.”
@ where-all-hopes-end-new-hope-emerges
Q Roosevelt wrote, “The light has gone out of my life,” and later became President. What does the article say explains such recoveries?
* An outer loss awakens the mind and will, which are greater than what was lost :: Well read. “The power of construction is stronger than the power of destruction.”
- Time heals every wound by itself
- Powerful friends come to the rescue
- Loss is usually smaller than it seems
R “What he has inside him is far greater than what he has lost outside of him… every external loss further activates the inner asset of a person, which is his mind.”
