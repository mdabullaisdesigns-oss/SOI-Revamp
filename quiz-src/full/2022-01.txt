Q The editorial quotes a passage from the magazine’s first issue about the rose bush. What does it say?
* Life, like the rose bush, holds both flowers and thorns; this is how God made the world, and we must fit in with it :: Yes. We cannot have things any other way.
- Thorns should be removed before flowers are enjoyed
- Roses grow only in certain soils
- Flowers fade, so they are not worth tending
R “Just as the rose bush, along with its exquisite flowers, also possesses piercing thorns, so also does life contain an amalgam of both pleasing and displeasing objects.”

@ the-importance-of-true-leadership
Q To what does the article compare someone who takes up leadership without the necessary knowledge and understanding?
* A person who jumps into a river without knowing how to swim :: Yes. And he bears the blame for where he leads others.
- A farmer who sows in winter
- A traveller without a map
- A merchant without capital
R “To swim in the river, only that person will venture who knows the art of swimming. Similarly, only a person who has fulfilled essential conditions of leadership should come into the field of leadership.”
@ islamic-virtues
Q On becoming Caliph, what did Abu Bakr tell the people about the weak and the strong among them?
* The weak would be strong in his eyes until given their due, and the strong weak until they gave what they owed :: Correct. “I am no better than you… when you see me waver, set me straight.”
- The strong would be given charge of the weak
- The weak would be sent away from the city
- All would be treated according to rank
R “The weak among you will be the strongest until I have ensured that they received what is rightfully theirs. The strong among you I will look upon as the weakest, until I have made sure that they pay their due.”
@ islamic-virtues
Q Which three actions did the Prophet call the most difficult?
* Being fair to others where one’s own interest is involved, giving from one’s own possessions, and remembering God at all times :: Right. Each asks one to go against the self.
- Fasting, travelling and keeping awake
- Learning, teaching and writing
- Trading, building and farming
R “Being fair with others on matters concerning oneself; helping others by giving from one’s own possessions; and remembering God at all times.”
@ patience
Q How does the article describe patience?
* Not passivity, but wise, well-planned action that takes the facts and consequences into account :: Well reasoned. Impatience is leaping without thought.
- Waiting and doing nothing
- Accepting defeat quietly
- Hiding one’s feelings
R “Patience is neither a passive attitude nor any kind of inaction… Patience means wisdom-based thinking. It means planning your strategy, assessing your resources.”

* Provision arriving where none could be grown would awaken an extraordinary sense of gratitude :: Well read. “So that they may be grateful.”
- Fruit is the healthiest food
