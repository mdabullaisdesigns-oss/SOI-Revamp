## 2013-06
@ the-age-of-neo-gandhism
Q How does “Neo-Gandhism,” as the editorial describes it, differ from the Gandhian method used before 1947?
* It works by re-educating minds, where the older method relied on mass mobilization :: Yes. “Mob activism” is to be replaced by “educational activism.”
- It allows force where peaceful protest fails
- It seeks help from foreign powers
- It gives up Gandhi’s ideas altogether
R “While the old pattern of the Gandhian method was based on mass mobilization, Neo-Gandhism will be based on the re-engineering of people’s minds.”
@ the-age-of-neo-gandhism
Q Why, according to the editorial, have protest methods lost their relevance in India today?
* There is no foreign “they” any more – the task is to build one’s own nation, a “we-we” equation :: Exactly. The target is now ourselves.
- Protests are now forbidden by law
- People have lost interest in public life
- The freedom struggle was never truly peaceful
R “Now, there is no ‘they’; all are members of the Indian nation. The present situation is based on a ‘we-we’ equation, and not a ‘we-they’ equation as it was in the past.”
@ experiencing-the-presence-of-god
Q Astronaut James Irwin felt God’s presence on the moon. Why, says the article, do most of us not feel it on earth?
* Familiarity – we have seen the world’s wonders since childhood and take them for granted :: Right. We must look “as if we are seeing them for the first time.”
- The earth is less wonderful than the moon
- Only a few chosen people can feel it
- Modern life leaves no time for nature
R “By observing the world around us from an early age, we become accustomed to it; we take it so much for granted that we fail to perceive its uniqueness.”
@ trust-is-golden
Q A Delhi street vendor was given cloth worth Rs 150,000 on credit. What had earned him this?
* Years of paying his debts before they were due :: Yes. Trust is built by acting, not by claiming to be trustworthy.
- A large cash deposit
- A relative’s guarantee
- His skill at persuading people
R “The way to establish trust is not by repeating how trustworthy one is. No, it is by acting in a trustworthy manner… consistently… over a long period.”
@ science-and-religion
Q What, according to the article, was the real conflict that made science seem opposed to religion?
* A conflict between science and an old theology built on Greek and Egyptian philosophy :: Well done. It was not divine religion that science contradicted.
- A conflict between Newton and the Church
- A conflict between Darwin’s followers and Newton’s
- A conflict between scientists of different countries
R “It was, in fact, a conflict between science and ancient theology, which had been founded on Greek and Egyptian philosophy rather than on divine religion.”
@ try-try-and-try-again
Q The article says every real achievement takes “fifteen years.” What point is it making?
* Lasting success comes only through long, continuous effort, in line with the laws God has set :: Exactly. Instant success is called an illusion.
- Fifteen is a number with special meaning
- Businesses should be sold after fifteen years
- Young people should not begin anything early
R “Those who long for a recipe for instant success are, in fact, living in a fool’s paradise… The only condition to succeed is continuous effort.”
@ disadvantage-turned-to-advantage
Q How did Mahatma Gandhi’s early shyness turn to his advantage?
* It taught him economy of words, so that he rarely spoke or wrote thoughtlessly :: Yes. He called his hesitancy “now a pleasure.”
- It kept him out of politics until he was ready
- It won him the sympathy of judges
- It led him to take up writing instead of law
R Gandhi wrote: “Its greatest benefit has been that it has taught me the economy of words… a thoughtless word hardly ever escaped my tongue or pen.”
@ criticism-and-denigration
Q When one side argues calmly with reasons and the other answers with mockery, what does the article say this shows?
* That the one who reasons is in the right :: Correct. The Prophets spoke the language of reason.
- That both sides are equally unsure
- That the calm speaker lacks conviction
- That the matter cannot be settled
R “This difference in approach is a sure indication that the former is in the right and his opponents are in the wrong.”
@ seeking-the-right-alternatives
Q What is “the lesson that the platform teaches” the passenger who missed his train?
* There is always a next train – a second opportunity to be patiently awaited and taken :: Right. Blaming others only wastes energy.
- One should always arrive early
- A missed chance never returns
- It is better to travel by another route
R “There is always that ‘next train’ for them to catch… being ready to avail of that God-given second opportunity.”
@ the-lesson-of-death
Q How, according to the article, would remembering death change the way a person treats others?
* He would see no sense in oppressing others or in pride, since he must soon come under the will of Another :: Yes. Greatness that does not last has no value.
- He would withdraw from people altogether
- He would hurry to enjoy what he has
- He would stop making any plans
R “He would realize that it is fruitless to oppress others if tomorrow he would have to come under the control of Another.”
