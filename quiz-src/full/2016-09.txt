## 2016-09
@ conversion-versus-dawah
Q How does the editorial describe dawah?
* A peaceful, two-way sharing of what one has found, in which each also learns from the other :: Yes. It is not debate, and nothing is imposed.
- A contest to prove one’s faith superior
- A campaign to change other people’s religion
- A set of rules for new believers
R “Dawah is a bilateral process which involves sharing of thoughts… Contrary to imposing one’s ideas on others, it stands for sharing one’s discovery with others.”
@ conversion-versus-dawah
Q According to the editorial, who is involved when a person adopts a faith?
* Only the person himself and his Creator; it is his own free discovery :: Right. There is no “converter.”
- The preacher who persuaded him
- His family and community
- The religious authorities
R “When a person accepts a faith different from his previous faith, he alone should make the choice on the basis of his discovery… there is no one in between him and His Creator.”
@ war-is-no-choice-at-all
Q Why, according to the article, does war not end when one side defeats the other?
* The defeated side does not accept defeat and is overtaken by a thirst for revenge :: Yes. So the problem returns in a worse form.
- The victors always quarrel among themselves
- Treaties are never written down
- Weapons are too easily bought
R “War cannot cease by one party defeating another. In practice, what happens is that the defeated party does not accept its defeat. Instead, a thirst for revenge overtakes it.”
@ harmonious-marital-relations
Q What outlook does the article recommend to a newly married couple?
* To see each other’s different qualities as complementary, and adjust instead of complaining :: Yes. Differences are part of the Creator’s plan.
- To make sure one of them always decides
- To avoid discussing anything difficult
- To live as far as possible like their parents
R “Women and men are not opposing genders, but complement each other… they will acknowledge and appreciate each other, instead of complaining and clashing.”
@ less-speech-more-silence
Q What does the article say a habit of listening more than speaking reveals about a person?
* Sincerity and humility – the essence of a fine character :: Correct. Much talk often means self-praise.
- Shyness and lack of knowledge
- Indifference to others
- A wish to hide one’s views
R “The practice of listening more than speaking… reflects rather a whole state of mind. Indicative of sincerity and humility, it is the essence of a fine character.”
@ the-acceptance-of-reality
Q The deposed King Faruq joked that soon only five kings would remain – four in a pack of cards and the King of England. What does the article admire in him?
* He understood the change of the age and accepted his loss, sparing himself bitterness :: Well read. He lived on peacefully.
- He plotted patiently to return
- He blamed his ministers
- He refused to leave his country
R “Acknowledging the end of dynastic rule, he was able to take his forced abdication as inevitable… he saved himself from negative thinking.”
@ the-goal-of-man
Q A disciple of Ibn Abbas explained the “worship” for which man was created (Quran 51:56). In what terms?
* As the realisation, or discovery, of God :: Yes. Truth found by one’s own reflection.
- As the performance of fixed rituals
- As obedience to rulers
- As withdrawal from the world
R “Mujaahid… has explained worshipping God in terms of the realization of God… Realization or Maarifat means the discovery of God.”
@ the-difference-between-a-believer-and-an-unbeliever
Q On his death-bed the Caliph Umar asked his son to rest his cheek on the dust. What contrast does the article draw from this?
* The believer humbles himself before God in this life, unseen; others will do so only when they see Him :: Exactly. Only the first has merit.
- The strong fear death more than the weak
- Rulers should be buried without honours
- The old are wiser than the young
R “An unbeliever will humble himself on seeing God: a believer does so without seeing Him.”
@ know-your-limits
Q In place of “The sky is the limit,” what guide does the article propose?
* Work to the full, but when stress sets in, recognise that your natural limit has been reached :: Right. Limits are marked by peace of mind.
- Aim low so as never to fail
- Stop at the first difficulty
- Let others set your limits
R “‘Stress is our limit’ makes much more sense… when we feel that we have fallen victim to stress we should know that we have reached our limits.”
@ procession-of-death
Q Why, according to the article, do people see deaths every day and yet miss their meaning?
* They think of each person only in worldly terms and have no living picture of the next world :: Well reasoned. They feel a loss, not a meeting with God.
- Death is hidden from public view
- They are too frightened to look
- They believe the dead will return
R “We see man in relation to this ephemeral world, but fail to see him in relation to the next eternal world.”
