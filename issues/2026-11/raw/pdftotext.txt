THE SUPERIOR SOLUTION

M

aulana Wahiduddin Khan wrote in his diary on October 19,
1986 that whenever a day passed without his discovering
something new, he felt that the day had been wasted.
This attitude reflects an important principle of intellectual and
spiritual growth: every day should bring some new discovery.
One morning, while reading the Encyclopaedia Britannica on
creativity, he came across an expression that particularly
impressed him: “superior solution.” The encyclopedia explained
that creative people are those who are able to discover superior
solutions to problems.
This expression contains a profound lesson. When faced with
a difficulty, most people react according to their established
habits. They complain, become angry, blame others, or repeat
methods that have already failed. A creative person, however,
takes a different approach. Instead of reacting emotionally, he
pauses and asks himself: Is there a wiser and better way of dealing
with this situation?

06

Spirit Of Islam Issue 133 November-December 2026

This is the secret of creative thinking. A problem is not merely
something to be overcome; it is also an opportunity to discover a
better way forward.
Arnold Toynbee used the expression “creative minority” for the
small groups that bring about great changes in history. Their
strength does not necessarily lie in numbers, wealth or political
power. It lies in their ability to respond creatively to challenges
and discover new possibilities.
There are situations in which an ordinary response can make
matters worse. Anger produces anger, argument creates further
argument, and confrontation closes the doors to communication.
In such cases, what is needed is not a stronger reaction but a
higher response—a superior solution.
The Treaty of Hudaybiyah provides a remarkable example. Its terms
appeared unfavourable to many of the Prophet Muhammad’s
companions. Yet the Prophet did not allow immediate emotions
to determine his course of action. He accepted a peaceful
settlement and thereby opened the way for communication
and wider interaction. What initially appeared to be a setback
ultimately became a historic breakthrough.
This teaches us that difficulties should not merely provoke us;
they should stimulate our creativity. Instead of asking, “How can I
defeat this problem?” we should ask, “What better possibility can
I discover through it?”
Every problem can become an invitation to think more deeply,
rise above conventional reactions and discover a higher course
of action. God has placed challenges in the world, but He has
also given human beings the intelligence and wisdom needed to
discover superior solutions.
A truly creative person is one who can turn obstacles into
opportunities and difficulties into discoveries. 

THE UNIVERSE AS GIFT

O

n 17 June 1999, while I was in Manchester, I had a small
experience that left a deep impression on me. I was
staying with an Arab brother. One morning, as I sat
quietly in my room, his five-year-old daughter, Qanita, knocked
at the door. She gently asked me in Arabic, “Turidu hajah?”—
“Do you need anything?”
It was an ordinary question, asked by an innocent child. Yet it
touched something deep within me. For a moment, I could not
answer. The words seemed to carry a meaning far greater than
the child herself could have understood.
At that moment, I remembered a saying of the Prophet
Muhammad recorded in Sahih Muslim. It tells us that God calls
out to His servants, asking whether there is anyone who has a
need that He may fulfil. The child’s question suddenly appeared
to me as a reflection of this divine invitation.
I began to feel that the question was not coming from the child
alone. It was as though the entire universe was asking the same
question: “Do you need anything?” The sky seemed to offer its
vastness, the sun its light, the rivers their water, the wind its
freshness, and the trees their shade. Everything appeared to
be silently saying, “We are here to serve you.”
This thought brought a new awareness of the blessings
surrounding us. Human beings are surrounded by countless
things that they did not create and cannot create. We did not

make the air we breathe, the sunlight that reaches us, the water
that sustains us or the food that grows from the earth. Yet all
these things are continuously made available to us.
The Quran draws our attention to this reality: “He has given you
all that you asked of Him” (14: 34). Elsewhere, it says: “Eat of the
good things We have provided for you, and be grateful.” (2: 172)
The important point is that receiving should lead to gratitude.
When a person recognizes that life is a gift, his entire attitude
changes. He no longer regards the world merely as a place for

This is perhaps one of the great purposes of the universe. It
is not simply a place in which human beings live; it is also a
vast school of spiritual learning. The changing seasons teach
us about order and renewal. The rising and setting of the sun
remind us that life moves according to a greater plan. The earth
provides food, the rain revives the soil, and countless processes
continue without human direction.
All these signs invite us to think beyond the visible world. They
remind us that the universe has a Creator and that human life
has a purpose.
The Quran says, “We have not created the heavens and the
earth and all that is between them except with truth.” (15: 85)
The universe, therefore, is not without meaning. It is part of a
divine plan in which human beings are being tested.
Our real task is to recognize our Creator, use His blessings
responsibly and prepare ourselves for the eternal life that
lies beyond death. A person who lives with this consciousness
gradually develops a deeper inner personality. Gratitude
replaces complaint, patience replaces impatience, and reflection
replaces heedlessness.

THE PURPOSE OF LIFE

T

he two most important days in your life are the day you
are born and the day you discover why.” This saying,
often attributed to Mark Twain, points to a profound
truth. Everyone experiences the first day. Only a few discover the
second.
Imagine a ship with engines but no compass. It may cross oceans,
yet without direction it cannot reach its destination. Human life
is much the same. A person may possess intelligence, education,
wealth and influence, but without a higher purpose, life can
remain busy yet empty.
Most people devote themselves to building careers, earning
money, gaining comfort and seeking recognition. These pursuits
have their place, but they cannot answer the question: What am
I living for?
The Quran gives an answer: “I created jinn and mankind only that
they may worship Me.” (51: 56) It means recognizing God as the
Creator, accepting His guidance and living responsibly before
Him.
When God becomes the centre of life, everything else finds
its proper place. Success produces gratitude instead of pride.
Failure becomes a lesson instead of despair. Wealth becomes a
trust rather than an obsession. Work becomes meaningful when
it is connected with a higher purpose.
The discovery of God transforms life. It gives direction to
ambition, purpose to work, peace to the heart and hope for the
future. Even ordinary activities can acquire spiritual value when
they are performed with sincerity and within moral limits.

A person who discovers this purpose gains a compass for life. He
knows where he is going and why he is travelling. The greatest
success is not to possess the world, but to discover its Creator

DEATH IS NOT EXTINCTION
Preparation for Eternal Life

D

ictionaries often define death as “the permanent cessation
of life”. This definition describes death from the standpoint
of physical observation. A person is alive; then his bodily
functions cease, and he is declared dead. Seen only from this
perspective, death appears to be the complete termination of
human existence.
Religion presents an entirely different understanding. According
to Islam, death is not the end of life. It is a transition from one
stage of existence to another.
Man has been created as an eternal being. His lifespan is divided
into two phases: the pre-death period and the post-death period.
The first is temporary; the second is eternal. The present world
is therefore not man’s final destination. It is the place where he
prepares himself for the world that comes afterwards.
The Quran states: “Every soul shall taste death.” (3: 185) The
expression “taste death” is significant. It indicates an experience
through which man must pass. Death does not mean that man
is reduced to nothingness. He passes through the experience of
death and enters another phase of existence.
The relationship between the two phases may be understood
through the example of cultivation. A farmer has one season in
which to sow his seeds. Once that season has passed, he can no
longer sow. The time of harvest then arrives, when he receives
according to what he planted earlier. Our worldly life is the season
of sowing; the Hereafter is the season of harvest.
The Quran says: “Whoever has done the smallest particle of good
will see it; while whoever has done the smallest particle of evil will
see it.” (99: 7-8) Nothing of moral significance is therefore lost.
Every good deed, every sincere intention, every act of patience

14

Spirit Of Islam Issue 133 November-December 2026

and every response to life’s tests becomes part of our eternal
record. This gives extraordinary importance to the present
moment.
After death, man will no longer possess the freedom of action that
he enjoys today. The examination will be over. There will be no
opportunity to return and rewrite one’s answers. The post-death
world is not a place for preparation; it is a place for receiving the
consequences of the preparation made here.
The Prophet Muhammad expressed this reality in practical terms:
“When a person dies, his deeds come to an end except for three —
continuing charity, beneficial knowledge and a righteous child who
prays for him” (Sahih Muslim) Death therefore carries a message
for the living: do today whatever must be done for tomorrow.
Every funeral silently delivers this message. The person being
carried to the grave once possessed plans, appointments,
possessions and ambitions just as we do. Then, suddenly, his
opportunity for action ended. What remains with him is not what
he owned, but what he became and what he sent ahead through
his deeds.
The wise person therefore remembers death not in order to
become pessimistic, but to become more conscious of life.
Awareness of death teaches him to distinguish between the
temporary and the eternal. It reminds him not to waste his limited
time in hatred, jealousy, revenge and meaningless pursuits.
Death is not a message of despair. It is a call to preparation.
Tomorrow belongs to God; today has been given to us. Whatever
goodness you wish to carry into eternity, begin creating it now.
Whatever correction you need to make in yourself, make it
today. For death does not announce the end of man’s journey. It
announces the end of his preparation. 

rebuilding, it transformed itself into one of the world’s leading

JUSTICE IN ACTION
No One Above Justice

A

horse race was once held in Egypt. Among the participants
were an ordinary Egyptian and the son of the governor,
Amr ibn al-As. The Egyptian’s horse won the race, but the
governor’s son could not accept defeat. Enraged, he whipped
the Egyptian, asking, “How dare you get ahead of the son of a
noble man?”
The Egyptian travelled to Madinah and complained to Umar ibn
al-Khattab, the second Caliph of Islam. Umar summoned the
governor and his son and listened to the complaint.
He then handed the whip to the Egyptian and told him to strike
the person who had struck him. The Egyptian did so. Umar also
instructed him to strike the governor, explaining that his son had
become arrogant because of his father’s position. However, the
Egyptian declined, feeling that justice had been served.
On this occasion, Umar uttered words that have become a
timeless expression of human dignity: “How can you enslave
people when their mothers gave birth to them in freedom?”
These words express the essence of justice. No one should be
considered superior because of wealth, family, authority or social
position. A ruler and an ordinary citizen, a rich person and a poor
person, must stand equally before the law.
Justice is not merely a principle to admire; it must be practised.
It requires the powerful to exercise restraint and the weak to
receive protection. It also demands that people rise above
personal interests and recognize the rights of others. A society
becomes stable when people trust that justice will prevail. When
privilege overrides justice, resentment grows and social harmony
begins to collapse.

18

Spirit Of Islam Issue 133 November-December 2026

True justice places truth above power and fairness above privilege.
It is a divine principle and a test of human character. The real
measure of a society is not how it treats the powerful, but how it
treats those who have no power. When justice is upheld without
fear or favour, human dignity is preserved, public trust grows and
society becomes worthy of peace. 

THE GREATEST ATTAINMENT
From Deprivation to Peace

W

hy do some people live with peace of mind while
others remain consumed by anger and hostility?
Why does one person respond to an unfavourable
situation with patience, while another turns towards hatred
and violence?
One important answer lies in two contrasting states of mind:
the sense of deprivation and the sense of attainment.
A person who constantly thinks that something belonging to
him has been taken away develops a negative psychology. He
believes that others have received what should have been
his. He begins to see people not as fellow human beings, but
as rivals who have deprived him of his rightful share. If this
thinking continues unchecked, resentment accumulates
within him. Under certain circumstances, this resentment
may eventually express itself in aggression or violence.
A sense of attainment produces the opposite psychology.
A person who feels inwardly fulfilled does not constantly
compare himself with others. He has no reason to live in
jealousy or resentment. His mind is free from the burden of
continually asking: Why does he have what I do not have? Why
was he given an opportunity that was denied to me? Such a
person possesses inner peace because he feels that he has
already discovered something worth possessing.
This principle applies to individuals as well as groups. Persistent
hatred often reveals an underlying sense of grievance. People
become obsessed with what they believe has been taken
from them—their position, wealth, recognition, opportunity
or power. Their entire interpretation of life gradually becomes
centred on deprivation.
The crucial question, therefore, is: What is the greatest
attainment available to a human being? The answer is the
discovery of God.
20

Spirit Of Islam Issue 133 November-December 2026

If a person has truly discovered God, he has discovered the
greatest reality of existence. After this discovery, all other
gains and losses become secondary. He may possess little in
material terms, yet inwardly he possesses an inexhaustible
treasure. Conversely, a person may possess enormous wealth,
status and power, yet remain inwardly deprived if he has failed
to discover the Creator.
This is the paradox of human life: a materially deprived person
can be spiritually rich, while a materially prosperous person can
be spiritually poor. The Quran says: “Surely in the remembrance
of God hearts can find comfort.” (13: 28) This verse points to
a fundamental spiritual law. Lasting peace cannot be secured
merely by arranging favourable external circumstances. It
requires an inner centre, and that centre is found in God.
Consider the nature of worldly attainment. Every material
achievement is limited. If you acquire wealth, you immediately
discover someone wealthier. If you gain fame, you find that
fame is temporary. If you acquire power, you remain anxious
about losing it. If you achieve professional success, another
ambition soon appears before you. Worldly attainment,
therefore, cannot completely satisfy man’s unlimited nature.
Human desire has no natural stopping point. A person who
possesses one million wants ten million. The person who
occupies one position wants a higher position. Achievement
creates another target, and the cycle continues. If peace
depends entirely upon fulfilling worldly desires, peace will
always remain somewhere in the future.
God-realization changes this equation. When a person
discovers God as the Creator and Sustainer of the universe,
he discovers that his life is not meaningless. He understands
where he has come from, why he is here and where he is going.
He realizes that this world is not the final destination but a
temporary testing ground. Success and failure, gain and loss,
praise and criticism—all acquire a different meaning.
This discovery gives him something that material possessions
cannot give: a sense of existential fulfilment.

THE HEART’S TRUE QUEST
The Search for Lasting Fulfilment

E

very human heart contains an empty space. It seeks
support, beauty, meaning and someone to love above all.
If this space is not filled with God, it will inevitably be
filled with something else—wealth, power, fame, personalities
or worldly attachments. This is a hidden form of worship.
Whatever receives a person’s deepest devotion becomes the
centre of his life.

THE WISDOM OF HELPLESSNESS
Discovering Strength Through Prayer

W

hy did God create man helpless? This question arises
naturally in the human mind. Man lives in a vast universe,
yet his control over it is extremely limited. He cannot
prevent illness, stop the ageing process, guarantee success or

moment of his life lies beyond his control.
At times, this experience produces a complaint: Why did God
make me so powerless? The answer is that human helplessness is
not a defect in creation. It is part of the divine scheme.
God did not give man absolute power, but He gave him access to
the All-Powerful. He did not make man independent; instead, He
opened before him the door of prayer. Human limitation is meant
to lead man towards the discovery of divine power. The Quran
says: “O mankind! It is you who stand in need of God—God is selfsufficient, and praiseworthy.” (35: 15)
This verse defines man’s true position. He is a creature of needs.
He requires oxygen every moment, water every day, food regularly
and innumerable favourable conditions merely to remain alive.
He created none of these things. He receives them. Human life is
therefore an experience of dependence.
Modern civilization sometimes conceals this reality. Technology
has given man astonishing powers. He can fly through the air,
communicate instantly across continents, explore distant planets
and treat diseases that were once incurable. These achievements
may create the impression that man is becoming increasingly
independent. But technology changes man’s capabilities, not his
fundamental position.
A person travelling in the most sophisticated aircraft still depends
upon the laws of nature. A surgeon capable of performing an
extraordinarily complex operation cannot guarantee that his own
heart will continue beating. A billionaire can purchase the finest

attribute success entirely to ourselves. We begin to believe
that our intelligence, wealth, influence or planning is sufficient.
Then something unexpected happens. A carefully prepared plan
collapses. An illness appears without warning. A relationship
changes. A business fails. An unforeseen event overturns years of
calculation.

and control. He can plan, but he cannot exercise absolute control
over the outcome.

frustrated; another turns towards God. The first sees helplessness

prayer begins at this point.
Prayer is much more than asking God for particular things. At its
deepest level, prayer is an acknowledgement of reality. It is man’s
confession that he is limited and that God is unlimited. When a
person sincerely says, “O God, I cannot, but You can,” he has
discovered his own position as well as the position of his Creator.

Choosing Peace Over Ego

not whether differences occur, but how we respond to them.

disagreement can then develop into a prolonged conflict,
with each side more concerned about proving itself right
than finding a solution.
There is, however, another way. Instead of treating the matter
as a struggle between competing claims, adopt a mutual
approach. Say, “I want peace and reconciliation. Let us both
put aside our egos and resolve this matter together.” Such
words can transform the atmosphere. A conciliatory approach
may awaken the other person’s conscience and encourage
him to reconsider his demands.

YOUR QUESTIONS ANSWERED
The remedy for ignorance is asking questions. (Prophet Muhammad)
The spirit of enquiry is the hallmark of an open society and the above
saying of the Prophet aptly illustrates this principle. A culture of curiosity
and open-mindedness will foster development in any society by motivating
its members to learn enthusiastically and enrich their knowledge. This is
because awareness of one’s ignorance is half of knowledge, as it becomes
a stepping-stone to seeking and finding answers. A questioning mind is
like a flowing river that is replenished with fresh thoughts and ideas and
continues on its journey.

What is faith?
Faith is the discovery of a higher reality. It marks the beginning
of a deeper understanding and opens the way to a vast world of
meaning. Faith is a journey from the limitations of the material world
towards the infinite reality of spiritual existence. It is a process
in which every new experience can bring greater awareness and
understanding.
The Quran presents faith as a living and developing reality. It
describes how God sends tranquillity into the hearts of believers
“so that they may increase in faith upon their faith.” (48: 4) Faith,
therefore, is not a static condition. It grows as a person reflects,
learns and becomes increasingly conscious of God. It is a form of
higher knowledge that continually develops with divine guidance.
How does faith increase?
Faith grows through reflection, contemplation, learning and
experience. A person reads the Quran and reflects on its message,
while also contemplating the signs of God in creation. The Quran
praises those “who remember God standing, sitting and lying on
their sides, and reflect on the creation of the heavens and the
earth.” (3: 191)
Faith also grows through meaningful discussion and intellectual
exchange. Umar ibn al-Khattab is reported to have said to his
44

Spirit Of Islam Issue 133 November-December 2026

companions, “Come, let us strengthen our faith for an hour.” Such
exchanges encourage reflection and deepen understanding.
The Quran teaches believers to pray: “My Lord, increase me in
knowledge.” (20: 114) This prayer reflects a lifelong desire to learn.
A person should therefore keep the windows of the mind open—
reading, observing, thinking and listening to people of knowledge
and experience. Humility is essential. Whenever a person
encounters someone from whom he can learn, he should set aside
ego and listen attentively.
Faith grows with God’s help, but human effort is also necessary.
In this world, development takes place through means and
circumstances. Spiritual understanding likewise grows through
sincere effort, reflection and learning.
Why is it important to be beneficial to others?
A truly meaningful human life is one in which a person becomes
beneficial to others. Human beings are not meant to live only for
themselves. They receive countless benefits from society and
therefore have a responsibility to contribute to the wellbeing of
others.
The best person is not merely one who possesses knowledge,
wealth or ability, but one who uses what he has for the benefit of
others. Such a person becomes a giver rather than merely a taker.
This quality brings value to life and contributes to success both in
this world and in the Hereafter.
How can one be beneficial to others?
The Prophet Muhammad said: “Whoever among you is able to
benefit his brother, let him do so.” (Sahih Muslim, 2199)
Benefiting others does not require great wealth or exceptional
resources. Every person can contribute according to his ability.
A kind word, sincere advice, practical assistance or guidance can
make a real difference to someone’s life.
One may carry another person’s burden, help with a task, guide
someone who is lost, provide financial assistance according to

Even when physical help is impossible, praying sincerely for

The important thing is to develop a giving mentality. Every person

What is the secret of inner peace?
The Prophet Muhammad said: “Look at those who are below
you and do not look at those who are above you, for this is more
suitable for you, so that you do not underestimate the blessings of
God upon you.” (Sahih Muslim, 2963)
This is a profound principle of life. Human beings naturally differ
in wealth, health, abilities and circumstances. These differences
cannot be completely eliminated. Inner peace therefore requires
freedom from unhealthy comparison.
Constantly looking at those who possess more can produce
envy, dissatisfaction and frustration. In contrast, recognizing the
blessings one already possesses develops gratitude. Gratitude
brings contentment and protects the mind from unnecessary
anxiety.
There is, however, a positive form of comparison. The Prophet
also drew attention to two people whom it is permissible to envy
in the sense of wishing to possess similar goodness: one whom
God has given wealth and who spends it righteously, and another
whom God has given wisdom and who acts upon it and teaches it
to others. (Sahih al-Bukhari, 73; Sahih Muslim, 816)
Thus, comparison should not become a source of resentment. It
can instead become a source of healthy aspiration.
How should a believer respond to personal loss or hardship?
Human life is constantly changing. No person can expect
circumstances to remain permanently favourable. The Quran
warns believers that they will be tested through fear, hunger and

who remain patient. (2: 155)
When hardship occurs, some people respond with complaint,
despair and protest. A believer, however, looks at difficulty as a
test rather than as the end of life. He accepts the reality of what
has happened while placing his trust in God.
The Quran describes the patient as those who, when calamity
strikes them, say: “We belong to God, and to Him we shall return.”
(2: 156) This attitude does not mean passive acceptance. Rather, it
helps a person regain emotional balance and face the future with
courage.
The Prophet taught a prayer for times of hardship: “O God, reward
me for my affliction and replace it for me with something better.”
(Sahih Muslim, 918)
A believer therefore does not allow a painful experience to become
the final chapter of his life. He learns from the past, accepts what
cannot be changed and turns towards the future with hope. After
loss, he looks for new possibilities. After failure, he begins again.
Even when one door closes, he continues searching for another.
The ability to move forward after hardship is one of the greatest
forms of spiritual strength. It enables a person to transform a
difficult experience into an opportunity for patience, learning and
renewed effort. 

deprived of them. This is a hint which shows how, in the world to
come after death, human beings are going to fare. There, this
principle of the ‘distribution of blessings’ will be applicable in a
perfect manner, with perfect justice; everybody will receive only
that to which he is entitled and will receive nothing that he did
not deserve. There are countless stars in the sky. All of them
